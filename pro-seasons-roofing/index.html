⭐ The booking note contains the answer to its own question. Word of mouth and door-to-door are both fair-weather channels. Knocking doors in a Willamette Valley winter is physically hard, and word of mouth runs on a back catalogue of finished jobs. Those are the two channels that weaken most in exactly the season you are asking about, so a slow winter is not a surprise, it is the predictable shape of having only those two.
What We Found Before Speaking To You
⭐⭐ You already sell the right winter service. It is invisible.

Your homepage says, in capitals, “24/7 EMERGENCY LEAK RESPONSE”. It says “Roofing for Every Season”. Your business is literally named Pro Seasons.

⭐⭐ You have already worked out what the winter answer is. Winter does not remove roofing demand, it changes its shape: replacement work stops because nobody re-roofs in the rain, and leaks, storm damage and emergency call-outs go up. You sell that. It is on your site.

🔴 But an emergency leak is the one job that never arrives by word of mouth or a knocked door. It is 9pm, water is coming through a ceiling, and somebody reaches for their phone and searches. That is the entire winter market and it is a search market. You are not in it.

🔴🔴 A firm that registered three months AFTER you is already running ads, under a name almost identical to yours

On the Oregon CCB active register, in Salem, there is 4 SEASONS ROOFING & CONSTRUCTION LLC, registered 9 June 2026. You registered 18 March 2026. They are three months younger than you.

We checked their website. They are running a Google Ads conversion tag, they have Google Analytics, and they have complete local business markup — postal address, geographic coordinates and opening hours in the code where search engines read it. You have none of those three things.

🔴🔴 And then there is the name. Pro Seasons Roofing and 4 Seasons Roofing, same city, same register, registered twelve weeks apart. Word of mouth almost always ends in a search. Somebody is told your name at a barbecue, types something like it into Google a week later, and the firm paying for that search result is the one that gets the call. That is a live risk to the one channel you say is working.

🔴🔴 You have no tracking at all, so “slowed way down” cannot be checked

No Google Analytics. No Tag Manager. No advertising tag. No pixel. Nothing.

⭐⭐ And this matters more for you than for almost anyone we audit, because of your age. Your CCB licence was registered 18 March 2026. That makes the business about six and a half months old, which means this is your first autumn. You have no previous winter to compare this one to.

⚠ So when you say things have slowed way down, there are three different things that could be true and nothing currently installed can tell them apart: normal seasonality, fewer people finding you, or the same number finding you and fewer calling. Those three have completely different fixes.

⭐ This is the cheapest item on this page and every other decision depends on it.

🔴 The website is two pages, and the second one is a copy of the first

Your sitemap lists exactly two addresses: your homepage, and a store page at /ols/products. We fetched both. They return the same 706 words. It is the same page twice.

Also on the homepage itself:

  • “Salem” appears once. “Oregon” does not appear at all. Neither does Marion County, Polk County or Keizer.
  • No service-area content of any kind — nothing saying which towns you cover.
  • No local business markup — no address, coordinates or hours in the code. For “roof leak repair near me” that is the single biggest gap.
  • Two identical page headings, both reading “Roofing for Every Season”. “Roofing for Every Season” is repeated eight times in the first few lines of the page.

⚠ That repetition is the website builder’s template duplicating a block, not a choice you made. We are not telling you your site is bad taste. We are telling you it was never finished.

⚠ A Gmail address on a domain you already own

Your published email is Proseasonsroofing@gmail.com. You own proseasonsroofing.com.

⚠ For a homeowner this changes nothing. ⭐ For insurance work and for any commercial or property-manager job it reads smaller than you are, and your own site already offers commercial roofing.

Why Word Of Mouth Alone Gets Harder, Not Easier

Roofing firms on the Oregon CCB active register, in Salem, that have been trading longer than you

79

There are 86 roofing firms on the active CCB register in Salem alone. 79 of them registered before you did, with a median of 5.4 years trading; 41 have five years or more and 13 have ten or more. The longest, Cascade Roofing of Salem, has been registered since 1994. ⭐⭐ Word of mouth is the one channel where seniority compounds, because it runs on finished jobs and years of neighbours. So the only channel you are using is the exact channel where you are 79th in the queue, and no amount of effort shortens that queue.

⭐⭐ But the opposite is true of search, and that is the opening. We looked at the long-established firms. Cascade, registered 1994, has a 337-word website. Roof Enhancer, registered 2007, has 172 words and no tracking whatsoever. Seniority in this market has not translated into web presence, with one exception: Pfeifer Roofing, registered 1996, runs 105 pages with analytics, a Meta pixel, a heat-map tool and live chat. 🔴 So the market is one firm doing it properly, a long tail of old firms coasting, and the newest entrant buying ads. Age is not what is beating you.

  • CCB licence 260826, Residential General Contractor, active, expires 18 March 2028.
  • $25,000 bond with RLI Insurance Company, current to 18 March 2028.
  • $1,000,000 liability insurance with SiriusPoint Specialty, current to 18 March 2027.
  • Registered to Salem, Marion County, OR 97304, responsible party Emmanuel Oviedo Duran.

⭐⭐ Everything on that list is a trust signal a homeowner wants before they let someone on their roof in the rain, and your website mentions CCB exactly once and your bond and insurance not at all. You are carrying the cost of being properly bonded and insured and getting none of the marketing benefit.

⭐ And one more thing in your favour: you got the offer right on your own, with no help. “24/7 emergency leak response” on a business called Pro Seasons is the correct strategy for a wet Oregon winter. The thinking is already done. It is the plumbing underneath it that is missing.

What We Would Want To Ask
  • What does a typical job pay you? Split it for us if it varies — an emergency leak call-out, a repair, and a full re-roof. ⭐ We deliberately have not put a revenue number anywhere on this page, because we do not know this one and we are not going to guess at it. It is the first thing we need.
  • How many jobs a month were you doing in the summer, and what is it now? Even roughly. ⚠ Without tracking this is the only version of that number that exists.
  • Who actually does the work — you, employees, or subcontractors? The CCB register lists you as Exempt, which normally means no employees on the books. We want to know what your real capacity is before suggesting anything that creates demand.
  • How many emergency leak calls did you get last winter? ⚠ Trick question, and we know it — you were not trading last winter. So nobody knows what that number should be, including us. That is worth saying out loud on the call.
  • Do you have a Google Business Profile, and is it verified? For “near me” and emergency searches that profile usually matters more than the website does, and it is free.
  • Are you aware of 4 Seasons Roofing & Construction? We would like to know whether the name overlap has already cost you a call, or whether you have never heard of them.
  • Do you want winter work, or do you want year-round evenness? Those are different briefs. Chasing emergency leaks is high-urgency and low-planning; booking re-roofs ahead for spring is the opposite. They can both be done, but not first.
⚠⚠ What we are not going to tell you on this call. We will not tell you how many enquiries anything would produce, what they would cost, or where you would rank. Anybody who gives you those numbers for a six-month-old business in a market they measured yesterday is making them up. ⭐ The honest first step for you is small and it is measurement, because you are the one business we have looked at this week that cannot yet tell a bad season from a bad website.
