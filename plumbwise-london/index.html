The short version
⭐ What is already working
  • 4.96 out of 5 from 229 reviews on TrustATrader, member since 2013, newest review 6 November 2026. Customers praise the engineer’s manner: "Really very polite and went above and beyond."
  • A limited company since 15 October 2012 (Companies House) and "over fifteen years experience in the trade" on TrustATrader.

  • A clear service list for heating: boiler installs, repairs, servicing, cylinders, controls, power flushing.
⚠ What stops a good engineer finding you
  • No careers page and no job advertised, on your site or on Reed. The engineer you want cannot find out you are hiring.
  • The site sells 24-hour emergency work, night rates and commercial maintenance. The engineer you want (private homes, no social housing, good with long-standing customers) may read that and move on.
  • No names, no team, no real photos. An engineer cannot see who he would work with or the kind of homes he would work in.
  • Nothing to apply through and nothing to screen with. Every applicant has to be phoned to find out the basics.
  • Nothing is measured. The only tracking tag is one Google switched off in 2023, so a job advert could not be tracked.

Your business, through a job seeker’s eyes
⚠ What a good engineer finds when he looks you up
  1. He searches your name on Google. He finds "Plumbwise", a gas engineer listing with 4.5 stars from 30 reviews and a link to your website. Nothing says you are hiring.
  2. He opens your home page. The first thing he reads is "24 hour emergency plumbing in London" and an emergency call-out number. On the Emergencies page: "an operative at your London property within the hour" and a "24 hour manned call centre".
  3. He looks for a careers or jobs page. There is none in the menu or the footer. If he types /careers/ he lands back on the home page.
  4. He looks for who he would be working for. No names, no team, no photos of vans or real jobs. The about page says "the engineers we use", which can read as subcontractors.
  5. He checks the Rates page. It lists charges for every time band through to 12am to 7am. If he is weighing you against a firm advertising "Monday to Friday, no weekends" (one West London firm does, on Reed today), that matters.
  6. He finds your TrustATrader profile. This is where you shine: 229 reviews, Sam named as owner, customers praising the engineer who came. But he has to go looking for it.
  7. To apply, he has one option: ring the emergency number or use the customer quote form, and hope it reaches Sam.

Behind the pages
⚠ Code and set-up
  • Platform: not WordPress or any site builder we recognise; last sitemap update 8 February 2021.
  • Tracking: Universal Analytics UA-36620364-1 only. Google stopped collecting data with Universal Analytics in 2023, so today the site effectively measures nothing. No GA4, no Tag Manager, no Meta pixel.
  • Forms: one customer quote form (first name, last name, email, phone, postcode, message, photo upload, privacy tick) repeated on each page.
  • Markup: Plumber and LocalBusiness on the home page only, with a review figure that is out of date.
  • Images: 7 image tags across 24 pages, 6 without a description. The pictures in use are stock or manufacturer photos.
  • Security: loads over HTTPS; www and non-www both reach the site; robots.txt and sitemap present.
