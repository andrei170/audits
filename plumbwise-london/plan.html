The hiring plan, in order

Offer, page, adverts, filter, speed, checks, trial. The demo careers page shows steps 2 and 4 working; try the application as a good candidate and as a weak one.

1Write down the offer (the call, then a day)

Pay or day rate, employed or self-employed, days and hours, any call-outs, the patch, van and parking. Every advert competing for this engineer leads with these. Without them an advert is ignored by exactly the people you want.

2A careers page that tells the truth about the job

Private homes, long-standing customers, no social housing, the must-haves, your reviews and how you hire. Your own site gets a "Careers" link in the menu. The demo shows it.

3Adverts aimed at engineers who already live near the work

Facebook and Instagram adverts shown to people in and around your patch, plus a sponsored job on Indeed, all naming the patch and the hours so engineers far away rule themselves out. Job adverts have their own targeting rules on each platform; we set them up within those rules. You told us good engineers will not commute in: the advert goes to where they live.

4A short application that does the first interview for you

Nine questions: Gas Safe and number, Level 3, years in homes, where the experience is (private, social housing, new-build or commercial), own van and tools, licence, home postcode, name, mobile. Anyone missing a must-have gets a polite answer straight away. You only see the ones worth a call.

5Reply the same day

Good engineers apply in more than one place. Every applicant who passes gets a text within minutes and a call slot, so the good ones are not lost while you are on a job.

6Check before you trust

Gas Safe number on the Register, Level 3 certificate, ACS dates, references from recent domestic work, and photos of their own recent installs. This is the step that stops a repeat of the gas leaks and callbacks.

7A paid trial day

A day alongside you or your longest-serving engineer on real work. You see how they work and how they treat a customer before they go near a 20-year client on their own.

8Keep the near-misses

Applicants who were nearly right stay on a list, so the next time someone leaves you are not starting from zero.

9Let the applications answer the east London question

Every application carries a home postcode. Within a few weeks you can see where the engineers who meet your standard actually live, before deciding anything about where you are based.

What we need from you on the call
  • Pay or day rate, and employed or self-employed.
  • Days, hours and whether there are call-outs or evenings.
  • The patch: which parts of London most of the work is in.
  • Van, fuel and parking arrangements.
  • How you check applicants today, and what happened with the agency hire.
  • Whether an engineer living in east London or Essex and covering London would suit you.
What we are not saying

We cannot promise a hire, a number of applicants or a date. What this does is put a clear, honest job in front of engineers who live near your work, filter out the wrong ones before you spend time on them, and let you check the right ones properly. Much of the website part could also be done by whoever looks after your site now; the demo is a blueprint, not a demand to switch.
