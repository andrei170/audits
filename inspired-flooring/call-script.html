HOW TO PLAY IT (read first)

This is not a "your website is broken" call. The D is held back by growth-and-trust gaps, not faults. The parts most firms get wrong, they got right: live analytics (they are not blind), enquiry forms and WhatsApp on the page, two Prestige Awards, a real showroom and a broad range. Lead with respect for that. The honest story: the site earns trust and captures the enquiry, but nothing measures which clicks become sales, the shop is missing from the local map, and the reviews are a click away instead of on the page. Our edge over every other cold call is that we read their site and have the detail. The expansion into a home store is the live angle: that new range needs demand on purpose.

Open (with respect, do not skip)

"Gregg, thanks for making the time. Before today I went through inspiredfloors.co.uk properly. First thing I'll say is a lot of what most firms get wrong, you've got right. You've actually got analytics running, so you're not flying blind; you've got enquiry forms and WhatsApp on there; and you've got two Prestige Awards and a real showroom behind you. So this isn't me telling you the site's bad. It's about a few things that would turn the searches and showroom interest you already get into booked home visits and sales, and let you advertise on purpose, especially as you widen the range. Give me a few minutes and if it's not useful you tell me and we're done. Fair?"

Tie-down: "Fair?"

Frame (the home visit, the fitted work and the new range)

"Ben, who set up the call, mentioned you're expanding into more of a home store, the furniture, the sofas, the beds, on top of the flooring. The way I see it, the money jobs are the fitted floors and now the bigger furniture buys, and the moment one of those starts is usually a free home visit or someone walking into the showroom. Is that fair?" (Let him confirm and talk about the business.)

"Here's what I noticed. The site earns trust well and it captures the enquiry, but a couple of things after that are left on the table. Nothing's measuring which clicks turn into booked visits, so you can't really run ads for the floors or the new furniture and know what they earn. The site's also missing the tag that puts a shop into the local map results, which for a showroom is footfall. And your reviews are a click away on Google instead of on the page. That's the opportunity, and it's machinery, not the shop."
