Where the bigger jobs come from

The high-value work and the new range

Fitted flooring, the growing furniture and home range, and the system that sells more of both

A single rug or a tin of accessories is the bread and butter. The jobs that move the business are the bigger ones: a full house of LVT or herringbone supplied and fitted, carpets through a whole home, and now a room of furniture, a new sofa, beds and dining set as the range widens into a home store. Those are considered purchases, worth many times a small sale, and they are exactly where a proper system pays for itself. The event that wins them is usually a free home visit and measurement or a showroom appointment, and this page is about getting more of those booked on purpose, including for the new range.

The honest answer first. One advert does not win a full fitted floor or a room of new furniture on its own. What advertising does is put you in front of the right person at the moment they are searching, and keep you there over the days or weeks they take to decide. The visit, and then the sale, is won by your showroom, your awards and your advice, and by following up properly. Ads get you on the shortlist; your store and your service close the work.

Who buys the high-value work locally

  • Homeowners re-flooring a room or a whole house. Usually researched carefully, compared across two or three stores, and increasingly chosen on reviews and on seeing and feeling the product in a showroom before they commit.
  • People part-way through a renovation or a move. New flooring plus furniture for the same rooms at the same time. This is exactly the customer your move into furniture and home styling is built for, the one who would rather buy the floor, the sofa and the beds from one trusted local store than chase three.
  • Repeat and referred customers. Someone you fitted a floor for who now needs a bedroom furnished, or their neighbour who saw the finished room. These are the warmest buyers and the ones a simple follow-up keeps coming back.

What those buyers check before they enquire

For a purchase this size, a buyer does their homework first. The ones you want to win over are checking:

  • Whether the store is real, established and local, and whether they can visit a showroom. Your Motherwell showroom and twenty-five years are a real advantage here.
  • Reviews and awards that show other people trusted them. You hold two Prestige Awards; the reviews just need to be on the page, not a click away.
  • That the range actually covers what they need, now that a customer can get the floor and the furniture in one place.
  • That they can book a home visit, a measure or a showroom appointment in one step from the page they are reading, without hunting for a contact page.

Where paid search fits

  • Commercial-intent searches for the fitted work: "LVT flooring Motherwell", "herringbone flooring Glasgow", "carpet fitters Hamilton". These are people ready to spend and worth sitting at the top for.
  • Searches for the new range: "sofas Motherwell", "furniture store Lanarkshire", "beds near me". A measured campaign is how you create demand for the home store rather than waiting for footfall to discover it.
  • A landing page per product and area, with the booking form in the hero and your showroom, awards and free home visit up front, so the click that cost money lands somewhere built to convert, not on a general homepage.
  • Conversion tracking first, always. None of this can be run sensibly until a conversion tag is in, because otherwise you are spending without knowing which clicks became booked visits or sales. That is the first fix in the audit and the foundation for all of the above.

The follow-up is where the money is

Bigger jobs are rarely won on the first contact, and a store widening its range has a real chance to sell the same customer more than once. The firms that do this well have a simple, consistent follow-up, and most do not. A system that would fit this business:

1

Every enquiry captured and logged, with what they asked about and the likely value, so nothing slips through on a busy Saturday in the showroom.

2

The home visit or appointment booked quickly, then the quote followed up on a set schedule rather than when someone remembers, because a quiet quote is a lost one.

3

The range cross-sold to the right people: the customer who just had a floor fitted gets a gentle nudge about the matching furniture or the new sofas, which is exactly what the home-store move is for.

4

After each job, a photo and a review request. This directly fills the proof gap in the audit: the review that wins the next customer and the captioned photo of the finished room both come from the job you just completed.

5

Source tracking on every job, so you can see which searches and which pages bring the fitted work and the furniture sales, and put more behind what works.

What we would measure

Home visits and appointments booked by area and product, cost per enquiry on the paid campaigns, enquiry-to-sale rate, and the value of the follow-up pipeline, including repeat sales into the new range. No forecasts and no promises on ranking or volume; the point is to make the high-value work and the new range visible and steerable instead of waiting on footfall.
