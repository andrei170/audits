Getting you out of the day-to-day comms

John, you told Andrei you want to streamline the business and get out of the day-to-day comms. Right now every callout, clean and question comes to you or the office on WhatsApp or Gmail, and someone types every reply, books every engineer and chases every invoice by hand. That is where the 10-hour office day goes.

This page is what we would build to change that. It is a starting point to shape with you: nothing here is priced, and we have not put a number on hours saved, because that depends on answers only you have.

1. One inbox and one phone line for every job
Everything that comes in, in one place
  • WhatsApp, phone calls, Gmail and your website form all land in one shared inbox, each message tied to the venue it came from.
  • One business phone line for the office and you, with missed calls answered by a text straight away, so a callout never waits for someone to be free.
  • You and your office manager see the same thing: who asked what, what was booked, what is still open. Nobody has to scroll back through a chat to find a job.
How it works with WhatsApp
  • You keep WhatsApp: venues carry on messaging you the way they do now. It plugs in through WhatsApp Business, using Meta’s official connection for businesses.
  • WhatsApp lets a business reply freely while a conversation is open (up to 24 hours after the venue’s last message). Messages you start yourself after that, such as clean reminders and arrival times, go out as short pre-approved templates, which we write and set up with you.
  • Whether your current number connects as it is, or moves onto the business connection, depends on how it is set up today. We check that with you before anything changes, and nothing moves without your say-so.
2. The AI answers, triages and books, you approve
So you and the office approve instead of typing
  • It reads each message and works out what it is: a callout, a clean to book or move, an install enquiry, a question, a payment.
  • It asks the triage questions you would ask: which venue, which lines, no pour or fobbing or warm beer or gas, is the whole bar down, a photo of the cooler.
  • It books the nearest free engineer from the diaries and tells the venue when to expect him.
  • You set the rules: what it may book on its own, what it drafts for you to approve with one tap, and what always comes straight to you. Anything it is not sure about, it passes to you rather than guessing.

3. Dispatch for your 3 engineers
Jobs on their phones, not in a chat
  • Each engineer has his own diary, and the job arrives on his phone with the venue address, access notes, lines, cellar notes and any photo the venue sent.
  • Photos and a voice note on site become a tidy job sheet, and the venue signs off on the phone.
  • The office sees where everyone is: next job, free slots, what is running late.
4. Line-clean schedules that run themselves
Each venue on its own cycle
  • You set the cycle per venue, whatever you agree with the licensee. (Your website says lines should be cleaned at least every seven days; the system uses the cycle you set.)
  • A WhatsApp reminder to the venue before the clean is due; they confirm or pick another time by replying, and it lands in the engineer’s diary.
  • An overdue list, so a venue that has lapsed gets a nudge, then a call from the office, before it drifts to someone else.
5. Invoicing and chasing
From sign-off to paid
  • The invoice goes out when the engineer signs the job off, with the job sheet and photos attached.
  • Polite reminders at intervals you choose, until it is paid, then it is marked paid on its own.
  • Connected to your accounting software if you use one. [Which one: to confirm on the call]
6. Brewery and installation projects on their own board
  • Installs, including the brewery work you do in London, run on a separate board: enquiry, site survey, quote, scheduled, installed, invoiced, paid.
  • Survey notes and photos from the engineer’s phone feed the quote, which you check and send.
  • Parts list and plans attached to the install job, a handover sheet to the client when it is done.

You do not need to learn AI

You told Andrei you wish you knew AI but cannot get into it because you are too busy. You do not have to. We build it, set it up around how you work, and look after it. Your part is checking what it drafts and saying yes or no, from your phone.

What we would need from you
  • How jobs come in now and the questions you always ask on a callout, so the AI asks the same ones.
  • Your venue list, however you keep it (WhatsApp contacts, Gmail, spreadsheet, invoices), and which venues are on a regular clean.
  • Your engineers’ areas and hours, your call-out rules, and how you charge for each job type.
  • Access to set up WhatsApp Business and a phone line in your business’s name, and your accounting software if you use one.
  • A little of your and your office manager’s time to check it before it goes live.
What stays in your name
  • Your venue list, messages, photos and job history are your data, in an account in your business’s name.
  • Your WhatsApp, phone number, email and Google listing stay yours.
  • If you ever stop working with us, the data comes with you.
