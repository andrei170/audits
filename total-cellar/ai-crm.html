Why this comes first

Jordon told us your main interest is AI and a CRM, so this is the first part of the pack. Here is what we would build for a drinks dispense firm like yours, based on the work your website and Facebook page describe: line cleaning, maintenance, breakdowns, new installations, event bars and party kegs, for pubs, bars, clubs and restaurants.

Everything below is a starting point to shape on Friday around how you actually work. Nothing here is priced, and we have not put numbers on hours saved or money made: those depend on answers only you have, so they are questions for the call.

1. A CRM built around venues, not just names
Every venue is an account
  • Pubs, bars, clubs, restaurants and event clients as accounts, each with the licensee or manager, site address, access notes and opening times.
  • Equipment and cellar notes per venue: number of lines, products, fonts, cooling, gas set-up, anything you noticed last visit.
  • Photos from every visit stored against the venue, so the next engineer (or you, six weeks later) sees the cellar before arriving.
Every job has a type and a stage

Job types: line clean, maintenance, breakdown, install, event bar or party kegs. Every job moves through the same stages: Enquiry → Quoted → Booked → Done → Invoiced → Paid. One board shows where everything is.

2. Line-cleaning schedules that run themselves
Each venue on its own cycle
  • You set the cycle per venue, whatever you and the licensee agree. (Your own website says lines should be cleaned at least every seven days; the system does not assume any interval, it uses the one you set.)
  • Automatic reminders to the venue before a clean is due, and to you, so nothing relies on memory.
  • An overdue list: every venue past its cycle, at a glance, so a lapsed customer gets a call before they go elsewhere.
  • The venue can confirm or move the slot by replying to a text.
3. An AI receptionist for breakdowns
When you are in a cellar and the phone rings
  • Missed-call text-back: anyone you cannot answer gets a text straight away, so the call does not go to the next engineer on Google.
  • Out-of-hours triage: the AI asks whether the cellar is down, which venue, and what is showing (no pour, fobbing, warm beer, gas), and asks for a photo.
  • Booking into your diary for non-urgent jobs, and an urgent alert to you for a breakdown, with the venue’s history attached.
  • You stay in control: you choose what it can book and what it must pass to you.

4. Quotes, job sheets and getting paid
From the cellar floor to the invoice
  • Photo and voice notes to a job report: talk through what you found and fixed, take photos, and the assistant writes a tidy job sheet for the venue.
  • Quotes from the same notes for installs and repairs, using your own rates and parts list, for you to check before it goes out.
  • Invoices raised when the job is marked done, sent automatically, and polite payment chasers until it is paid.
  • Connected to your accounting software if you use one. [What you use now: to confirm on the call]
5. Reviews and a monthly report
  • A review request after each job, by text, with your Google review link. We could not find a Google Business Profile for you (see the audit), so setting one up comes with this.
  • A monthly report: venues on a regular cycle, cleans done and overdue, breakdowns attended, and revenue by venue, so you can see which venues are worth the most and which have gone quiet.
What we would need from you
  • Your list of venues, however you keep it now (spreadsheet, phone contacts, invoices), and which are on a regular clean.
  • The job types you do and how you charge for each, so quotes and invoices match how you work.
  • Your hours, call-out rules and areas, so the AI books and triages the way you would.
  • Access to your phone number for call forwarding, and to your accounting software if you use one.
  • An hour or two of your time to check it before it goes live.
What stays in your name
  • Your customer list, venue records, photos and job history are your data, in an account in your business’s name.
  • Your phone number and your Google listing stay yours.
  • If you ever stop working with us, the data comes with you.
Questions for Friday

We have not guessed any of these. The answers decide what is worth building first.

  • How do jobs come in today, and who answers when you are in a cellar?
  • How many venues are on a regular clean, and how do you track who is due?
  • How many breakdown calls a month, and how many come out of hours?
  • How long does paperwork take you each week: job sheets, quotes, invoices, chasing?
  • What does a closed bar cost a venue per hour on a Friday night, and does that change how fast they need you?
