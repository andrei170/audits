The short answer

Commercial work is won by being on the shortlist, then by following up. A facilities manager does not see an advert and sign a maintenance contract that afternoon. They shortlist, ask for documents, get a few quotes and wait for sign-off. So the website has to do the shortlisting for them, and the follow-up has to carry the quote to a yes.

This page shows who buys, what each buyer needs to see, a maintenance contract offer and an enquiry form you could put on your site.

Who buys, sector by sector
Schools, academy trusts and collegesWho decides

The school business manager or the trust’s estates lead

What they need

Fire alarm servicing, emergency lighting testing, intruder alarms and CCTV, and access control at the gates and reception. Work booked into holidays and half terms.

What your website would need to show them

A schools section, a reference from another school, and a clear line on how your engineers are vetted for working on a school site.

Care homes and surgeriesWho decides

The home manager, or the group’s compliance lead

What they need

A fire alarm that is serviced on time, emergency lighting tests, quick call-outs when a fault sounds at 2am, and service records ready when an inspector asks.

What your website would need to show them

Your call-out times in writing, 24/7 monitoring explained in plain words, and a reference from another care setting.

Warehouses, factories and industrial unitsWho decides

The site, operations or facilities manager

What they need

Larger fire alarm systems designed from drawings, CCTV over yards and loading bays, access control, and monitored intruder alarms.

What your website would need to show them

A write-up of a similar-size site, your insurance level, and RAMS ready to send before you set foot on site.

Offices and business parksWho decides

The facilities manager or the managing agent

What they need

Access control and CCTV across shared entrances, fire alarm servicing across several tenants, and visits planned outside office hours.

What your website would need to show them

A multi-site or multi-tenant example, and a maintenance contract they can read before they ring.

Landlords, HMOs and blocks of flatsWho decides

The landlord, letting agent or block manager

What they need

Fire alarms and emergency lighting in the shared areas, regular testing, and records they can show the council or their insurer.

What your website would need to show them

A landlords section, and one contract that covers several properties on one renewal date.

Main contractorsWho decides

The project manager or quantity surveyor

What they need

Fire and security designed from drawings, installed and commissioned to the programme, with handover paperwork done properly.

What your website would need to show them

Project write-ups, your certifications with numbers, and whether you hold Constructionline, CHAS or SafeContractor (to confirm with you).

⚠ The standards and needs above are general trade knowledge for discussion. Which sectors you want more of, and what you already do for them, is the first thing to agree on the call.

What every commercial buyer checks first
  • Certification they can look up: your site shows BAFE SP203-1 and logos for FIA, SSAIB and ECS. A certificate number and a link to each register saves them a phone call
  • Insurance and the level of cover
  • RAMS for working on their site
  • Similar jobs: the same kind of building, the same size of system
  • A reference from another manager, like the one you already have from Redrock Automation
  • Practical things: call-out times, 24/7 monitoring, invoicing on account

Today most of this is not on your site, and the first line Google shows them is home alarms from £299.

A maintenance contract offer, written for your website

Service work can be booked ahead, so it fills November to January. Fire alarm systems are serviced at regular intervals under BS 5839-1, so a contract signed in the autumn puts visits in the diary through the winter, every year it renews. Below is a mock-up of how the offer could read. What goes in each level is your call, and there are no prices on it.

Essential

For a single site that just needs to stay compliant

  • Fire alarm servicing visits under BS 5839-1 (typically twice a year)
  • Service record and certificate after every visit
  • Reminder before each visit is due
Standard

Everything in Essential, plus

  • Emergency lighting annual test (BS 5266) and a log for the monthly checks
  • Intruder alarm and CCTV maintenance visits
  • Priority call-outs during working hours
Complete

Everything in Standard, plus

  • 24/7 monitoring through an Alarm Receiving Centre
  • Out-of-hours call-outs
  • A named engineer and a yearly review of the whole system

Each level ends with one button: “Ask for a maintenance quote”, which opens the form below. And every system you have already fitted is a client worth offering one of these to before winter.

What the system does
  • Replies within a minute to every enquiry, from your name and number
  • Reminds the buyer the day before a site survey
  • Follows up every quote on a schedule you approve, until they say yes or no
  • Nudges before budgets are set, as many organisations plan spending ahead of the new financial year in April
  • Reminds clients before each service visit and contract renewal
  • Asks for a reference after each job, which is what the next commercial buyer wants to see
  • Shows which enquiries came from where: website, Google, MyBuilder or a referral
What stays with you

The site survey, the price and the relationship. Every message is written with you and approved by you before it goes out.
