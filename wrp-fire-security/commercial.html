The honest answer

One ad does not win a big commercial contract. A facilities manager does not see an advert and sign a large install or a maintenance contract that afternoon. Commercial buyers shortlist, ask for documents, get several quotes, wait for a budget and often a sign-off from someone else.

So ads do a different job here. They get WRP onto the shortlist of the people who buy this work, and keep you in front of them for the months it takes to decide. The follow-up is what closes it.

Who buys fire and security work locally

The organisations that pay for installs and maintenance contracts, and the person who usually makes the call:

  • Warehouses, factories and industrial units: the site, operations or facilities manager
  • Office buildings and business parks: the facilities manager or the managing agent
  • Blocks of flats and mixed-use buildings: the managing agent or block manager
  • Housing associations: estates, asset or compliance managers
  • Schools, academy trusts and colleges: the business manager or estates lead
  • Care homes and surgeries: the home manager or the group’s compliance lead
  • Councils and public bodies: usually through a tender or an approved-supplier list

Which of these are your best jobs is the first thing to agree, because it decides every ad, page and message below.

What they check before they ring you
  • Third-party certification: your site shows BAFE SP203-1 and logos for FIA, SSAIB and ECS. Buyers and their insurers often check these on the scheme’s own register, so a certificate number and a link save them a step
  • Insurance: public liability and the level of cover
  • RAMS: your risk assessment and method statement for working on their site
  • Photos and write-ups of similar jobs: the same kind of building, the same size of system
  • References: another manager who will vouch for you, like the one you already have from Redrock Automation
  • Practical things: response times, 24/7 monitoring, out-of-hours call-outs, invoicing on account

Today a buyer has to ring to find most of this out, and the first thing Google shows them is home alarms from £299. Many will move to the next name on the list.

Where ads actually fit
1. Google search, for commercial searches only

Searches like “fire alarm maintenance contract”, “commercial CCTV installer” or “access control for offices” in your towns are typed by people with a job to give out. We would test a small set of these, with domestic and DIY searches blocked, sending clicks to a commercial page, not the home page.

2. LinkedIn, aimed at the people who buy

LinkedIn can show your work to facilities, estates, compliance and property managers by job title, in the North West only. The aim is not an instant enquiry. It is that when their alarm contract comes up for renewal, WRP is a name they already recognise.

3. Staying in front of people who already looked

Anyone who visits your commercial page keeps seeing your work for months afterwards. That matches how long these decisions take.

4. A commercial page that does the shortlisting for them

One page with your certifications and their numbers, photos and write-ups of commercial jobs, the documents buyers ask for, and a “Book a site survey” form that asks the type of site, the systems on it and when the current maintenance contract ends. Every ad above points here.

The follow-up is where commercial work is won

A big commercial quote can take weeks or months to turn into a yes. Many are lost not on price but because nobody followed up when the budget finally came through. This is the part a system does better than a busy director running jobs.

Every commercial enquiry moves through the same stages, on one screen
  1. New enquiry
  2. Site survey booked
  3. Quote sent, with its value
  4. Following up
  5. Waiting on budget or sign-off
  6. Won and scheduled
  7. Service visit or contract renewal due

So at any moment you can see every open quote, what it is worth, and which ones need a nudge today.

What the system does for you
  • Replies within a minute to every enquiry, from your name and number, with your certifications attached
  • Reminds the buyer the day before a site survey
  • Follows up every quote on a schedule you approve: a few days after, a week after, two weeks after, then monthly, until they say yes or no
  • Nudges before budgets are set, because many organisations plan spending ahead of the new financial year in April
  • Renewal reminders: fire alarm systems are serviced at regular intervals under BS 5839-1 (typically every six months), so every system you fit can become a service visit, and every contract has a renewal date worth chasing before anyone else does
  • After each job: a short photo report and a request for a review or reference, which is what the next commercial buyer wants to see
  • Tells you which enquiries came from which ad, so money goes where the commercial work comes from
What stays with you

The site survey, the price and the relationship. Every message is written with you and approved by you before it goes out, so nothing is sent in your name that you would not say.

What we would measure

Commercial enquiries, site surveys booked, quotes sent and their total value, quotes won, and contracts due for renewal. No number of jobs or contracts is forecast here. Commercial work is too lumpy for that, and we would rather show you your own figures after the first few months.
