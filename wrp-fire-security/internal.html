Positioning in one line

Ask in this order
1. Tell me about the biggest job you did this year (the 18-month ~£176k project). Who was it for, and how did it find you? Was WRP the main contractor or subbing to someone?
2. Which kind of client do you want more of: warehouses and industrial units, offices, managing agents, housing associations, schools, care homes? Which pay best and pay on time?
3. 'More independent' next year: does that mean less subcontracting for main contractors, and more direct clients?
4. The 3 rolling contracts: what are they, what are they worth a year, and how did each one start?
5. From first contact to a yes on a commercial job, how long? Who signs it off on their side?
6. How many commercial quotes are out right now, and roughly what are they worth together? (the follow-up gap in his own number)
7. After you send a quote, what happens? Who chases it, when, and where is it tracked? (expect: his head, phone, Rosie?)
8. Have you lost a big one that just went quiet? What happened?
9. What do buyers ask for before they say yes: BAFE/SSAIB certificates, insurance level, RAMS, references? Confirm which certifications are current and the numbers (site shows BAFE SP203-1 + FIA, SSAIB, ECS logos; registers not checked).
10. Every system he installs needs servicing (BS 5839-1, typically 6-monthly). Does every install become a maintenance contract? Who reminds the client at renewal?
11. Capacity: 13 engineers, 8 vans, 5-20 jobs a day. How many more contracts could he take on before hiring? Is Nov-Jan a real gap?
12. Have you tendered, or got on an approved-supplier list (council, housing association, Constructionline/CHAS)?
13. Why do you think ads can't bring this work in? What did you see or try? (ask before answering the objection)
Objections
“We get our work from word of mouth and previous connections.”Agree, it is clearly working (the conversion on referrals is very high). Then: the person who hears about WRP still Googles WRP, and the first line they read is home alarms from £299, and the About page is called “New Page”. We are making the referral land, not replacing it. And word of mouth alone is why he is reliant on others for the big jobs, which is what he wants to change.
“Ads can’t win contracts.”Agree first: a cold ad never closes an 18-month install. Ads get WRP on the shortlist of facilities managers by job title, and follow-up closes. Then the fallback reply below.
“MyBuilder fills the diary.”Good for small diary-filler jobs, and keep it. But MyBuilder buyers are mostly domestic and price-shopping; it does not bring a managing agent with a 40-building maintenance contract. Different job.
“We’re busy enough / hand to mouth, reinvest everything.”Then start with the free fixes and the follow-up on quotes already out, which costs time not ad spend. Point at the Nov to January lull: that is when the follow-up and renewal work can be set up.
“Send me something to look at.”Send the hub link (assets.html), but book the next call before hanging up.
Fallback reply if he still says ads can’t do it

“Fair. Then let’s start with the part that does not depend on ads: following up the quotes you already have out, and making sure every system you have fitted gets a service and renewal reminder. If that wins back even one contract, it pays for itself, and ads become an add-on you can test later.”
