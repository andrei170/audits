Objections
“We get our work from word of mouth and previous connections.”Agree, it got you to £750k and a record year (~90% conversion). But word of mouth does not choose commercial work: it brings whoever rings. Then: the person who hears about WRP still Googles WRP, and the first line they read is home alarms from £299, and the About page is called “New Page”. We are making the referral land, not replacing it. And word of mouth alone is why he is reliant on others for the big jobs, which is what he wants to change.
“Ads can’t win contracts.”Agree first: a cold ad never closes an 18-month install. Ads get WRP on the shortlist of facilities managers by job title, and follow-up closes. Then the fallback reply below.
“We’ve no time for this.” (15 min booked)Agree, so keep it tight: one question on the goal, one on margin, show the £299 Google line, then book the full call. Don’t try to cover the pack.
“MyBuilder fills the diary.”At what margin? Diary-filling residential is the under-10% work. Good for small diary-filler jobs, and keep it. But MyBuilder buyers are mostly domestic and price-shopping; it does not bring a managing agent with a 40-building maintenance contract. Different job.
“We’re busy enough / hand to mouth, reinvest everything.”Then start with the free fixes and the follow-up on quotes already out, which costs time not ad spend. Point at the Nov to January lull: that is when the follow-up and renewal work can be set up.
“Send me something to look at.”Send the hub link (assets.html), but book the next call before hanging up.
Fallback reply if he still says ads can’t do it

“Fair. Then let’s start with the part that does not depend on ads: following up the quotes you already have out, and making sure every system you have fitted gets a service and renewal reminder. If that wins back even one contract, it pays for itself, and ads become an add-on you can test later.”
