26 / 100
0 of 14 checks passed, 2 partly there, 12 not yet in place.
The business behind the site is clearly a serious commercial installer: BAFE SP203-1, open and closed protocol systems, design from drawings, 24/7 monitoring. The website does not show it. It leads with home alarms from £299, hides its About page as “New Page” and has nothing written for a commercial buyer.
GradeWhat we checkedWhat we found (2 November 2026)
FA page for commercial and industrial buyers
There is no commercial or industrial page. The home page mentions “commercial, residential, or industrial” once, in one sentence. A facilities manager looking for a contractor for a warehouse, school or care home has nothing written for them.
FWhat Google shows under your name
The search description on your home page reads “Home or buisness alarms starting from £299, Home or buisness cctv systems starting from £350”. That is a domestic, price-led message (with a spelling slip) on the first thing a commercial buyer reads about you.
FYour About page
Your About page is live at /blank and is titled “New Page”, in the menu and in the browser tab. It has no main heading and no search description. It holds your best material: your story, your directors, your BAFE certification and your only testimonial.
DAccreditations shown
⭐ Your About page names BAFE SP203-1, and the home page shows BAFE, FIA, SSAIB and ECS logos. That is real buyer currency. But they are pictures with file-name labels, with no certificate numbers and no link to the scheme’s register, and the home page text only says “3rd party accreditations” without naming them.
FReviews and references
One testimonial, from the General Manager of Redrock Automation, and it is on the “New Page” rather than the home page. No review score anywhere on the site.
FProof of work
The home page photo slideshow is captioned with file names such as “Screenshot 2026-07-07” and “gallery 1_edited”. No job write-ups: no site type, no system fitted, no scale of job.
FEnquiry form
One form on the home page: first name, email, address, phone, message. It does not ask what kind of site, which systems, or whether they want a maintenance contract or a site survey, so a commercial enquiry looks the same as a doorbell job until you ring back.
FTap-to-call
Your mobile is shown as plain text, not a tap-to-call link. Your landline only appears inside the search description, not on the page.
FService pages
Five service pages (fire alarms, access control, intruder alarms, CCTV, maintenance), 79 to 161 words each, with no form or phone number on them. They are set up as one-hour “Quote” booking slots.
CHeadings and page titles
⭐ Each main page has one main heading, which is right. The home page title ends “Cctv&Alarms”, and the About page has no main heading at all.
FLocal business markup
The only markup tells search engines “this is a website”. Nothing says you are a fire and security contractor, where you work, or your hours.
FArea coverage
“The North West” only. No page for St Helens, Warrington, Wigan or Newton-le-Willows itself. One nearby competitor has a page for each of those towns.
FMeasurement
No Google tag, no Google Ads tag and no Meta pixel in the page code. Nothing on the site records where a visitor or an enquiry came from.
FReady for commercial ads
No commercial page to send a click to, no survey form, no tracking. An ad today would land on a page that leads with £299 home alarms, and nobody could tell which clicks became a site survey.

⭐ The hard part is already done: the certifications and the experience. Almost every failure above is about the website not showing them, which is fixable without a rebuild.

⚠ Not assessed, and left out of the score rather than guessed: page speed, Google Business Profile and Google reviews, search positions, and anything inside an ads account.
