Our first call, 17 July 2026
What you told us then
  • You were not short of work. The problem was that enquiries came in from the website, the phone, Google and directories, all in different places.
  • When the team was on site, calls went to voicemail and quotes went out with nobody following them up.
  • Every enquiry got treated the same, so a large school job and a single door waited in the same queue.
  • You and Leanne were holding it together between you.
What we showed you
  • An audit of how enquiries were handled, which graded the business D+ (42 out of 100). The trade itself scored highly. Speed to reply, missed calls and having everything in one place scored lowest.
  • A mock-up of a CRM: one board where every enquiry lands, an instant reply to every new lead, a text back when a call is missed, and automatic follow-ups on day 2 and day 5.
  • The idea of a repeat inspection list: under the Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022, communal fire doors in buildings over 11 metres need checking every quarter and flat entrance doors every year, so every door you have fitted is a future inspection.
The thinking we agreed on

Fix the leaks before turning up the tap. You did not need more leads. You needed to stop losing the ones you had.

What has changed since
  • You are now fully booked until the end of January.
  • The work has grown in size: hotels, Westminster council, Greenwich, plus commercial and domestic jobs.
  • The problem has moved from handling the work to having the people to do it.
For Tuesday

We would like to hear how the last four months have gone: how enquiries are being handled now, what worked and what did not, and then spend most of the call on hiring.
