🔴 Why Indeed Was Never Going To Work
🔴🔴 A qualified roofer with twenty years behind him is already employed

He is not browsing a job board on a Tuesday evening. So any channel built around people actively looking for work reaches the wrong half of the trade by design.

⭐⭐ Which also explains the quality complaint exactly. If the only people who see your advert are the people currently out of work, you are selecting from the part of the trade that is available rather than the part that is good. That is not bad luck, it is the channel doing what it does.

⭐ Reaching a working tradesman means interrupting him where he already is — a social feed, a trade group, the merchant counter — not waiting where jobseekers go.

🔴 Your website has 252 words on it

Thirty-six pages in your sitemap, and the homepage carries 252 words. For comparison, the other roofing sites we audited this week run to one and a half thousand.

⚠️ Which matters for hiring as much as for customers. A good roofer deciding whether to come and work for you will look you up. Right now there is almost nothing to find, and no careers page at all.

🔴 No tracking, and no accreditation shown

No analytics, no tag manager, no pixel. And we found no NFRC, CHAS, TrustMark or Checkatrade on the pages we read. ⚠️ That is not evidence you do not hold them. It is evidence a customer, or a roofer, cannot see them.

⚠️ A Gmail address on a company that does three to four roofs a week

Your published contact is efroofing.con@gmail.com. At three to four roofs a week you are turning over real money and it reads smaller than you are. ⭐ Half an hour to fix.

What The Missing Roofer Is Costing

Your own numbers

£2,800 – £10,600 a roof

At three to four roofs a week you are already a substantial operation. ⚠️ We do not know how many roofs you are turning away for lack of a qualified hand, and that is the number that decides whether this is worth doing. One extra roof a week at your mid-range is roughly £350,000 a year.

⚠️ Arithmetic on the range you gave us, not a forecast. And it only means anything if the work is there to take.
⭐ What We Are Not
⛔ Said plainly before anything else

AG1 is a marketing company, not a recruitment agency. We will not interview anybody, assess a roofer's competence, check a reference or tell you who to take on. We cannot guarantee a hire, or a number of applicants, and anybody promising you that is guessing.

⭐ What we can do is the advertising half — getting the right people to see that you are hiring and giving them a reason to want it. The deciding stays entirely with you.

⚠️ And if the honest answer is that the pay is below the going rate, we would say so rather than take your money.

What We Would Want To Ask
  • 1. How many applied, actually? ⭐ Zero, or a handful who were no good? Different problems.
  • 2. What does the advert say, and does it state the pay? ⚠️ If not, that is probably most of it.
  • 3. Employed or subcontract? They attract completely different people.
  • 4. What is the contract you are tied into for a year? ⚠️ We would want to know before suggesting anything that overlaps it.
  • 5. How many roofs are you turning away?
  • 6. What is good about working for you? ⭐ Three to four roofs a week is steady work, and steady work is worth a lot to a roofer. It is not on your site.
⭐⭐ Question 4 comes first. If you are tied into a marketing agreement for a year we need to understand what it covers before we propose anything, and we would rather find that out now than after.
