⚠ Read the net line again. You are turning over £20,000 to £25,000 a month and keeping £3,000 to £4,000 of it. That is the real problem, and more £300 tests does not fix it.

The two things that are actually happening to your business
🔴🔴 1. Your main revenue line was a one-off legal event, and it has nearly finished happening

Your words: “the tests we're doing is EICRs, and they became law that they had to be done. So nearly every landlord's now got them done.”

⭐⭐ That is not a marketing problem. That is a market that has been consumed. The regulation created a one-time wave of mandatory tests, you rode it well, and the wave has passed. You also said the property managers have “maybe about another 20 or 30 there” left, and that the company who “promised us 500 houses” has produced none.

🔴 So no amount of advertising for EICR tests would help you. The people who needed one have one. Anybody selling you EICR leads right now is selling you a market that no longer exists, and I am not going to be the second company to do that to you.

🔴🔴 2. Meanwhile the profitable line collapsed by 97%

300 → 7

Two years ago: 300 installed. Between last year and this year: seven.

And on those, your words: “the profitability in them there is amazing, and you're not stressed about getting more than one done a day.”

⭐⭐ So the work you are full of is the work with the thin margin, and the work that collapsed is the work with the good one. That is the whole picture in one sentence, and it is why the plan I had yesterday was wrong.

Your EV collapse may not be your fault, and which reason it is changes what to do
⭐⭐ You said the big EV jobs were “down in Dublin”. That matters more than it sounds.

Dublin is the Republic of Ireland, which is a different grant regime from Northern Ireland. And in January 2024, the Irish SEAI home charger grant was cut from €600 to €300 - halved.

⚠ Your 300 installs were about two years ago and the collapse to seven came after. The timing lines up with that cut. We are not telling you that is the cause - we cannot prove it from here, and it would be exactly the kind of confident claim you have been sold before. But it is a candidate, and it is checkable.

🔴 And the second candidate, which you said yourself

Your words: “they were big ticket jobs down in Dublin, and that's not as if it was our own private work, we were sort of subbed in for most of it.”

⭐⭐ If the 300 installs came to you through a main contractor, then they did not stop because homeowners stopped wanting chargers. They stopped because that contractor's pipeline stopped, or because they stopped using you.

🔴🔴 That distinction decides the entire plan, and it is the single most important thing to settle on this call:

  • If the 300 were subcontracted volume, then “get more EV work” means replacing a commercial relationship - finding another contractor or developer with a pipeline. Advertising to homeowners would not touch it.
  • If they were your own retail customers, then it is a demand problem and advertising is the right instrument.

⚠ Those two need completely different work, and we would be guessing which one you need until you tell us.

⚠ One more piece of context on your home market

In Northern Ireland the domestic grant is the UK OZEV scheme, and OZEV closed to houses on 31 March 2022. It still pays £350, but only for flats and rental properties.

⭐ So for a NI homeowner in a house there has been no charger grant for four years - which is worth knowing before anybody spends money advertising home chargers here. ⭐ But it also points at the part of the market that still has a grant: landlords and flats. And landlords are the people you already have relationships with through the property managers.

⚠ We have not verified whether you are an OZEV-approved installer. The grant can only be claimed through one, so it is the first thing to check if that route interests you.

⭐⭐ Why this section is on the page at all: you lost £6,000 to somebody who never asked why your work had changed shape. Before we sell you anything we would rather establish whether the EV market moved, your contractor moved, or the grant moved - because two of those three cannot be fixed with marketing and we would rather say so now than invoice you first.

Why Yell cost you £6,000 and brought you bulb changes

You were with them a year, at £500 a month. That is £6,000. You got about six jobs, and in your words they were “30, 40, 50 pound jobs, going out and changing bulbs or going out and just stuff you don't want to be doing.”

⭐⭐ Here is the mechanical reason that failed, and it is not that the leads were fake. Yell sold you volume of any electrical job. Nobody ever specified what a good job was. £500 a month buying £40 call-outs loses money at every possible volume - even if they had sent you fifty of them, you would have lost more, not less.

🔴 So the lesson is not “advertising does not work for me.” It is that nobody ever told that channel which jobs you wanted. A directory listing cannot be told that. It has one setting: electrician, near this postcode.

⭐ That is the single difference in what I am proposing. Targeting by job type and job value, which is the one thing a directory can never do and the one thing your £6,000 never bought.

The three doors, in your order of preference

All three are things you told me you want. None of them is an EICR test.

  • 1. EV chargers. You said you prefer them, the profitability is “amazing”, you already own the equipment, and one a day is comfortable. You are currently doing one a fortnight. ⭐ The gap between those two numbers is the largest single opportunity in your business and it needs no new skill, no new kit and no new staff.
  • 2. Commercial and industrial testing. Your words: “definitely in the commercial, because it's a completely different ballgame” and it pays “a hell of a lot more” than a £250 domestic test. ⭐ And you already have two testers and the equipment sitting in the van. This is the same work your people already do, sold to a buyer who pays several times as much for it.
  • 3. New-build estates. Your own idea: “a new estate's being built, I wouldn't mind trying to get into there. At least it means we can grow and know that there's money coming in that way.” ⭐ This is the only one of the three that produces predictable recurring volume rather than one job at a time, and it is the one a website genuinely cannot win on its own. It is a relationship with a developer or an M&E contractor. ⚠ You already proved you can do that part - you sat down with a list of 100 numbers and did not stop until you had three.
