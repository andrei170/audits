The short answer
⭐ Our recommendation: WooCommerce with a firearms-approved processor
  1. It does what you described without paying for what you did not ask for. Firearms paid online and collected in store is standard WooCommerce. Gearfire and AmmoReady charge $149 to $179 a month and up, mostly for distributor feeds and drop shipping you have not mentioned needing.
  2. The lowest fixed monthly cost while the store is new. You told us on 23 October that Georgia Safe Rooms is "running off of capital just to get in the doors", so a monthly platform fee matters.
  3. It sits inside the website you have already approved, with the same look, menus and categories, and you own it.

When Gearfire would be the better choice: if you want to list distributor stock you do not keep on the shelf, or want their own payment processing in one package. Essentials at $149 a month is the cheaper of the two specialists.

⚠ The step that takes longest is the card processor, so it starts first

Whichever you choose, a processor has to approve a firearms store before it can take a payment. None of the three publishes how long that takes. With the online store aimed at Black Friday, the application should go in this week. They will usually ask for your FFL, your business documents and a look at the website.

What your store has to do
  • Firearms paid for online, collected in store for the background check. In your words on 20 October: "if it's a gun, it'll go directly to us, and then they'll have to come in and pick it up. We'll do the background check and all that stuff."
  • Parts and accessories shipped direct, plus ammo, safes, vault doors, gun mounts, body armor, suppressors and accessories.
  • Checkout that asks for name and date of birth, never a Social Security number.
  • You add and edit products yourself, with categories inside categories. On 23 October: "It's that online store that's going to have a lot of editing through like the first few months as we bring in new inventory."
  • A card processor that will actually approve a firearms store. This is the part that rules most platforms out.
  • A low monthly cost while the new store finds its feet.

So Shopify and Square are out. Stripe is not a plug-in option either, because it needs a separate approval with no published timescale. That is why the three options below all come with a processor that works with firearms dealers.

What stays the same whichever you pick
  • Background checks stay in store, as now. No store platform does that part for you.
  • Suppressors go through the ATF's own approval process, so we suggest showing them online as "enquire in store" rather than pay-now.
  • Ammo shipping rules differ by state. Simplest at launch is ammo for pickup only, or shipping inside Georgia only, until you decide.
  • Safes and vault doors are heavy. Either a delivery quote per order or "call to arrange delivery".
