The findings, in plain English
1. Google Ads is not being told what works

When someone clicks your ad and then calls you or fills in your form, Google Ads only learns about it if something on the website (or in your ad account) reports it back. We found no Google Ads conversion tag anywhere on the site or in Tag Manager. GA4 is installed, and it is possible your Ads account counts calls another way (for example Google's own call reporting on the ad, or goals imported from GA4). We cannot see inside your account, so this is a question for Sunday, not a verdict.

Why it matters: when Google Ads cannot see which clicks became jobs, it optimises for clicks, not customers.

2. One phone number for everything

The same number, (909) 719-7711, appears on all 24 pages, and we found no call tracking. That is good for customers, but it means a call from Google Ads, Angi, your Google listing and a returning customer all look the same. That is why it is hard to know what an Angi job really costs compared with a Google job.

3. The service pages have no form

Your 15 service pages are the natural place for an ad to send someone. Each has a tappable number, but none has a form. Someone searching at 10pm who does not want to call has nowhere to ask for a quote on that page.

4. No pages for the towns you serve

You list 20 towns, from Glendora and San Dimas to Pasadena, Rancho Cucamonga and Irvine. Every service page is written for Glendora only, and there is no page for any other town. People search "AC repair West Covina", and Google prefers a page about West Covina.

5. Hours and small details disagree
  • Most of the site says 24/7. The Contact and Areas pages say "Mon to Sat: 24 hours, Sun: closed". Your markup tells Google you are open every day.
  • The map coordinates in your local business markup are empty.
  • The Instagram link points to instagram.com/popular/sean-patrick-hvac, a general tag page, not a business profile.
  • Six pages have no main heading.
  • GA4 loads twice on every page (directly and through Tag Manager), which can count each visit twice. Worth checking in your GA4 reports.
6. Proof you have, but do not show

Public CSLB records list Sean Patrick HVAC, Inc. under licence 1107117 (C-20, active). Homeowners in California often check this before a big job, and it is not on the site. Public listings also show reviews on Yelp (27, as listed by MapQuest) and HomeAdvisor (9 at 5.0), which the site does not mention.
