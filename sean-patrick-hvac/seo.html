AI answers (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and similar)
Named in two of three answers, both times from a directory, never from your own site

We asked three questions the way a homeowner would ask an AI assistant:

  • “Who is the best HVAC company in Glendora California for a new AC system?” Led with Canyon Air Systems (473 reviews on Experience.com) and Comfort Zone Inc. (146 reviews, Trane dealer). You were listed under “other local options”, with only a note that your licence is verified.
  • “Which HVAC company should I call for emergency AC repair in the San Gabriel Valley?” Named AIRONE Heating and Cooling, General Heating & Air, Global Green Solutions and Central A/C. You were not named, although your site offers 24/7 service.
  • “Who installs heat pumps near Covina and Glendora CA?” Named you, from your Switch Is On listing, alongside Mad Mechanical, Home Runners Heating & Air, Souza’s, McLay Services, Comfort Zone and Glendora Hills AC.
⭐ The pattern

The firms AI leads with have hundreds of checkable reviews on directories (Experience.com, HomeAdvisor, Angi) and a clear brand or dealer status. When you do appear, it is because a directory says what you do. Your own 15 service pages are not the source, which means AI is not reading them as answers yet.

Three questions on one AI search tool on one day is a snapshot, not a measurement of every assistant.

What the new site does well, and what it still misses
Credit where due

The rebuild added 15 service pages with FAQs, Google Analytics 4 and Tag Manager. That is real groundwork, and we would build on it rather than replace it.

  • No town pages. Nothing for Azusa, San Dimas, Covina or La Verne, so those searches have nowhere to land. Service Champions has 14 town pages, including Glendora.
  • The location in your site’s code is empty. The business details Google and AI read carry no map position.
  • Opening hours disagree: the site says 24/7 in one place and “Sunday: closed” in another.
  • Six pages have no main heading, so search engines have to guess what they are about.
  • No licence number shown, and no financing or written warranty. Cypress shows its licence and financing on its site.
  • Three different addresses across directories. AI tools read those listings and lose confidence when they disagree.
  • No Google Ads conversion tag, so Google cannot learn which clicks became jobs (covered in the audit).
What we would do
Build on the 15 pages you have
  • A page per town you want work from, starting with Glendora, Azusa, San Dimas and Covina.
  • The location, hours and services filled in properly in the code, with one main heading on every page.
  • One address, phone and start date everywhere: Google, Yelp, HomeAdvisor, Angi, BBB and Switch Is On.
  • Your licence number, financing and warranty shown where homeowners and AI can see them.
  • A review request after every job, so the count keeps climbing where AI looks.
  • Short answer pages for questions people ask: “Repair or replace a 15-year-old AC?”, “Is a heat pump worth it in the San Gabriel Valley?”

⭐ Why it matters alongside ads and Angi: ads and Angi stop the moment you stop paying. These pages keep bringing calls you own, and they make every ad cheaper because Google rates the landing page higher.
