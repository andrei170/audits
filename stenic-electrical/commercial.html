The honest answer first

One ad does not win a commercial contract. A facilities manager does not see an advert and hand over an office fit-out or an annual emergency lighting contract that afternoon. Commercial buyers shortlist, ask for documents, get several quotes, wait for a budget and often need a sign-off from someone else.

So ads do a different job here. They get Stenic onto the shortlist of the people who buy this work, and keep you in front of them for the months it takes to decide. The follow-up is what turns that into signed work.

Who buys the work on your commercial and industrial pages
  • Facilities and office managers: office installations, LED upgrades, emergency lighting, periodic testing
  • Letting agents, landlords and block managers: EICRs across a portfolio, landlord surveys, remedial work
  • Care homes and supported housing: nurse call systems, warden alarms, disability adaptations, fire alarms
  • Factories, warehouses and industrial units: mains distribution, switchgear, power factor correction, UPS, high bay lighting
  • Caravan parks and event organisers: park connections and meter units, exhibitions and site temporaries
  • Main contractors and fit-out firms who need a reliable NICEIC electrical subcontractor
  • Schools, councils and housing associations: usually through a tender or an approved-supplier list

Which of these are your best jobs is the first thing to agree, because it decides every page, ad and message below.

What they check before they ring you
  • NICEIC approval (you have it) and the registration number
  • Insurance: public liability and the level of it
  • Risk assessments and method statements (RAMS) for working on their site
  • Health and safety accreditations, if you hold any. Some buyers only use approved contractors
  • Photos of similar jobs: the same kind of building and size of job
  • References: another manager who will vouch for you
  • Practical things: working out of hours, invoicing on account, how fast you reply

Today your commercial and industrial pages show only the list of services. No commercial photo, no documents, no client types, no accreditation and no way to request a site survey. A buyer has to ring to find any of that out, and the phone number is not on those pages.

Where ads actually fit
1. Google search, for commercial searches only

Searches like “emergency lighting installation Kent”, “commercial electrician Dartford” or “EICR for landlords” are typed by people with a job to give out. We would test a small set of these, with domestic and cheap searches blocked, sending clicks to a commercial page, not the home page.

2. LinkedIn, aimed at the people who buy

LinkedIn can show your work to facilities managers, property managers and contractors by job title, in Kent and South East London only. The aim is not an instant enquiry; it is that when their emergency lighting test is due, Stenic is a name they already know.

3. Staying in front of people who already looked

Anyone who visits your commercial page keeps seeing your work for months afterwards. That matches how long these decisions take.

4. A commercial page that does the shortlisting for them

One page with commercial photos, the documents buyers ask for, and a “Request a site survey” form that asks the premises, the job and the timescale. The sample site has one: see it here.

The follow-up is where commercial work is won

A commercial quote can take weeks or months to turn into a yes. Most are lost not because the price was wrong but because nobody followed up when the budget came through. That is the part a system does better than someone on site all day.

Every commercial enquiry moves through the same stages, on one screen
  1. New enquiry
  2. Site survey booked
  3. Quote sent, with its value
  4. Following up
  5. Waiting on budget or sign-off
  6. Won and scheduled
  7. Retest or renewal due

So at any moment you can see every open quote, what it is worth, and which ones need a nudge today.

What the system does for you
  • Replies within a minute to every enquiry, from your name and number
  • Reminds the buyer the day before a site survey
  • Follows up every quote on a schedule you approve until they say yes or no
  • Nudges before budgets are set, as many organisations plan spending ahead of the new financial year in April
  • Reminds you before retests come round: EICRs, periodic inspections and emergency lighting tests recur, so a finished job is the start of the next one
  • After each job: sends a photo report and asks for a review or a reference
  • Tells you which enquiries came from which source, so money goes where commercial work comes from
What stays with you

The site survey, the price and the relationship. Every message is written with you and approved by you before anything goes out in your name.

What we would measure

Commercial enquiries, site surveys booked, quotes sent and their total value, and quotes won. No number of jobs is forecast here. Commercial work is too lumpy for that, and we would rather show you your own figures after the first few months.
