What AI tools are told about your site
Your robots.txt opts you out of AI training. It does not hide you from search

Every website has a small file called robots.txt that tells crawlers what they may read. We read yours on 24 October and again on 25 October 2026. Alongside the usual WordPress lines, it says:

  • GPTBot: do not read anything. This is OpenAI’s crawler for training its models. ChatGPT’s live search uses a different crawler (OAI-SearchBot), which is not blocked
  • Google-Extended: do not use anything. This opts you out of Google’s Gemini models. It does not affect normal Google search or Google’s AI Overviews, which use the ordinary Google crawler
  • CCBot (Common Crawl, a public web archive many AI models are trained on): do not read anything
  • A crawler named “AdsBot”: do not read anything. Google’s ads checker identifies itself as AdsBot-Google. Whether it treats this line as its own is not something we can see from outside, so it is worth tidying up before any Google Ads run rather than finding out after.

What this means in practice: Google search, ChatGPT search, Perplexity and other assistants can still read the site. The bigger reason AI tools say little about Stenic is the one on the scorecard: seven pages, no page per service or town, and markup that never says you are an electrician. Blocks like these are often switched on by a hosting or SEO setting rather than chosen, so it is a question for Monday, not an assumption.

Markup and structure

Your SEO plugin adds basic company markup (name, phone, email, founded 2006). It does not say you are an electrician, which services you offer, where you work or your reviews. The sample site carries full Electrician markup with your services, areas and Checkatrade rating.
