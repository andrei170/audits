Positioning in one line

“Twenty years of NICEIC work and 80 perfect reviews that a new customer cannot see, and a phone number they cannot find.” Lead with the visibility gap (phone, form, name, proof), then commercial: shortlist plus follow-up plus retest reminders. Commercial is a real thread here (36 commercial/industrial services listed), so run the commercial question set.

Ask in this order
1. How is the business split today between domestic, landlord EICRs, commercial and industrial? Which pays best?
2. Biggest commercial or industrial job in the last year: who for, what worth, how did they find him? (keep values off client pages)
3. Which buyers does he want more of: facilities managers, letting agents, care homes, factories, main contractors, caravan parks?
4. From first enquiry to yes on a commercial job, how long? Who signs off on their side?
5. How many commercial quotes are out right now, and roughly what are they worth together? (the follow-up gap in his own number)
6. After a quote goes out, what happens? Who chases, when, where is it tracked? (expect: his head, phone or a notebook)
7. A big one that went quiet: what happened? (let him tell it; that is the CRM pitch)
8. What do commercial buyers ask for: insurance level, RAMS, CHAS/SafeContractor/Constructionline? (only list what he holds)
9. Recurring work: EICR retests, emergency lighting tests, maintenance contracts. Does he remind customers, or wait?
10. Capacity: filed average headcount is 1. Does he bring in other electricians for bigger jobs? How big a job can he take?
11. Checkatrade: annual cost, enquiries per month now, and why reviews dropped to 1 in 12 months. Still worth it?
12. Who built the site and set the robots.txt AI blocks? Deliberate? Is there a web person we would be stepping on?
13. 24 hour call-out: Checkatrade ticks it, the site does not say it. Does he actually want out-of-hours work?
14. Has he ever tendered or tried approved-supplier lists? Why does he think ads can or cannot bring commercial work?
15. Decision: is it just him? (the co-founding director resigned in 2023)
Objections
“Most of my work is word of mouth / Checkatrade.”Agree: 80 reviews at 10/10 proves the work. Then the number: Checkatrade itself says 1 review in the past 12 months. Ask what it costs a year and how many jobs it sent this year. Word of mouth still needs a site that shows a phone number on the home page; today it does not.
“Ads can’t get commercial work.”Agree first: a cold ad does not close a contract. Ads get him on the shortlist and keep him there; follow-up closes. Ask why he thinks so before answering. Fallback: start with following up the commercial quotes already out and retest reminders on past EICR and emergency lighting customers.
“I’m busy enough.”Then the point is better jobs, not more: steer toward the commercial and recurring testing work he prefers, and the text-back so he stops losing time on calls he cannot answer.
“I already have a website.”Do not knock it. Show facts: phone not on home page and not tappable, one form with no phone field, his name not on the site, commercial pages with no proof. If he wants to keep the host, the sample site is a checklist. Our value is the system around it.
“It’s just me, I can’t take on much more.”Filed headcount is 1, so take this seriously. Size it to capacity: higher-value commercial and recurring testing work rather than volume, plus the follow-up so fewer quotes go cold.
