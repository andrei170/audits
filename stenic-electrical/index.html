Your website, page by page
A 7-page WordPress site that makes it hard to call you
  • Seven pages: Home, Services, Domestic, Commercial, Industrial, Gallery and Contact. Service pages last changed 5 August 2025; the footer still says ©2024.
  • No phone number on the home page. It appears once, on the contact page, as plain text that a phone cannot tap.
  • One form on the site, on the contact page: name, email, message and a domestic, commercial or industrial tick box. No phone number, postcode, job type or photos.
  • About 60 services listed as bullet points across three pages. EICRs, fuse boards, rewires, fire alarms, emergency lighting and EV charge points have no page of their own.
  • No tracking: no Google Analytics, Tag Manager, Google Ads tag or Meta pixel.
  • Your name is not on the site except inside customer reviews, and there is no About page.
  • ⭐ Good: a clear logo, accreditation logos, three real reviews on the home page, a link to Checkatrade, an email on your own domain, and a gallery of 27 real job photos.
The commercial and industrial pages

The commercial page lists 20 services (emergency lighting, full office installations, nurse call, warden alarms, trunking, caravan park connections, periodic testing, landlord surveys) and the industrial page lists 16 more (mains distribution, LV switchgear, power factor correction, fire alarms, UPS, generator backup). That is serious capability, but neither page shows a single commercial photo, a client type, an accreditation, a document a buyer would ask for, or a way to request a site survey. See the commercial page.

Checkatrade
Strong record, slowing down

Your Checkatrade profile is excellent: 80 reviews, 10 out of 10, with customers naming you personally and praising the explanation, the tidiness and the timekeeping. But Checkatrade itself says the score is “based on 1 review over the past 12 months”, and the most recent review is from April. The profile also ticks “24 Hour Call-out”, which your website never mentions; worth deciding which is right.

Your reviews live on a platform you pay for. A Google review count could not be read (see below), so we cannot say whether customers are also leaving them on Google.
