Our first call, Tuesday 20 October

Kristina, this page is what you told us on Tuesday, what we showed you, what you asked, and what is still unchanged on the site. If anything here is wrong, tell us on Monday and we will correct it.

What you told us
⭐⭐ The business
  • Oregon has been built over about 20 years, mostly on word of mouth. You have also recently opened in Texas, where you said around 80% of customers now come from AI recommendations off the websites Kevin built. One caller said they found you through Claude
  • In Oregon you put the website and AI share at about 5 to 10%, and roughly another 10% comes from other HVAC companies that pass you work, because Kevin builds custom ductwork, fabricating sheet metal on site
  • Full residential HVAC, start to finish, without subcontracting, which you said most companies cannot do
  • Mostly smaller maintenance-type jobs, plus a handful of large projects a month. Large projects are sometimes booked out six to eight weeks
  • You would rather have more of the large jobs, and you said you have room for roughly another 100 jobs a month
  • The business was bigger in 2022. You put the drop down to a company pretending to be you in 28 states, Kevin moving from Oregon, and referring HVAC firms selling out to national brands. It has been rebuilding since, mostly through the website and AI
  • A team of four: you, Kevin, your son Tyler, and Tyler who runs the Oregon branch. You or Kevin answer every call, and you are the follow-up system, with Housecall Pro sending some of it
  • You had sold four mini-split installs and a full heat pump system the day before, with contracts still to send out
🔴 What has burned you before
  • Angie’s List, 2023 to 2024, on both its pay-per-lead and its monthly contract programs: leads that were shared, someone already on site by the time you called back within seconds, and jobs that were not HVAC at all. You said it roughly broke even, and you were held to the contract
  • Thumbtack: still on the account, not used this year because Kevin has been busy enough
  • Google Ads, run with Google directly: monthly campaigns and “thousands of dollars”, and nobody mentioned there was no tracking. Google has asked you to meet again this week
  • A Texas website from an agency that cost a lot, promised monthly checkups and delivered no updates and no SEO. Kevin took it over in April and moved it from the 60s and 70s to position 27
  • And the one that matters most: Google mostly recognizes The Duct Guys for air duct cleaning, which is not your focus, a leftover from the companies that ran the site before
What we showed you
  • The graded scorecard: 10 of 14 checks passed, graded C, 60 out of 100. The four that failed: no Google Ads tag or Meta pixel, no business type in the page code, no photos of finished work, no careers page
  • Four competitors: three of the four are set up to measure ads. You told us the big names in Oregon are AAA and First Call, so both are in Competitors Pt.2
  • The order we would do it in: count what comes in, tags ready, pages that take an inquiry, tell Google what you are, then ads on the jobs you choose, with follow-up alongside
What you asked us
  • “Other than correcting the tags within Google and running ads again, how do you correct it?” You said you are “a little iffy” about spending thousands again. Audit Pt.2 answers it point by point
  • Show comparable results from other HVAC companies, in booked jobs or closed revenue, not methods. We will go through these on Monday
  • The Facebook side and name recognition, and the system behind it
  • Do you take ownership of anything? No: everything built is in your name and you hold the logins
  • Contracts: you want to know something works before you commit money
Still unchanged on the site, re-checked 24 October
  • No Google Ads tag and no Meta pixel. Google Analytics is the only tag
  • Request forms only on the homepage, the Contact page and the blog page. None on the heat pump, duct replacement or town pages
  • Your service and town pages are labelled as blog articles in their code, not as an HVAC business offering a service
  • No photos of finished jobs and no careers page
