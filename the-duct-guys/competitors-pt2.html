What it shows
⭐⭐ Finding 1 · Nobody says what you do, including you

None of the five homepages mentions building ductwork on site, and neither does yours. You told us it is why other HVAC companies send you work. It is the one thing on this table that is yours alone, and it is invisible.

🔴 Finding 2 · They put their promises in writing

AAA states a lifetime workmanship warranty; Efficiency states a 2-year labor warranty and puts financing on its homepage; Sky has financing and a maintenance plan in its top menu. Your site states no warranty and mentions financing only in a blog post. On a full system, homeowners compare those lines side by side. Only what you actually offer should go up.

⚠ Finding 3 · You cannot win on review count. You can win on who answers

AAA, Sky, Efficiency and Better Air each claim 1,000 or more reviews; you show 67. Your reviews name Tyler and the owner, and Efficiency shows that a “Meet the Owner” page is worth having even at 1,500 reviews. For a four-person firm, the owner doing the work is the selling point. Your BBB profile is A+ with no reviews on it, which is a cheap place to start asking.

⚠ Finding 4 · You are older than most of them on the register

Your license dates from November 2007. Of the five, only AAA’s license is older; Efficiency’s is 2009, Better Air’s 2011, First Call’s 2022 and Sky’s current one 2023. Your homepage does not say how long you have been going; your About page does.

The short version: you are not behind on what you can do. You are behind on saying it, putting it in writing, and measuring what comes in. And AAA, the oldest name on this list, has no ad tracking either, so being set up first is still open to you.
