Each thing you said you need, checked again

Tuesday’s audit was about the website. This part takes the things you told us, one by one: what we found when we looked again on 24 October, and what would fix it. Kevin built and runs the site, so every fix below is something we would hand him in writing or do with his say-so.

1 · You said, 20 October

“The main issue is that Google mostly recognizes us for air duct cleaning, which is not our forte”

What we found: Your own site is not the problem. Every page title we read leads with HVAC (“HVAC Repair, Installation & Ductwork in Portland”). The listings about you elsewhere are. On 24 October: your BBB profile is filed under “Duct Cleaning”, its first category and the word in its web address; your Nextdoor page says you “specialize in air duct and dryer vent cleaning”; a Yelp listing describes “quality venting services”; and a Chamber of Commerce directory files you under real estate agents. Search engines read these listings alongside your site. ⚠ The Yelp and Chamber wording is from search results; we did not open those pages.

What fixes it: Rewrite every listing you control to say HVAC first, with the same description and categories everywhere: Google Business Profile, BBB, Nextdoor, Yelp. It costs nothing but time. ⚠ Not assessed: your Google Business Profile’s primary category, the single biggest signal. Worth opening together on Monday.

2 · You said, 20 October

“Kevin has custom ductwork, fabricating sheet metal on site. Being able to do the whole project all at once, not having to sub it out like other companies do”

What we found: No page on your site says this. We searched all 63 pages on 24 October: the words “fabricate” and “sheet metal” appear only in a general blog article on how duct installation works, and “custom duct installation” appears once, on the Troutdale page. None of the five competitor homepages we read says it either (see Competitors Pt.2).

What fixes it: One page that leads with it: custom ductwork and full system replacement, one crew, nothing subcontracted, with photos of Kevin’s work (marked as placeholders until he sends real ones) and a project request form. It is also the page you would send referring HVAC companies to.

3 · You said, 20 October

“Obviously the large ones. The ones you get more profit from”

What we found: The pages for large jobs cannot take an inquiry. Heat pump installation, duct replacement, AC and furnace installation: no form on any of them on 24 October. Someone reading about a full system can call or leave for Housecall Pro, and nothing records that they were there.

What fixes it: A short project form on every install page: job type and zip code first, then home size and timeframe, then name and phone. Large-job inquiries then arrive already sorted from the small ones. See Bigger jobs for where ads fit.

4 · You said, 20 October

“We poured thousands of dollars into [Google], yet the Google guy didn’t say anything”

What we found: Re-checked 24 October: still no Google Ads tag and no Meta pixel anywhere in your page code or your Google tag. So any campaign run on this site today still could not report which call or booking it produced. Google has asked you to meet again this week.

What fixes it: Tags in first, with no ad spend at all. Then, if you do meet Google, four questions: which conversions will the account count; how will a phone call be tied to an ad; whose name is the account in; and what report will you get each month. If they cannot answer those, the money is spent blind again.

5 · You said, 20 October

“I want to make sure something’s going to work before I waste money”

What we found: Angie’s List sold the same lead to several companies, so speed decided who won. An inquiry from your own site or your own ad goes to nobody else, and none of that is being counted today.

What fixes it: Measure first, then a small test on the large jobs only, in an ad account in your name, on your own card, with a daily budget you can stop at any time. You see every dollar and every inquiry yourself. Nothing here predicts the result; the point of the test is to find out cheaply.

6 · You said, 20 October

“For Texas, almost, I’d say, 80% of them are coming through AI recommendations”

What we found: AI assistants answer from what is published about you, much like search engines. So the listings in point 1 that call you a duct and dryer vent cleaner matter for AI answers too. ⚠ Your Texas website was not part of this audit; we do not have its address.

What fixes it: Protect what is working: nothing we change should disturb the Texas site. Send us its address and we will read it the same way, so Monday covers both states.

7 · You said, 20 October

“We’re not getting any traction on Facebook, really”

What we found: Your site links to Facebook; we did not assess the page itself. The site has no photos of finished work, which is also what a Facebook post needs.

What fixes it: Every finished install photographed once, used three times: on the site, in a Facebook post, and in the follow-up message to people still deciding. We will show you the planner on Monday.

⚠ Two smaller things from the re-check
  • Your service and town pages are labelled as blog articles in their code (“Article”), and the only “HVAC business” label sits on the blog page. Labelling them as services of an HVAC business is a short job for Kevin
  • Financing is mentioned only in a 2025 blog post, and no workmanship warranty is stated on the site. Two of the competitors state both on their homepage (see Competitors Pt.2)
Questions that change what we would build
  • Oregon, Texas, or both: which market do you want more large jobs in first?
  • What is your Google Business Profile’s primary category today, and who has the login?
  • What is the smallest job worth a site visit for a full system or ductwork quote?
  • How many quotes are out right now, and what happens after a quote is sent?
  • What do you offer in writing: a workmanship warranty, financing? Only what is true goes on the site
  • Does Kevin want to make the site changes himself, with a written list from us, or have us do them?
