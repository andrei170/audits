What this means for you
1Nobody near Canterbury is advertising for a finish carpenter, so the good ones are not job-hunting.

Out of 32 Reed results only two were carpentry, and neither was site finish work for a small firm. The carpenters you want are working, for themselves or for someone else. They will not see a job board. They might see an advert in their own feed showing an oak frame or a herringbone floor you laid, and then look you up.

Source: Reed and Totaljobs searches, 20 November 2026.

2The adverts that do run compete on vans and salaries, not on the work.

Morgan Sindall and Clarion Housing offer a van and a package for maintenance and voids work. A small firm wins a different way: better work, a boss who cares about the finish, and a say in how the job is done. Your site already says all of that; it just never says it to a carpenter.

Source: Totaljobs adverts, 20 November 2026.

3No small carpentry firm near you has a careers page. The first one stands out.

Of the ten firms we read, only Denne Joinery (a large joinery manufacturer) invites people to work with it. A small East Kent carpentry firm with a proper work-with-us page and a five-minute application would be the only one of its kind.

Source: Home pages and careers checks of each firm, 20 November 2026.

4Your past adverts probably were not the problem on their own.

Without a page to land on, a way to apply or a pixel, an advert can only send people to a customer contact form, and it cannot learn who is a good fit. One bite from two or three adverts is about what that set-up produces. The fix is the landing page and the filter, then a small advert left running.

Source: Your site and code, 20 November 2026; your adverts as you described them.

5On Google, they are visible and you are not.

The firms around you show 5.0 from 12 to 78 reviews (Sam Bowditch 78, DC Carpentry 47 at 4.9). We could not find a listing for you. A candidate checks a firm much as a customer does.

Source: Google Maps from central Canterbury, 20 November 2026.
