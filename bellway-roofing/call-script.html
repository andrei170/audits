The call, in order
  1. Open: "Thanks Tommy. Before I show anything, what made you take the call, and what would make this half hour worth it?"
  2. Where work comes from: "Mostly word of mouth? Is BookABuilder bringing anything? Checkatrade, Facebook groups?"
  3. The set-up: "Is it just you, or a crew? Are you on your own jobs full-time?"
  4. The work he wants: "Repairs, fascias and guttering, or full re-roofs? Which do you want more of?"
  5. Value: "What does a typical job come to?" Let HIM say what one more a week is worth.
  6. The redirect: "When I typed in bellwayroofing.co.uk it took me to Southern England Roofing. Is that on purpose?" Listen. Do not accuse.
  7. Pain check: "1 to 10, how much do you want more of the right jobs in the new year?"
  8. Summary back, wait for a clear yes, then share the audit, then the demo website.
Finding capacity and revenue without sounding salesy
  • "How many jobs a week can you get through? How far ahead are you booked?"
  • "What does a re-roof take you, start to finish?"
  • "Are you turning work away, or are there gaps?"
  • Then let HIM do the sum: "So one more re-roof a month is worth roughly what?"
How to pitch it
  • Frame: "Right now, when someone hears your name and looks you up, they find another roofer. We fix that first: your own site on your own address, reviews on show, and a quote form, then adverts once it is working."
  • Anchor on one extra job a month, not lead counts.
  • Fit: website first, ads only if he has capacity. Figures are in the brief. Say them only if asked, never in writing.
  • No promise of enquiries, jobs or dates.
Close
  • "If your own site was live on your address with your roofs and a quote form, is there any reason not to start this side of Christmas?"
  • "Then I need 5 to 10 photos of roofs you have done, and the towns you want."
  • Not ready? Book the follow-up before you hang up. Recap by email, no pricing.
