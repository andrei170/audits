The part that matters most

You have paid for Google Ads for about ten years and you do not know your cost per job, and nobody sends you a report. That is not a problem with Google Ads. It is a problem with how it is run and reported. Everything below is built so you see, every week, what each lead and each job cost you.

Six questions to ask whoever runs your ads now
If they can answer these quickly, they are doing a decent job. If not, that is the gap.
  1. What did each lead cost last month, and each job? Not clicks. Leads and jobs.
  2. Can I see the search terms report? The actual words people typed before they clicked. Most roofing accounts we look at pay for “roofing jobs”, “roofing courses” and “DIY flat roof”.
  3. Is a phone call counted as a conversion, and only if it lasts over a minute? Otherwise wrong numbers and sales calls count as leads.
  4. Whose name is the ad account in? It should be yours. If you left tomorrow, you keep ten years of data.
  5. Do the ads send people to a page about the job they searched for, or to your home page?
  6. Why do leads swing from 2 to 10 a week? Budget running out mid-week, weather, or bids changing are the usual reasons, and each has a fix.
How we would run it: four campaigns, each with its own budget
1Roof repairs and leaks (fast, steady work)
  • Who it is for: homeowners with a problem now
  • Searches it bids on: roof repair Winchester, roof leak Southampton, roofer near me, emergency roofer Eastleigh
  • Where it sends them: a repair page with a short form and tap-to-call

Why this one: it is the highest-intent search there is and the quickest job to book. It also gets you into the house for the bigger work.

2Re-roofs and replacements (the big tickets)
  • Who it is for: homeowners planning a new roof
  • Searches it bids on: new roof cost Hampshire, roof replacement Winchester, re-roofing Southampton
  • Where it sends them: a replacement page with real jobs, your workmanship warranty and a free survey form asking the postcode and type of roof first

Why this one: fewer searches, but each job is worth many repairs. Its own budget means repairs never eat it.

3Flat roofs, fascias, soffits and guttering
  • Who it is for: smaller jobs that fill gaps in the diary
  • Searches it bids on: flat roof repair, fascias and soffits Winchester, guttering replacement Southampton
  • Where it sends them: a page per service

Why this one: cheaper clicks and quick installs, and a way in to roof work once they trust you.

4A reminder for people who looked
  • Who it is for: only people who visited the site and did not get in touch
  • Searches it bids on: shown on YouTube, Gmail and other sites, plus Facebook and Instagram
  • Where it sends them: back to the page they were on

Why this one: a re-roof is a considered buy. Most people compare two or three quotes before they ring.

And three things that sit under all four
  • Call tracking: every call from an advert is recorded as a lead, with the search that caused it.
  • Negative keywords from day one: jobs, courses, DIY, materials, prices-only searches and towns you do not cover never get a click.
  • A weekly report: leads, cost per lead, booked surveys, jobs won and cost per job. The thing you said you have never had.
