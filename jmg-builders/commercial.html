The honest answer first

Your Checkatrade profile lists commercial work, insurance work, new builds, agricultural buildings and equestrian stables, and your company is registered for commercial as well as domestic building. One ad does not win a new build or a farm building. Ads and a good site get you on the shortlist and keep you there; follow-up over weeks or months wins the job.

Who buys this work round here
  • Farmers and estates: agricultural buildings, stables, yards, barn conversions for diversification or holiday lets.
  • Self-builders and families with a plot: new houses and bungalows, usually with an architect already involved.
  • Architects and designers who need a builder they can recommend to clients.
  • Insurers and loss adjusters: storm and damage repairs.
  • Landlords, property managers and local businesses: alterations, repairs and small commercial units.

Which of these are your best jobs is the first thing to agree, because it decides every page and message below.

What they check before they ring you
  • Photos of similar jobs: the same kind of building and size. You have them; they are just not written up.
  • Insurance: public liability cover and its level.
  • Trade bodies: you are an FMB member; TrustMark and CSCS too if current.
  • References: your 49 reviews, several from repeat customers.
  • Risk assessments and method statements for farm and commercial sites, if you provide them.
  • How fast you reply and whether the quote is clear and in writing.

Today your website shows the reviews and some photos, and none of the rest. The sample site has a commercial and agricultural page with a site-visit request form.

Where ads fit
1. Searches with a big job behind them

“Barn conversion builders County Durham”, “agricultural building contractors”, “self build builder Teesdale”. Typed by people with a project, not a small repair. Each sent to a page about exactly that.

2. Staying in front of people who already looked

A self-builder or farmer who visited your new build page keeps seeing your stone houses for months. That matches how long these decisions take.

3. Architects and estates, by name

A short list of local architects and estates who could send you work, kept in touch with directly: a project write-up each time you finish something they would care about.

The follow-up is where big jobs are won
Every bigger enquiry moves through the same stages, on one screen
  1. New enquiry
  2. Site visit booked
  3. Quote sent, with its value
  4. Following up
  5. Waiting on planning, finance or sign-off
  6. Won and scheduled
  7. Finished, review and photos requested

So at any moment you can see every open quote, what it is worth, and which needs a nudge today.

What the system does for you
  • Replies within a minute to every enquiry, from your name and number.
  • Reminds the customer the day before a site visit.
  • Follows up every quote on a schedule you approve: a few days after, a week after, then monthly, until it is a yes or a no.
  • Keeps in touch while they wait for planning, which can take months on a new build or a conversion.
  • After each job: photos and a review or reference request, which is what the next big customer wants to see.
  • Shows which enquiries came from where, so effort goes where the big jobs come from.
What stays with you

The site visit, the price and the relationship. Every message is written with you and approved by you before it goes out.

What we would measure

Bigger enquiries, site visits booked, quotes sent and their total value, and quotes won. No number of jobs is forecast here. Big jobs are too lumpy for that, and we would rather show you your own figures after the first few months.
