What this means for you
1You have the most pages for the most towns, by a distance.

16 town pages and 30 service pages, against 0 to 5 town pages for everyone else. Strategic lists 100 addresses, but many are articles about local parks and museums rather than electrical work. Content is not your gap.

Source: Each firm’s sitemap, read 13 and 14 November 2026.

2Reviews are the gap on Google Maps: Buchanan has 304, you have 50.

Your 5.0 average is as good as it gets, and your reviews are recent and detailed. Buchanan Electric has been collecting since 1980 and shows 4.9 from 304. A steady ask after every job is the only way to close that.

Source: Google Maps, 13 and 14 November 2026.

3Two competitors lead with generators, a big Gulf Coast job.

F&D calls itself a Generac Certified Dealer; Strategic has pages for specific Generac and Cummins models and "in-stock" generators. Your Generator Installation page is long and helpful but names no brand, and the only next step is the same general booking page.

Source: fdelectservices.com and strategicelectricalsolutions.com home pages and sitemaps, 13 and 14 November 2026.

4Two of the five measure their visitors; you measure none.

Benjamin Electric runs Google Analytics 4 and a Meta pixel; Ivy Leach runs Google Analytics 4. They can see which pages and ads bring calls. Your site, with far more pages than either, carries no tracking at all.

Source: Home page code of each site, 13 and 14 November 2026. A tag shows a firm is set up to measure or advertise, not how much it spends.

5Three of the five show their contractor license number. You show the Master number.

Buchanan (TECL #23241), F&D and Ivy Leach (TECL 39848) put the contractor license on the home page. Yours shows 748217, Brandon’s Master license; the company’s TECL 40772 is not on the site.

Source: Home page text of each site and the TDLR register, 13 and 14 November 2026.
