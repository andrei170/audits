The short version
⭐ What is already working
  • 5.0 from 74 reviews on Google, and a claimed, well-filled Google listing.
  • Guild of Master Craftsmen member since October 2021, and an SIA installer member logo on every page.
  • Honest, expert writing: 18 blog posts and an FAQ, including why a three-day course does not make a stove fitter.
  • 45 real photos on the Gallery, 10 more and a video on the stonemasonry page.
  • Proper contact routes: tap-to-call on 27 of 29 pages and a professional email address.
⚠ What holds it back for hiring
  • Nothing on the site is aimed at a tradesperson. No careers page, no vacancy, no way to apply on any of the 29 pages.
  • Your listed-building work is invisible. "Listed" appears once, as a planning rule. No job story shows it.
  • Nobody is introduced. The 2025 team section has gone, so a candidate cannot see who they would work with.
  • Nothing is measured. The only analytics tag is a switched-off Universal Analytics one; no pixel, no Ads tag.
  • The 5.0 from 74 never appears on your own site.

What you told our team (as you said it, not checked by us)
  • Finding qualified engineers is extremely tough. You install solid fuel heating, and very few engineers can do it properly. A lot of the work is in grade one and listed buildings.
  • Jobs run to roughly six to eight thousand a day, with a small team you call "top drawer". You have been out on site for the first time in six months, working days and nights, seven days a week.
  • You plan to move into solar or biomass within a year, because the skilled staff to grow the current trade are not available. What holds that back: the time to learn solar, and the tax on reinvesting.
  • More jobs is not what you need: you are "drowning" in work. You book work in later, first come first served, because your reputation matters more than volume. You will not take on more until you have quality staff. The bottleneck is staff, not leads.
  • You used a specialist recruitment agency for six months (a package of three hires). You took on an operations person and a fitter; neither worked out, and both had gone within three weeks. There may be one hire left in the package.
  • You want a senior engineer who can be left alone on site: compliant with building and fire regulations, a high-quality finish, a professional manner. Not someone who learned on new builds and relies on nail guns and router jigs; you expect traditional skills like cutting a mortise and tenon.
  • In your trade, poor work can "kill people and burn houses down". There is no middle ground on quality. Staff cost a lot to employ, and a bad hire costs even more.

From the notes taken when the call was booked. Everything else on this page is what we measured ourselves.
