The plan, in order

Make the job visible and honest, filter hard, then test on site. The demo careers page shows steps 2 and 3 working, including what you would receive for each applicant.

1Agree the offer (when we speak)

Pay band, hours, van and tools, the travel area, and whether the trial day is paid. We do not guess any of these; you set them, and the advert says them plainly. A clear offer is what makes a working engineer stop scrolling.

2A careers page that sells the work, not the firm

One page on your site: the listed and historic jobs, the standard you hold, who they would work with, and what you will not accept. The demo shows it.

3An application that does the first sift for you

Five minutes on a phone. Knock-out questions that politely turn away new-build-only fitters, people who cannot cut a joint by hand, and anyone outside the travel area, then photos of three to five jobs they did themselves. Every applicant gets an instant, honest reply. You only see the ones who pass.

4Adverts where skilled tradesmen actually are

Facebook and Instagram adverts shown to people within commuting distance of Stirling who follow joinery, stonemasonry and heritage-building content. The picture is your own listed-building work, ideally a short clip of you on site. Employment adverts are aimed by area and trade interest, never by age or gender.

5Two pools, two adverts (only if you want the second)

Advert one: experienced stove and fireplace engineers. Advert two: skilled traditional joiners and stonemasons who would learn stoves from you. Kept separate, so each speaks to one person.

6A screen that protects your time

We read the photos and answers first. You get a short list, each with their photos and answers on one page. Then one 15-minute call per candidate, all booked into one slot in your week.

7A trial day on a real job

Set tasks you choose: set out a hearth, cut a mortise and tenon, plan a flue route, talk to the homeowner. Six hours tells you what an interview never will, before anyone is on the books.

8Bring them in properly

A first month paired with you, and a short sign-off list (building and fire regulations, finish standard) before they work alone. Most hires that go wrong go wrong in the first weeks; this is where it is caught.

9See it working

Each week: applications, how many passed the questions, photos reviewed, calls held, trial days booked. You can see whether it is working without chasing anyone.

What we are not saying

We cannot promise a hire, a number of applicants or a date. Senior stove engineers are scarce, and that is the whole problem. What this plan does is put your job in front of skilled people who are not looking, filter out the wrong ones before they cost you an hour, and test the right ones on a real job before you commit. On cost and tax, your accountant is the right person to ask.
