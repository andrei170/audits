What you told me you need, gone through properly

On 12 October I went through the first audit too quickly. This page goes back through what you actually said, one point at a time, and what each one means for the tree side.

1 · You said

“I just need help with leads on tree service.”

What we found: Online, your tree business goes by a different name from the one on the first audit. Directory listings show Robinsons Quality Tree Care, on (937) 450-4800, at 221 N Shaffer St. The A+ BBB rating and the 24 years belong to Robinson’s Quality Improvements, your home improvement side. Somebody looking at one can’t tell it’s connected to the other.
⚠ One listing also says the tree business started in 2021 and is run by two brothers. You told us you started it in 2017.

What fixes it: One name, one number and one story for the tree business, the same on the website, Google, Facebook and every directory.
✅ Settled on 15 October: the tree business stays separate from Robinson’s Quality Improvements, with its own contracts and insurance. So the website is the tree business only, and the roof side of a storm job is done by the sister company on its own contract.

2 · You said

“It would have been also like reviews being more visible.”

What we found: We found 3 reviews, all 5 stars, on HomeAdvisor. One reads: “Daniel was very efficient and hard working! Got the job done and cleaned up all the branches.” Another, from October 2025, talks about a four-tree job delayed by rain and still finished at a good price.
🔴 The Springfield tree services you compete with show between 17 and 49 reviews on the same kind of listing (see Competitors Pt.2).
🔴 And HomeAdvisor belongs to Angi. When the contract ends, those reviews stay on their site, not yours.

What fixes it: A review request sent by text after every finished job, going to your own Google profile, and your best reviews on the front page of the website. At 15 to 20 jobs a month, asking every time is how the gap closes. We can’t say how many people will leave one.

3 · You said

“Most of the time they don't even call back, or you leave a message, or you text them.”

What we found: Your 24-minute average callback is good, better than most. The problem isn’t speed, it’s reaching them. A lead from Angi has also been sent to other firms, so by the time you call, they’ve often talked to someone else or stopped picking up.

What fixes it: An automatic text within a minute of a form, from your number, with your name in it, so the first contact has happened before you’re off the tree. A missed-call text back for when you can’t answer. A form that asks how urgent it is, 1 to 4, so the emergencies get rung first. And because these people found you, nobody else got sent the same lead.

4 · You said

“I don't want all the leads to come in in the first week, and then I have to sit for three weeks.”

What we found: That pattern is how lead services usually work: you buy a monthly amount, they use it up early, then nothing until next month. It’s the billing, not how many people need tree work.

What fixes it: Search advertising runs on a daily budget, so enquiries come in across the month, and it can be turned down in a busy week and back up in a quiet one. The one exception is storms. After a storm, searches jump, and that’s the work you want most.

5 · You said

“Roughly right around 2,000 [a job].”

What we found: Directory averages for tree removal in Springfield are around $500 to $800. Your average is two to four times that. So you’re doing the big, awkward and dangerous removals, not quick trims.

What fixes it: The website and ads should go after big jobs: large trees near houses, storm damage, dead and leaning trees. Not small trimming, and not people looking for the cheapest quote. Somebody shopping on price in a directory is the wrong customer for a $2,000 job.

6 · You said

“I've done billboards, and I've had a website.”

What we found: Neither could tell you which jobs they brought in, and the website, robinsonsqualitytreecare.com, shows “Site Not Found” today (checked 12 and 14 October).

What fixes it: This time every call and every form is tracked to where it came from, so after a month you know what’s working, and you can stop what isn’t.

7 · You said

“75% of my work comes from door knocking.”

What we found: Door knocking works, and it’s your strongest channel. But every one of those jobs costs your own hours, on top of a seven-day week.

What fixes it: The aim isn’t to stop door knocking. It’s to have work coming in that doesn’t need you on a doorstep, and run without you except for answering the phone and doing the job.

⭐⭐ And the one you agreed with

When we put storm damage as the headline offer, one call, with the tree and the roof both handled, you said “that’s fine.” None of the Springfield tree services we looked at can offer the roof as well. It stays as the headline.

⚠ Questions that change what we’d build
  • ✅ Which name should the tree business trade under? Answered 15 October: Robinsons Quality Tree Care, kept separate
  • Roughly what do you pay Angi and Yelp a month? That’s the number anything new has to beat
  • Of your 15 to 20 jobs a month, how many are storm or emergency work?
  • Who answers the phone when you’re up a tree?
  • How far from Springfield will the crew travel?
  • Are you insured for tree work, and do you have a bucket truck or stump grinder?
