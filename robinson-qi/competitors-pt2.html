🔴🔴 Finding 1 · The gap is reviews, not quality

Your rating is the highest on the list. Your review count is the lowest, by a long way. When somebody compares five tree services on their phone, 3 reviews next to 43 or 49 looks like a new firm, whatever the stars say.

This is the one you raised yourself on Monday, and it’s the cheapest gap to close: a text asking for a review after every job.

⚠ Finding 2 · Nobody competes on big jobs

Directory prices for tree removal in Springfield average around $500 to $800. Your jobs average about $2,000. Those directories are full of people comparing prices on small jobs.

So don’t compete there. Go after the searches that mean a big job: emergency tree removal, tree on house, storm damage, dangerous tree. Fewer people search for them, but each one is worth more to you.

⚠ Finding 3 · Guyton is who you’re up against

Guyton Tree Care has the same A+ BBB rating, 24/7 emergency cover and free estimates, with a crew of four. Somebody with a tree down at night will find them before you.

⭐ What they can’t say: we’ll fix the roof too.

⭐⭐ Finding 4 · The roof is still the thing none of them have

All five firms above are tree only. After a storm, the homeowner has a tree in the roof and needs two companies. You’re the only one who can be both, and you’ve got 24 years of roofing behind it.

Where Angi fits

Most of these firms also turn up on Angi’s Springfield tree service pages. So an Angi lead often goes to you and to them at once, which is why people stop picking up. A customer who finds your own website and fills in your own form has only asked you.

The short version: you’re not losing on quality, price or experience. You’re losing on being found and looking established. Part 1 covered being found. Reviews and the storm offer cover looking established.
