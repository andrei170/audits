The plan, in order

Open the door, filter hard, then test on a real job. The demo careers page shows steps 2 and 3 working, including what you would receive for each applicant.

1Agree the terms (on Monday)

Self-employed, as you prefer: the day rate or price-work basis, the kind of jobs, how many days a week, whether he brings his own van and tools, and the insurance you expect. We do not guess any of these; you set them, and the page says them plainly.

2One page that opens the door

A "Work with us" page on your own site, in your own words: the work, the standard, the two of you, and exactly who you are looking for. The demo shows it. It costs you nothing to leave it open while you are busy, which is the point: the unicorn needs a door to knock on.

3An application that does the first sift

Five minutes on a phone. Questions that politely turn away anyone without real bespoke fitting experience, anyone who cannot scribe by hand, and anyone outside your 30 miles, then photos of three to five fitted jobs he did himself. Everyone gets an instant, honest reply. You only see the ones who pass.

4A small, steady advert, not a big push

Facebook and Instagram adverts shown to people within commuting distance of Wroughton who follow joinery and woodworking. Your own photos, ideally a short clip of you in the workshop. Run small and continuously, so the right person can turn up when they are ready. Aimed by area and interest, never by age or gender.

5Fix the tracking first

Make the Facebook pixel count visits on awiconcepts.com as well as www, so the advert learns which people actually apply. A quick change for whoever built your site.

6A screen that protects your time

We read the answers and photos first. You get a short list, each with their photos on one page, then one 15-minute call per person, booked into one slot in your week.

7A paid trial day on a real job

One day alongside you on a fit you choose: a scribe to an uneven wall, a run of doors set to your shadow gap, how he leaves the room. A day tells you what a month told you last time, before you rely on him.

8Bring him in slowly

A few jobs working with you, then one he runs alone that you check. That matches the slow, steady growth you want: one good joiner, not a team you cannot keep busy.

9See it working

Each week: applications, how many passed, photos reviewed, calls held, trial days booked. You can see it working without chasing anyone.

⚠ One thing to think about

Your site promises: "The brothers who quote are the brothers who fit." That is a big part of why customers choose you. A joiner working with you changes it slightly, so it is worth deciding how you would say it: for example, that every job is still quoted, set out and signed off by one of you. The careers page in the demo is written that way.

What we are not saying

We cannot promise a hire, a number of applicants or a date. Skilled bespoke fitters are scarce, which is the whole problem. What this plan does is put your work in front of good joiners who are not looking, turn away the wrong ones before they cost you an hour, and test the right one on a real job before you rely on him. On self-employed status and tax, your accountant is the right person to ask.
