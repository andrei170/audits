The honest answer

You are right that a single ad rarely wins a large commercial contract. A facilities manager does not see an advert and book a five-figure clean that afternoon. Commercial buyers shortlist, ask for documents, get several quotes, wait for a budget and often a sign-off from someone else.

So ads do a different job here. They do not close the contract. They get Diromax onto the shortlist of the people who buy this work, and keep you in front of them for the months it takes to decide. The follow-up system is what turns that into signed work.

Who buys this work

The organisations that pay for large exterior cleaning, and the person who usually makes the call:

  • Holiday parks, caravan parks and hotels: the park or operations manager, often before the season opens
  • Housing associations and property managers: estates, maintenance or contracts managers
  • Letting agents and block managers: the property manager, for communal areas, gutters and render
  • Schools, colleges, care homes and surgeries: the business or facilities manager
  • Retail units, industrial estates and car parks: the site or facilities manager
  • Councils and public bodies: usually through a tender or an approved-supplier list

Which of these are your best jobs is the first thing to agree, because it decides every ad, page and message below.

What they check before they ring you
  • Insurance: public liability cover, and the level of it
  • Risk assessment and method statement (RAMS): how you work at height and keep the public safe on their site
  • Health and safety accreditations, if you hold any (for example CHAS or SafeContractor). Many buyers will only use approved contractors
  • Photos of similar jobs: the same kind of building, the same size of job
  • References: another manager who will vouch for you
  • Practical things: out-of-hours or weekend working, invoicing on account, how fast you reply

Today none of this is on your website, so a buyer who finds you cannot tick their own boxes without ringing first. Most will move to the next name on the list instead.

Where ads actually fit
1. Google search, for commercial searches only

Searches like “commercial gutter cleaning North Wales”, “cladding cleaning” or “car park cleaning” are typed by people with a job to give out. We would test a small set of these, with domestic and cheap searches blocked, sending clicks to a commercial page, not the home page.

2. LinkedIn, aimed at the people who buy

LinkedIn can show your work to facilities, estates and property managers by job title, in North Wales only. The aim is not an instant enquiry. It is that when their gutters or render need doing, Diromax is a name they already recognise.

3. Staying in front of people who already looked

Anyone who visits your commercial page keeps seeing your before-and-after work for months afterwards. That matches how long these decisions take.

4. A commercial page that does the shortlisting for them

One page with your commercial photos, the documents buyers ask for, and a “Book a site survey” form that asks the site, the job and the timescale. The ads above all point here.

The follow-up is where commercial work is won

A big commercial quote can take weeks or months to turn into a yes. Most are lost not because the price was wrong but because nobody followed up when the budget finally came through. This is the part a system does better than a busy person up a ladder.

Every commercial enquiry moves through the same stages, on one screen
  1. New enquiry
  2. Site survey booked
  3. Quote sent, with its value
  4. Following up
  5. Waiting on budget or sign-off
  6. Won and scheduled
  7. Repeat or renewal due

So at any moment you can see every open quote, what it is worth, and which ones need a nudge today.

What the system does for you
  • Replies within a minute to every enquiry, from your name and number, with your documents attached
  • Reminds the buyer the day before a site survey
  • Follows up every quote on a schedule you approve: a few days after, a week after, two weeks after, then monthly, until they say yes or no
  • Nudges before budgets are set, because many organisations plan their spending ahead of the new financial year in April
  • Reminds you before a contract or annual clean is due, so you re-quote before anyone else does
  • After each job: sends before-and-after photos and asks for a review or reference, which is what the next commercial buyer wants to see
  • Tells you which enquiries came from which ad, so money goes where the commercial work comes from
What stays with you

The site survey, the price and the relationship. Every message is written with you and approved by you before it goes out, so nothing is sent in your name that you would not say.

What we would measure

Commercial enquiries, site surveys booked, quotes sent and their total value, and quotes won. No number of jobs is forecast here. Commercial work is too lumpy for that, and we would rather show you your own figures after the first few months.
