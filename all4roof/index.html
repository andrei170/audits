1. The address your listeners type
REQUEST https://all4roofs.com/ ANSWER 301 Moved Permanently GOES TO https://all4roofs.wpcomstaging.com/ REQUEST https://www.all4roofs.com/ ANSWER 301, to https://all4roofs.com/, then on to the staging address Checked 6 November 2026.
⚠ Why this is first

A staging address is where a website is built before it goes live. Yours never moved over. A permanent redirect tells Google and every browser that the staging address is the real home of your site, so the name you say on the radio hands its visitors to an address with WordPress’s name in it.

This is a settings fix in WordPress.com, not a rebuild.

2. Google is asked not to read the site
https://all4roofs.wpcomstaging.com/robots.txt User-agent: * Disallow: /
⚠ What that means

Every website has a small file that tells search engines which pages they may read. Yours tells all of them to read nothing. So when a Friday listener searches “All4Roof” or “techos Denver” instead of typing the address, Google has been asked not to look at your pages. This usually comes from a WordPress setting left on while a site is being built. It is a switch, not a project.

3. A listener on a phone cannot tap to call

Your number, (720) 984-0830, is printed on every page. But on none of the 12 pages is it a tappable link, so someone on a phone has to remember it or copy it. Anyone who looks you up on a phone after the show meets this first.

And the Request inspection button, plus every Contact us button, opens a page with no form, only the footer. The home page does have an enquiry form; we did not submit it, so we cannot say where it goes.

4. Nothing counts the radio

No Google Analytics 4, no Tag Manager, no Google Ads tag, no Meta pixel and no call tracking: only Jetpack’s basic visit counter. So nobody can say how many people came to the site after a Friday slot, or how many calls came from radio compared with word of mouth.

There is also no page for radio listeners: all4roofs.com/radio ends on a page that does not exist.

5. Nothing in Spanish

As you told us, most of your customers are Spanish-speaking homeowners, and you close those deals yourself. On the site, not one page has Spanish on it, there is no Spanish version, and the site tells browsers it is English only. A Spanish speaker who looks you up after the show finds nothing in their language and nothing that says you speak it.

⭐ None of the three Denver roofers we checked has a Spanish page either (see competitors). That makes Spanish an open lane, not a crowded one.

6. The small things Google reads
  • Title: “all-4-roofing · The Best Roofing In Colorado”. No town, no job.
  • Description: “The Best Roofing In Colorado”, 27 characters, the line Google shows under your name.
  • Two main headings on every page, and four on the process page.
  • Two page titles are swapped: commercial roofing and commercial PCA.
  • No local business markup telling Google you are a roofer at 4101 S Navajo St, and no canonical tag.
  • No towns named anywhere, and no page for hail and insurance work, which you do.
  • Two WordPress sample posts from May 2024 are still live, and the owner page sits at /about-page-4.
