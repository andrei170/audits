First: count the radio
The radio page and the radio number

Radio is working. The first job is to see it working, so you know what each Friday is worth.

  1. A radio phone number. A separate local number that rings your phone as normal. You read it out on air instead of your main number. Every call to it is a radio call, and it is counted.
  2. A radio page: all4roofs.com/radio. A short page in Spanish and English: “You heard Grisel on Friday”, the radio number to tap, and an inspection form. Say “all4roofs punto com slash radio” on air.
  3. Then you can see it: how many calls and forms came in after each slot, and which turned into jobs. That tells you whether 2 minutes or 6 minutes makes a difference, and what the radio is really worth to you.

See the radio page in the demo.

Step 1: fix the front door

Before anything else, all4roofs.com must show your site on your own address, and the setting that tells Google not to read it must be switched off. Until then, everything below is building on a closed door. This needs the WordPress.com login, and about an hour of our time with you.

Step 2: your Google listing
Google Business Profile, the listing on Google Maps

When someone searches “roofer near me” or “techos cerca de mí”, Google shows a map with three businesses under it. That listing is free. We could not check whether you have one, so on the call we will look together.

  • Make sure you own it and it shows your real address, phone, hours and the towns you work in.
  • Pick the right categories: roofing contractor first, plus the jobs you do, such as gutters and hail repair.
  • Say in the description that you serve customers in Spanish and English.
  • Add real photos of your jobs and your team every week or two.
  • Answer questions people ask on the listing, in the language they asked in.
Step 3: reviews
Reviews are what Google and homeowners trust most
  • After every finished job, send one text with a link to leave a Google review, in the customer’s language. We set up the text; you or your team press send.
  • Spanish reviews count the same as English ones, and they show Spanish speakers that people like them chose you.
  • Reply to every review, good or bad, in the language it was written in.
  • Never buy or invent reviews. Google removes them, and they put the listing at risk.
Step 4: ads, only once the counting works
  1. Google’s per-lead ads (Local Services Ads). The ads with the Google badge at the very top of the page. You pay for a phone lead, not a click, and Google checks your licence and insurance first. These would go to you only, not shared with three other roofers.
  2. Google search ads for searches like “roof replacement Denver” and “reparación de techos Denver”, sending people to the right page in the right language. We have not measured how many people search in Spanish; we would find out in the first weeks.
  3. Facebook and Instagram ads to homeowners in the towns you choose, in Spanish and English, with the same message as your radio slot, so people who never heard the show still hear from you.

Every ad gets its own tracking, so you can see which calls and jobs each one brought in, the same way as the radio.

Step 5: never miss a lead
Follow-up
  • If you miss a call, the caller gets a text back within a minute, in Spanish or English.
  • Every form gets an answer the same day.
  • Every lead sits in one place, so you can see who still needs a call back.
What this needs from you

You told us you spend about an hour a week on marketing. This plan is built to fit that: you send job photos, approve what we write, and ask happy customers for a review. We do the rest.

What we will not promise

Google decides who shows up and where. We will not promise a ranking, a number of calls or a number of jobs. What we will do is make every call countable, so you can judge it yourself.
