The real prize

A channel you can turn on to fill the diary

The opportunity here is a good one to build around, because most of the hard parts are already done. You have a live, secure site, a page for every service and town, and a strong review base. What you do not have is a channel you can turn on to bring work in when you want it. Today the work comes from being found, word of mouth and the time of year, and when those dip there is nothing to pull on, which is exactly the spell the setter says you are in. This page sets out how to fix that in order: put tracking in so every pound is countable, point a focused page at the high-value jobs, then run a measured local campaign you can turn up when the diary has room and down when it does not. None of it is a rebuild of your site, none of it steps on whoever handles your SEO, and none of it is a forecast or a promise of a number of jobs. It is the sequence that gives a capable, ready team a lever to pull.

The core point. Paid advertising only pays when you can measure it, and measurement is the one thing missing from a setup that is otherwise further along than most. So the order matters: get analytics and conversion tracking in first so any spend can be judged, point the adverts at a focused page built for one high-value job, then scale. Do it the other way round, spend first and sort the tracking later, and you are spending blind, which is the mistake this plan exists to avoid.

The order of play

1

Put measurement in

Add Google Analytics (GA4) and track enquiries as conversions, so every click and every form or call can be counted back to where it came from. You already have a tag container on the site, so this sits on top of it and is a short job. Nothing more is spent until it is done.

2

Build one focused page

A single landing page about one high-value job (a new roof, a full re-roof), built to turn an advert click into an enquiry and count it. It sits alongside your site and reuses your real guarantee and your 54 Google reviews. There is a worked example in this pack.

3

Run a measured campaign

A tracked local campaign for the work you want more of, pointed at that page. Because everything is counted, you see cost per enquiry and how many become booked roofs, and you turn the dial up when the diary has room and down when it is full, rather than on a hunch.

4

Feed it back in

Use the tracking to see which jobs and which areas (Leeds, Bradford, the wider West Yorkshire towns) pay best, and concentrate the spend there. Point new customers at your review page so the count keeps growing, which makes the next round of adverts convert better. A loop, not a one-off.

Why "a tap you can turn on" is the whole point for you

A firm with no team and no spare capacity does not need this; a full diary is the limit. You are the opposite: the setter says you have the team, the vans and the gear ready, and work has slowed. That is the exact situation a measured channel is built for. Put the tracking in and a campaign answers, from the first weeks:

  • Which adverts and searches bring roofing enquiries. So you spend on what works and cut what does not.
  • The cost of each enquiry, and each booked job. The number that tells you whether the channel pays, in pounds.
  • Which areas respond. Leeds, Bradford or the wider towns, so the spend goes where the work is.
  • How much to open the tap. Turn it up on a quiet week, down when the diary fills. You control the flow.

What a measured roofing campaign needs

The pieces, in plain terms:

  • Analytics and a conversion tag, so every click and enquiry is counted back to the pound. Without them, any spend is guesswork.
  • A dedicated landing page that talks only about the big job (a new roof, a full re-roof), so the advert click lands somewhere built to convert it, not on a general homepage. There is a worked example in this pack.
  • A fast, caught reply, because even a planned roof rewards the firm that replies first and confidently, and on paid traffic a slow reply is a lost job.
  • The numbers to judge it by: enquiries per week, cost per enquiry, and how many become booked roofs. So you can tell at a glance whether it is paying and turn the dial up or down.

One thing to settle before any of this scales: how work actually comes in today, and whether anything is being spent on marketing now. We found no ad tags on the site, which points to no paid channel, but we cannot see offline marketing or a paused account from the outside. [How work comes in today, your real coverage, your crew size and whether anything is being spent now are for the call; this plan does not assume them.]

The order, in one line. Put measurement in, build one focused page, run a measured campaign you can turn up and down, then feed the numbers back in. You are starting from a live site, a strong name and a ready team, which is exactly what makes a measured channel the right lever for a quiet spell.
