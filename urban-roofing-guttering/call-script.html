Urban Roofing and Guttering (Booking 0109)

Friday, 27 November 2026, 2:00 PM UK time (GMT). Prospect: Jackie Pollard. Setter: Ben Wilson. Grade: C, 61/100.

PRIVATE. Not sent to the prospect. Ticks and notes save to this browser only.
The one thing to hold in your head: this is NOT a "your website is bad" call and NOT a rebuild call. The site is good and there is an SEO incumbent (Digital Five). The C is pulled down by one thing: there is no reliable channel Jackie can turn on to bring work in. Ben says they have a team, vans and gear and work has slowed. The pitch: "you have the team and the reviews, let's give you a tap you can turn on to fill the diary", then the tracking and a focused page that make it measurable.

0. Before you dial

1. Open with respect and the credit

"Hi, is that Jackie? Thanks for making the time. I looked at your site properly before the call, and I want to be straight with you: it is a real, working site, it is secure, you have a page for every service and town you cover, and your reviews are genuinely strong, your site shows 4.7 on Google from 54 of them. So this is not a 'let me rebuild your website' call, and I am not here to tread on whoever does your SEO. What I want to talk about is the one thing that would stop a quiet spell hitting you the way it has."

2. Frame (you have the team, you have no tap to turn on)

"You have got the team, the vans, the gear, and from what Ben said, a bit of room in the diary right now. What you have not got is a way to turn work on when you want it. Everything comes in from people finding you, word of mouth, repeat, the time of year. Great when it is flowing. But when it dips, and it sounds like it has, there is no lever to pull. A measured advert is that lever: you open it on a quiet week and it brings enquiries in, and you turn it down when you are full. That is the whole thing missing, and it is the opposite of a problem with your site or your name."

3. Discovery (ask, do not tell)

"Ben mentioned a team and your own kit. How many of you are there on the roofs?"
"What areas do you actually cover? The site names Leeds, Bradford and a load of the West Yorkshire towns."
"Ben said things have slowed down a bit lately. Is that a seasonal thing you see every year, or does this feel quieter than usual?"
"When you ARE busy, where does most of the work come from, Google, word of mouth, repeat?"
"Are you spending anything on getting work in at the moment, Google ads, Facebook, a lead site?"
"Who looks after your website and Google side? It is well put together." (Finds Digital Five; respect them.)
"When a new enquiry comes in off the website, how quickly does someone normally get back to them?"
"If I could turn a tap on that brought new-roof and re-roof enquiries in the weeks you have room, would you have the team to take them?"

4. Pain questions (let them feel the gap)

"If next month goes quiet, what do you actually do about it? Is there anything you can switch on, or is it waiting for the phone to ring?"
"You have the team and gear ready. A quiet few weeks with the crew stood around is expensive in a way an empty van is not, the costs do not stop. How much does a slow spell cost you before it picks back up?"
"You have 54 reviews at 4.7, better than most roofers I speak to. But that only works on people who get sent to see it. How many homeowners planning a new roof in Leeds or Bradford this month never even find you?"
"If you did spend on getting work in, could you tell today what a booked roof cost you to win? Most cannot, so it feels like money into a hole. Doing it properly, you would see it, and only scale what pays."

5. The plan (plain, in order)

6. Tie-downs

"Does it make sense that the real gap is not your site or your name, it is that you have no way to turn work on when it goes quiet?"
"If you could open a tap on a slow week and see what each booked roof cost you, rather than waiting for the phone, that is the control you would want, right?"
"You have done the hard parts, the site, the reviews, the team. Adding a measured channel on top is a smaller job than it sounds, isn't it?"

7. Objections (short lines)

"My site is fine / I have someone for it." It is fine, and I am not here to rebuild it or step on your SEO. This sits on top, bringing work in, measured.
"It is just a seasonal dip." Maybe, and worth knowing. But a measured advert means you do not have to wait and hope, you bring work in on the quiet weeks.
"I get enough work normally." Normally, yes. This is about the quiet spells and the team sitting idle when they hit. If you would rather keep it as is, fair call.
"Never done ads / tried it and it didn't work." Most give up because they cannot tell what worked. That is why we start with the measurement, not the spend. You only scale what pays.
"Can't my web person do this?" Maybe, and if so, great. We add running it as a measured campaign, judged on booked roofs. Happy to work alongside them.
"How much?" Do NOT quote from memory. Ads-led: ad spend plus our side. Confirm the current figures from the live offer. Never in writing.

8. Close

9. Verify on the call

10. Offer and numbers (PRIVATE, never in writing)

Ads-led: monthly ad spend (to Google/Meta) plus AG1's side for building and running the measured campaign. Confirm the current management figure and the current minimum ad spend from the live offer sheet before the call; do not quote from memory.
What they get: tracking + conversion setup; a focused landing page; the campaign build and day-to-day management; fast lead handling; reporting on cost per enquiry and cost per booked job.
Results talk: ranges, and in terms of what is measured, never a guarantee. "We cannot promise a number of roofs. We can make every pound countable, and only scale what pays."
Fallback if they balk at spend: start small and measured, prove cost per booked roof, then turn it up. Never spending blind.

Hard rules

Open on strengths, not the grade. Bring-work-in pitch, not a rebuild or SEO. Respect the incumbent. Do not claim they advertise, do not assume the cause of the slowdown, the crew size, the area or the appetite. No verified rating (4.7/54 is what the site shows). No guarantees of enquiries, roofs, reviews or ranking. No pricing in writing, ever.
