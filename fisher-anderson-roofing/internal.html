Positioning in one line

“You don’t need more work, you need better work: bigger roofs, closer to home, and a full diary in January.” Lead with job selection and winter, then the site and Google profile, then the follow-up system, then local ads only when he has space.

Ask in this order
1. Is Scott on the call or does he need to agree? Two equal directors since 2019. Likely a two-person decision.
2. Phone number: the setter did not give one. Get the business mobile (for the demo and the Google profile).
3. The numbers do not add up: 6-10 enquiries a week at 50% = 12-20 jobs a month, but he says 4-6 jobs a month. Ask gently: are most enquiries small repairs he turns down or never quotes? That is the real wedge (job selection).
4. Average job value of a full re-roof vs a repair. Bigger roofs = fewer, better jobs.
5. Capacity: filed headcount is 2 (the two directors). Setter heard "team of 4, 2 vans", so 2 are likely subbies or labourers. Doubling jobs needs people. Who would he hire?
6. Winter: how many quiet weeks, and what does he do in them?
7. Yell site 10 years ago: what did it cost, why did it fail? (Template site, no ads, no follow-up. Let him tell it.)
8. Retiring in ~10 years: does he want a business that keeps finding work without him? (Handle softly. Do not pitch "sell the business". Ask what the plan is for the firm.)
9. Google Business Profile: has one? Who controls it? Any Google reviews? Facebook reviews?
10. Which towns: he wants more local. 50-60 miles today. Where does he NOT want to drive?
11. Insurance work, factors, commercial: any? (Bigger roofs, steadier winter work.)
Objections
“I’m booked till February, I don’t need more work.”Agree. Then: it is not more work, it is better work. Booked till Feb with 4-6 jobs a month while getting 6-10 enquiries a week means he is already turning work away; the system lets him choose the bigger, closer roofs and fill the winter gap. And he said he wants to double jobs next year.
“I tried a website with Yell and it did nothing.”Most likely true. A Yell template on its own is a brochure: no town pages, no Google profile, no reviews, no ads, nobody answering when he is on a roof. Show the sample site and the text-back. The site is one part of five.
“Word of mouth is enough.”It is what got him here. But even a referral searches his name and finds nothing, and word of mouth does not fill January.
“I need to talk to Scott.”Expected. Offer a short second call with both, and leave the hub link (assets.html) for Scott to read first.
“No time.”What we need: 30 minutes, his best photos off Facebook, his number, and access to the Facebook page. Everything else is done for him.
