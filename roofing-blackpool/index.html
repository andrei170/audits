The work and the reputation look strong. The plumbing behind the marketing is leaking. 13 things checked across your two websites and your ads. Every low grade is mechanical and fixable, and none of it is about your workmanship.

You told our team the Google and Meta ads did not work. Having looked at all of them, we think the honest answer is that nobody could have known. The ads themselves are better than most roofers’ ads. What sat around them was leaking: short-lived ads, forms that tell Facebook nothing, Google ads pointing at pages that do not load, and a second website nobody is counting.

None of this is about your roofing. All of it can be fixed before another pound goes on ads.

What is already good
  • You put yourself in it. “Meet the owner, Samuel Fisher” on sgfroofing.co.uk, and a talking-to-camera video ad. Homeowners buy a roof from a person. Most roofers hide.
  • Your ad copy is strong. “Your roof probably does not need replacing” and “Still waiting on a cowboy roofer to call you back?” are the right angles for this trade.
  • sgfroofing.co.uk has real conversion tracking. Calls, forms and WhatsApp clicks are wired to Google Ads. Whoever did that knew what they were doing.
  • Before and after photos and fixed prices in writing are on the site. Both answer the two biggest fears people have about roofers.
What is leaking
1. Two of your three Google ads point at “page not found”

The Google Ads Transparency Centre shows three ads for sgfroofing.co.uk, all from “Samuel Fisher, verified”. Two of them show the paths /new-roofs/reroof and /fascias-soffits/guttering. We opened both today and both return “page not found”.

If those ads are still live with those links, every click on them is paid for and lands on an error. Worth checking in the account in the first five minutes.

2. Meta never learns which leads became jobs

Most of your Meta ads used Facebook’s own instant form (“Get quote” on fb.me). Neither website has a Meta pixel. So Facebook only knows someone tapped a form. It does not know who answered the phone, who booked an inspection, or who paid. It then goes looking for more people who like filling in forms, which is not the same as people who want a roof.

3. Too many ads, run for too short a time

About 20 Meta ads since 17 July. Several ran for under a day, and most for under a week. An ad needs a couple of weeks and a steady budget before you can say whether it works. Switching that fast means every ad was judged on almost no data.

4. Two websites pulling in different directions

sgfroofing.co.uk is your main site. roofingblackpool.co.uk is a second site for the Fylde coast. It says “SGF Roofing & Plastics” in the text, spells it “SGF Rooring” twice, mentions “Acorn Roofing” (a different Blackpool firm), links to sgfroofingnw.co.uk which does not load, has no tap-to-call link, and only counts visits, not enquiries. Its testimonials are signed John Smith, Sarah Johnson and Michael Williams with no town or date.

A careful homeowner notices all of that. And because it is not tracked, nobody can tell you whether it has ever brought in a job.

5. The patch is wider than you want it

Your site says 25 miles from Chorley, then lists Manchester, Liverpool, Warrington and Rochdale. Your Meta ads say “across Lancashire”. If you want work closer to home, the ads and the pages both need to say so, or you pay for enquiries you do not want to drive to.

6. Your main site is heavy on a phone

The sgfroofing.co.uk homepage is about 3 MB, because 59 images are pasted straight into the page code rather than loaded as image files. On a weak signal that is a slow first screen, and paid visitors leave slow pages.

7. Your numbers do not match each other

Your site says 15+ years roofing and 1,620 roofs. Your ads say 20+ years and “thousands” of roofs. Pick one set and use it everywhere. Small mismatches are what make a sceptical homeowner hesitate.

Search and AI answers
  • sgfroofing.co.uk is set to be indexed and carries proper RoofingContractor markup, but it is essentially one long page plus a flat roofs page. There are no separate pages for repairs, re-roofs, roofline or for the towns you want, which is what both Google and AI assistants (ChatGPT, Google’s AI Overviews) use to match “roofer in Poulton” to you.
  • roofingblackpool.co.uk has a useful set of 11 question-style blog posts (“How much does a new roof cost in Blackpool”), which is exactly the format AI answers pull from. But the brand confusion on the page undercuts it: an assistant cannot tell whether the answer belongs to Roofing Blackpool, SGF or Acorn.
