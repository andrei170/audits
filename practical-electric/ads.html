Why ads might not show up here
  • Local Services Ads (the pay-per-lead ads at the very top) are run from a separate Google product and may not appear in this record the same way.
  • A campaign set up through GoDaddy or someone else may run under a different advertiser name.
  • Paused, out of budget, or ending early in the day.
  • Ads pointing somewhere else, such as a call-only ad or a different page.

⭐ Any of these is fixable. What matters is knowing which, and what each dollar is bringing back.

What is weak, whatever the account shows
The landing side cannot turn a click into a job you can measure
  • No conversion tag on the site. Google cannot tell which clicks became calls or forms, so it cannot learn who to show your ads to, and you cannot see cost per job.
  • No page per service. An ad for EV charger installation lands on a page that covers six services in six paragraphs.
  • The form does not ask the job type, urgency or zip. Every paid click that fills it in still needs a call to find out if it is worth the drive.
  • No call tracking number. Calls from ads and calls from Angi look the same.
  • The 24/7 promise invites night-time emergency calls you cannot take while working a full day job.
How we would run Google Ads for a few good jobs a week
Small, tight and measured, not big
  1. Only the jobs you want. Separate ad groups for the high-value work you name today (for example EV charger installs, troubleshooting, commercial), each with its own landing page.
  2. A tight area. Only the towns you want to drive to, with location set to people in the area, not people “interested in” it.
  3. Negative keywords from day one: jobs, careers, apprentice, DIY, how to, cheap, free.
  4. Ads only when someone can answer, or a missed-call text back that replies in seconds while you are on a job.
  5. Every call and form tracked, and every booked job marked in the CRM, so the account learns which searches turn into paying jobs.
  6. A budget sized to your capacity. If you only want a handful of good jobs, the account is built for a handful. We review the search terms weekly and cut anything that is not your work.
What we would ask to see on the call
  • The campaigns tab: type of campaign, budget, location settings.
  • The search terms report for the last 30 days: what people actually typed.
  • The conversions page: what, if anything, counts as a result.
  • Who has access to the account.
