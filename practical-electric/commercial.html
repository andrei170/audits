The honest answer first

Your site says you do commercial and industrial work: wiring, lighting, data cabling and emergency generators. One ad does not win a commercial contract. Ads and a good site get you on a shortlist; follow-up over weeks or months wins the work. If commercial is part of your independent plan, this is how it works.

Who buys this work around Seattle
  • Property managers and HOA managers (townhome and condo communities, small apartment buildings).
  • Facilities managers for offices, shops, restaurants and clinics.
  • General contractors who need a reliable electrical sub.
  • Small business owners fitting out or moving premises.
What they check before they call
  • Licence (yours is active: #PRACTEL755JJ).
  • Insurance certificate and bond.
  • Photos of similar jobs.
  • References or reviews from similar customers.
  • How fast you reply and whether you put a quote in writing.

Today your site shows none of these. The sample site has a commercial page with a site-visit request form.

Where ads fit, and where follow-up does
  • Commercial-only searches (“commercial electrician”, “tenant improvement electrical”) with homeowner searches excluded.
  • Retargeting so a property manager who visited sees you again for months.
  • A pipeline in the CRM: every quote logged with its value, a follow-up schedule you approve, and a reminder before the budget season.
  • After each job: a photo report and a request for a reference.

⚠ Whether commercial is worth chasing depends on your capacity and the work you want. That is a question for today, not an assumption.
