Positioning in one line

“Fewer, better jobs you own, and a setup that answers the phone for you.” Not volume. He wants to look like an established independent contractor, pick his jobs, and stop paying Angi for shared leads. Lead with time saved (text-back, booking, job-type filter), then ownership (Google profile, reviews, site), then tight ads.

Ask in this order
1. When exactly does he want to leave the townhome job? What has to be true (income per month, pipeline) first?
2. Which side jobs does he enjoy and earn most on? Average ticket for those vs the rest.
3. Angi: monthly spend, cost per lead, win rate, and whether leads are shared with other pros.
4. Google Ads: who set it up, monthly spend, campaign type (LSA?), what counts as a conversion, any jobs traced to it.
5. Who answers when he is on site 12 to 16 hours? How many calls go to voicemail a week?
6. Is the 24/7 emergency claim real? If not, take it off before it costs him reviews.
7. Is 10520 Stone Ave N his home? (It is on the register and Google's AI Overview repeats it.)
8. Insurance carrier and limits he is happy to show; bond on file with L&I.
9. Commercial: has he done any as Practical Electric, and does he want more? (Site claims it.)
10. Photos: does he have job photos on his phone we can use?
11. Decision: is it just him, or does anyone else weigh in (partner)?
12. Budget comfort for the setup and for ads, given he wants 4 to 5 jobs a month.
Objections
“I don’t want a lot of jobs.”Agree, and say it back: “Good, neither do we. You want 4 or 5 good ones a month that you choose, not 20 you have to sort through.” Then the point: Angi and a 3-page site send you whoever turns up; a site that asks job type, zip and photos, plus ads only on the work you want, sends you the jobs worth the drive. And the text-back and booking mean fewer calls to return at 9pm, not more. Less volume is the reason to do this, not a reason not to.
“I already have a website guy / GoDaddy does my SEO.”Do not knock GoDaddy. Ask: “What has the SEO brought in? Can you see any job that came from Google search?” Then show facts, not opinion: 3 pages, last changed May 2025, no service pages, no meta descriptions, no tag, not in any of 5 local searches. If he wants to keep GoDaddy as the host, the sample site is a checklist. Our value is the system around it: profile, reviews, text-back, booking, tracked ads.
“Angi works for me.”It does, and we keep it on. Ask what each lead costs, how many he wins, and how many reviews he has from those jobs (we saw one). The plan is to collect reviews from Angi customers on Google and turn Angi down only when the numbers say so.
“I already run Google Ads.”We could not find any ads pointing to his site, and no advertiser under his name (control check worked). Ask him to open the account on screen: campaign type, budget, locations, search terms, conversions. Probably LSA, GoDaddy-run, paused, or untracked. Either way there is no conversion tag on the site, so nothing is being measured.
“I’m stacked this month, let’s talk in November.”That is exactly why now: profile, reviews, site and tracking take a few weeks, so starting now means the November jobs are coming from his own channels, not Angi. Nothing goes live that sends work before he is ready.
“I have no time.”He is the ideal case for done-for-you: the setup exists to take the phone off him. What we need from him is 30 minutes: job types, towns, photos, licence/insurance details.
Value points to land
  • Licensed in May 2025, still a 3-page site and one review: the business is real, the presence is not.
  • Bright Line got licensed 9 months after him and already has 60 reviews and a paid map ad.
  • Every Angi customer is a Google review he is not collecting.
  • The text-back and booking form are what make one job a week possible without answering the phone on a ladder.
  • Fallback if he will not commit: start with the Google profile, review requests and text-back, then the site and ads.
