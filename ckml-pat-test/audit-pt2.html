What you told me, gone through properly

On Tuesday we went through the website. This page goes back through what you said about the business, one point at a time, and what each one means.

1 · You said

“We really could increase it significantly if need be.”

What we found: You do 10 to 12 jobs a month and told me you could do 30 to 40. The engineer, the equipment and the reputation are already paid for. What is missing is new customers finding you.

What fixes it: A steady way for new commercial customers to find and contact you, so the spare capacity gets used. That is the whole point of everything below.

2 · You said

“90% of our revenue is old, if not more. Maybe 95%.”

What we found: You are excellent at keeping customers: once you have the work, you rarely lose it. But almost nothing new is coming in, which is why sales have been flat for three years. It also means a lot rides on a few big accounts. You said yourself the Virgin Media contract is “not as big as it used to be”.

What fixes it: Add a few new accounts every month, so growth no longer depends on a big contract staying the same size.

3 · You said

“We respond to them as they come in. If we get the job, great. If not, then that's it.”

What we found: Every quote you don’t win is never followed up. And PAT testing comes round again, often every year, but nothing reminds a customer, or you, when it is due.

What fixes it: A simple CRM, which we will go through on Wednesday: every enquiry and quote logged, a follow-up on every quote you haven’t heard back on, and each customer’s next test date stored, with a reminder sent before it is due. Your repeat work stops depending on memory.

4 · You said

“He's put the form on there. A test came through and it worked.”

What we found: You were right. On 15 October both forms still sent to a placeholder, but on 20 October both are wired to a form service with real keys. Still live: the tab reads “Full Site Preview”, the phone number given to Google shows as XXXX, the price slider says “Example pricing shown for illustration”, and nothing measures visitors or enquiries.

What fixes it: One test enquiry together on the call, then the four leftovers above. They are small jobs, and measuring comes before any advertising.

5 · You said

“Commercial is our main business.”

What we found: Facilities managers, school business managers and office managers search for exactly what they need, for example “PAT testing for schools Sunderland”. Your site is one long page, so there is nothing that matches those searches.

What fixes it: A page for each kind of customer and each town: offices, schools, landlords, care homes, multi-site contracts, Sunderland, Newcastle, Durham and so on. The demo site shows one for each: five kinds of customer and 11 towns.

6 · You said

“We're cheaper than DRA, and dearer than ATCO at 60p. We're in the middle.”

What we found: In the middle on price, but ahead on what is included: free socket testing and microwave leakage checks, which none of the five competitors mention. Your site doesn’t make that case yet, and its price is marked as an example.

What fixes it: Sell what is included, not the price: everything in one visit, by a local engineer. Then show either a real “from” price or a quote form, but not an example.

7 · You said

“We were thinking of looking into fire safety and emergency lighting.”

What we found: HT PAT Testing already sells PAT testing, fire safety and emergency lighting in one visit. For a facilities manager, one visit and one supplier is less admin.

What fixes it: When you offer it, give it a page and a bundle: PAT, fire extinguishers and emergency lighting on the same visit. Until you do, we don’t advertise it.

8 · You said

“To grow it, in the off chance it could be a saleable asset in the future.”

What we found: Somebody buying a testing business pays for contracts that renew, a customer list with test dates on it, and a business that doesn’t depend on the owners. Today the customer records live in people’s heads and inboxes.

What fixes it: The CRM is what makes it saleable: every customer, every site, every test date and every contract in one place, plus new accounts coming in each month. That is a business somebody else can run.

9 · You said

“We've never done any advertising. It's just grown organically.”

What we found: We found no reviews for CKML on any public listing, and an AI search tool could not find CKML at all (see Search, AI & ads). DRA shows 147 reviews on FreeIndex and says over 480 on its own site.

What fixes it: Ask your repeat customers for a review. They already like you, and that is the quickest proof there is. Then advertise, once the form, the pages and the tracking are in place.

⚠ Questions that change what we’d build
  • Which customers do you want more of first? Offices, schools, landlords, care homes or multi-site contracts
  • How far will you travel for a new customer? All the North East, or a tighter area first
  • Who answers new enquiries, you or Clair? That decides who the CRM is built around
  • How often do most of your customers retest? Every 12 months, or does it vary?
  • Which accreditations and insurance can we show? CHAS, SafeContractor, DBS-checked engineers, public liability
  • Who is your website person, and are they still working on the site?
