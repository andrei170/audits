1 · The open (2 minutes)
  • Welcome Clair by name. “Neil filled you in a bit. Before I show anything, what did you make of the screenshot, and what would you want this to fix?”
  • Confirm the time: “I have you until about ten. Do you need to leave for the college job?”
  • Agenda in one line: a quick recap, what changed on the demo, the follow-up system, then the two options.
2 · Walk-through order
  1. Recap for Clair (3 minutes): recap-21oct.html. Goal of 20%, not found on Google or AI search, no reviews in 14 years, no follow-up when someone does not rebook.
  2. Demo, what changed (5 minutes): areas (the five towns Neil asked for), About (DBS, insurance, photo and logo slots), then one service page Clair cares about.
  3. Follow-up system (5 minutes), framed for Clair: she answers enquiries and does the admin. Show the enquiry list, the re-test reminder, the review request after a job. Say plainly that PatGuard stays as the test record.
  4. The price (5 minutes): share options.html live. It is not linked anywhere in the pack, so they will not have seen it.
  5. Close and next steps (5 minutes).
3 · How to present the two options
  • £1,500, the website, fully built. Answers Neil’s stated priority: being found. Pages for each kind of customer and each area, About, FAQ, quote form, set up for Google and AI search.
  • £2,000, the website plus the CRM. The extra £500 is the part that answers his own words: “If somebody doesn’t rebook, we don’t really follow up on it, which we should do.” Re-test reminders, quote follow-up, a review request after every job, one shared calendar. It is also what makes the business easier to sell, which was his longer-term goal.
  • Both: paid once for the build, half to start and half on delivery, everything in their name. The optional monthly plan is “something we can talk through”. No monthly price is set, so do not quote one on the call.
  • Lay both out, then stop and ask: “Which of these fits where you want CKML to be next year?” Let them choose. No first-person recommendation.
4 · Likely objections, and answers
They sayAnswer
“December is quiet, let’s look at January.”Neil already said it could go in at any time of the year. A quiet December is when there is time to learn it, so it is ready when testing picks up in January. Only give a build time you can stand behind.
“We already have PatGuard for test dates.”Agree: PatGuard keeps the test records. It does not contact the customer when nobody rebooks, chase a quote, or ask for a review. The CRM does that part; PatGuard stays.
“We don’t get many enquiries, so we don’t need a CRM yet.”Most of the value is with existing customers: 90 to 95% of the work is repeat, nobody chases a missed rebook, and there are no reviews after 14 years. Review requests are what Google and AI tools read.
“Our lad is already working on the website.”Respect him: he moved the site into your control. He does it on the side alongside a store manager job, and he is busy with your files and HR system. The new site is built in your name, and he can still look after hosting if you want.
“Where are the ads?”Ads come after the site and the follow-up, in the order agreed on 21 October. Ad spend is never in a build price. Google search first, in your own account.
“Can you do it for less?”The £1,500 website-only option is the lower price. Hold the prices on the page.
“We need to think about it.”“Of course. What is the one thing you would want to be sure about?” Then book a short follow-up with both of them before ending.
“What results will we get?”Do not give a number. The pack forecasts nothing. Say what will be measured (calls, forms, where each came from) so they can see it.
5 · Questions for Clair
  1. How do enquiries reach you today: phone, email or the form? Which do you check first?

  3. Do you use PatGuard every day, and who else needs to see bookings?
  4. Which clients would you be comfortable asking for a logo, or a review?
  5. What would make this a clear yes for you, and what is your biggest worry about it?

  • “Which option would you like to go with?” Then confirm half to start and half on delivery.
  • Agree who sends what: photos, logos with permission, the insurance certificate, the engineer’s qualification, office hours.
  • Set the date for the first look at the finished pages before anything goes live.
  • If not today: book the follow-up with both of them on the call.
