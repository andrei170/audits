B. Enquiries first: what we control, and what you control

Our job is getting the phone and the form to ring. That is the number you will see counted every month, with where each enquiry came from. Turning enquiries into jobs is yours: answering, surveying and pricing. You already win most of what comes to you by word of mouth.

C. What a job might cost to win (rough estimate)
⚠ This is an estimate, not a forecast

It uses a public UK click price range and our own assumptions for the rest. Real numbers can be better or worse. It is a rough guide to check against real numbers in month one. The ad budgets are what goes to Google, not our fee.

What a job is worth (public UK figures)
  • Roof repair: about £875 on average, usually £483 to £1,606 (Checkatrade, read 4 November 2026).
  • Full roof replacement: £7,000 on average, most £4,000 to £19,000 (Checkatrade 2025 guide); £4,000 to £13,000 for a standard semi or detached (MyBuilder 2026).
  • Your own private jobs on Tuesday: £5k to £50k.
The assumptions
  • Cost per click: inside the UK range of £3 to £20 for roofing keywords (Whito, May 2026). We assume repair clicks are cheaper than replacement clicks.
  • Visit to enquiry: 5% to 12% of people who land on the page call or fill in the form.
  • Enquiry to booked survey: 40% to 60%. Survey to won job: 25% to 40%.
  • These last three are our assumptions, not published figures. Month one replaces them with yours.

When does it pay?

It pays when what you keep from a job is more than it cost to win. Your margin decides that, so we need it from you. As an example only (not your figure): if you keep 30p in every £1, a job has to be worth more than about £2,604 to cover a middle-case replacement and about £1,562 for a middle-case repair.

That is why we would lead with replacements and bigger repairs, and use the enquiry form to sort out the small jobs before they take your time.

Google Search Ads (pay per click)

Shows you to people already typing “roof repair Liverpool” or “new roof St Helens”. Best for urgent repairs and for replacements, because the person is ready to book. You pay for each click, and you choose the towns, the jobs and the daily budget.

Google Local Services Ads (pay per lead)

The boxes at the very top of Google with Google’s verified badge on them. You pay per lead, not per click. Google checks your business and insurance first, and your reviews count a lot. That badge answers the trust problem directly.

⚠ Google’s UK help page lists roofers as a category, but says to check your own area. We have not checked L35 yet, and with few Google reviews it may not show you much at first.

Meta (Facebook and Instagram)

Puts you in front of people who were not searching yet. Better for planned re-roofs, using before and after photos and an offer like a free roof survey. Worse for emergency repairs. Enquiries are usually cheaper but less ready to book, so they need a fast reply and the enquiry form to sort them.

Our suggested order
  1. Tracking first, before any spend. You have been told you were “at the top” while the phone did not ring. This time every call and form is counted.
  2. Google Search on replacements and bigger repairs, core towns only. It catches people ready to book, and only two of the firms we checked are set up to measure Google Ads.
  3. Apply for Local Services Ads alongside it, if it is open at your postcode, and ask every private customer for a Google review so the badge has something behind it.
  4. Month 2: test Meta for replacements with your before and after photos, and add the mid ring towns.
E. What we need from you to firm these numbers up
  • The smallest job worth the drive, so the form and the ads skip anything below it.
  • Your margin on repairs and on replacements, so we know the job value where it pays.
  • How many extra jobs a month the crew can take, so we do not buy enquiries you cannot do.
  • Access to your Google Business Profile, for reviews and Local Services Ads.
