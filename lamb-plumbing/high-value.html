The brief from the booking is the right one to build around: catch more of the enquiries coming in, and take the admin off your plate. This page is about the journey an enquiry takes from the first ring to a paid, reviewed job, where the jobs and the evenings leak out of it today, and what a single system does to plug both. None of what follows is a forecast or a promise of a number; it is how the workflow fits together and where the current gaps sit.

The core point. A plumbing business loses money in two quiet places: the enquiries it never catches (a missed call mid-job, a message at night, a searcher who finds a holding page), and the hours it burns on admin after the work is done (quoting, chasing, invoicing, remembering who to ring back). Both are invisible, because a job you never knew about and an evening you spend on paperwork do not show up as problems. A system that catches every enquiry the moment it lands and then runs the admin behind it fixes both at once, and it is exactly what Ben flagged. The page is the front door; the system is the thing that stops the money leaking out the back.

The journey an enquiry should take

1

It lands and is caught

A form on the page, a tap-to-call, or a missed call. Right now a form does not exist and a missed call is simply gone. Step one is catching all of it, every hour of the day.

2

It is answered in seconds

An automatic reply to the form and a text back to a missed call, so the customer knows you exist and waits for you instead of ringing the next plumber. This is the single biggest lever on how much work you win.

3

It becomes a booking and a record

The enquiry turns into a slot in the diary and a customer record you can see, not a note on the back of your hand. Nothing is forgotten and nothing is double-booked.

4

It gets invoiced, chased and reviewed

After the job, the system sends the invoice, chases it if it is late, and asks the happy customer for a review, without you opening a laptop at night.

Where a system does the heavy lifting

A proper system (the kind AG1 sets up) is not a website. It sits behind whatever page you have and does the parts that eat your time:

  • Never miss an enquiry. Form submissions, calls and messages all land in one place, and a missed call triggers an instant text back, so the customer stays warm instead of ringing a competitor.
  • Reply instantly, even mid-job. An automatic first reply buys you time to get back to them when you are off the ladder, without the customer feeling ignored.
  • One place for every customer and job. Who they are, what the job is, the quote, the appointment and the invoice, all on one card, so the admin stops living in your head.
  • Invoicing and chasing that run themselves. The invoice goes out when the job is done and the reminder goes out if it is not paid, which is faster money and fewer awkward phone calls.
  • A review engine. A happy customer gets a one-tap review request automatically, which is how the next customer finds you and trusts you.
  • It is all measurable. How many enquiries came in, how many turned into jobs, how fast you replied, and where the work came from, so you can see what is working instead of guessing.

What to measure

  • Enquiries per week and how many you are currently missing, the number that shows the leak.
  • Speed to first reply, because on plumbing work the fastest reply usually wins the job.
  • Enquiry to booked job, so you can see how many enquiries actually become work.
  • Time to paid and the share of invoices chased automatically, the admin-and-cashflow number.
  • Reviews gained per month, the quiet engine behind the next month's enquiries.

All of these need an enquiry route and a system behind it. None of them can be measured today, which is why the page and the system are step one.

Why the website and the system go together

A brochure site alone looks nice and still lets enquiries slip through, because a page cannot answer a missed call or chase an invoice. A system alone has nothing feeding it if there is no page for a customer to reach. Built together, the page catches the enquiry and the system does everything after, which is why the recommendation is a page people can actually reach plus the system behind it, not one without the other.
