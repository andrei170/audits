So there is no ad, offer, landing page or tracking to critique. The useful question is whether a second channel is worth adding, and in what order.

Why a second channel matters when SEO is working
  • Rankings move. Every new enquiry today depends on positions you do not control. A second channel means a ranking drop is a slow month, not an empty one.
  • Damp searches are high intent. Someone searching “rising damp survey Hove” has a problem now. Paid search puts you at the top of that page for the towns in your radius only.
  • Your competitors already pay for the spot above you. On the Google searches we read: paid local listings from Nordic Damp Proofing and M. Ross Building Services (Shoreham), text ads from Surrey Hills Damp (Brighton, Hove, Lancing searches), and paid map listings from BWT Southern and MPC Plastering. Sussex Damp Experts has 2 Google ads on record. Your free listing sits below theirs.
How we would run it
Small, measured, and only after tracking is in
  • Tracking first: analytics, call tracking and a conversion on every form, call and WhatsApp tap.
  • Google search ads on damp survey, rising damp, condensation and replastering terms, limited to the towns within your 15 to 20 miles, sent to the matching page that already exists on your site.
  • A form that asks the job and postcode first, so a small skim and a full damp job are sorted before you call back.
  • Every paid enquiry gets the same instant reply and follow-up as the SEO ones, so nothing paid for is lost.
  • Facebook and Instagram later, mainly to show case studies and reviews to people who have already visited your site.
