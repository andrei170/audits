What you told us you need, and what we found
“When you need blokes the most, it’s a very busy time for everyone, so it’s harder to get guys”

What we found: Good engineers are already working, so they are not job hunting. They are scrolling Facebook in the evening, not reading agency emails.

What fixes it: Adverts that reach engineers who are not looking, in December and January when you said the timing is right, plus a list of good applicants kept for the next round.

“Agency… it’s a terrible waste of time”

What we found: A labour agency’s best people are kept by their clients, so what is left over is who gets sent out. A recruiter’s permanent fee is typically a share of the first year’s salary, per hire (see The campaigns).

What fixes it: Hire direct. You own the page, the adverts and the applicants, and there is no fee per person.

“It’s probably a better route to get them once they’ve done their apprenticeship”

What we found: You told us an apprentice cost you about £10,000 over six months and did not stay.

What fixes it: The application asks years since qualifying first, and anyone under your minimum is turned away politely before you spend a minute on them.

“Someone who’s quite confident that you can sort of put on a job without being babysat”

What we found: Confidence is the one thing an interview struggles to show and a trial shows quickly.

What fixes it: One application question about a job they ran on their own, a trial day with an experienced lad, then your three-month trial with check-ins.

“I lost a lot of contacts through bad workmanship”

What we found: Your contract clients and bathroom suppliers are where your work comes from, so one bad hire costs more than one job.

What fixes it: References required to apply, and new people start on your own jobs before going alone to a client.

“Until I can back that up, I can’t really do it”

What we found: You have a council contract in Luton with room to go into other areas, and suppliers recommending you for bathrooms. The work is there; the people are the limit.

What fixes it: Two engineers first, then a bathroom installer, so the bathroom referrals can become a line of their own.

“[Your website]”

What we found: We re-checked manplumbing.co.uk on 25 November 2026: it is still a blank parked page. An engineer who hears about you and looks you up finds nothing.

What fixes it: A real site with a careers page, so an applicant can see the firm, the work and the pay before they apply.

Questions that change what we build
What is the pay? Day rate or salary for an experienced engineer, and for a bathroom installer. It goes on every advert.
Employed or self-employed? It changes who applies.
Is Gas Safe a must for the engineer roles?
Two first, or four? And which role first.
Where should they live? South Essex only, or also people who could cover the Luton work?
Who does the first phone call, you or your sister?
