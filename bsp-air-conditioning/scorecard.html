Enquiry Intake Scorecard
F
36 / 100
2 of 12 checks passed. 8 failed.
This grades your website as an intake system, not as marketing. Customers can reach you easily and your reviews are strong. But the forms do not collect what you need to price a job, nothing tells people when you can fit them in, and the site invites enquiries from the whole UK. Each of those turns into a call or message you have to make.
GradeWhat we checkedWhat we found (2 November 2026)
BWays to get in touch
⭐ Tap-to-call on every page, an email address in the footer and a form on the home page, the contact page and every service page. No text or WhatsApp option and no chat, so anything outside a call or a form lands as a missed call or a voicemail.
FOne form, or three
Your site has three different enquiry forms, each asking different things. The home page asks name, phone and a message. The contact page asks name, email, phone, subject and a message. The service pages ask name, phone, an “Area Code” and the service. So what lands in your inbox depends on which page the customer was on.
FWhere the job is
Only the service-page form asks where the job is, and that box only accepts numbers. A UK postcode has letters in it, so a customer cannot type something like “EX1 2AB” there. The home page and contact page forms do not ask at all, so working out whether a job is in your area means a call back.
DWhat the job is
⭐ The service-page form has a dropdown (domestic, commercial, maintenance, refrigeration, solar, ventilation), which is good. The other two forms leave it to the free-text message.
FEnough to price it without a call
No form asks the property type, how many rooms or units, wall or ceiling, or where the outdoor unit could go, and none lets the customer send a photo. Those are the things that decide a quote, so almost every enquiry needs a call or a visit before you can say anything about price.
FGetting a quote back to them
The home page form does not ask for an email address, and neither does the service-page form. A written quote from those enquiries needs another call or text first just to get an address.
FSetting expectations on timing
Nothing on the site says how quickly you reply or how far ahead you are booking. The site promises “fast response”, so a customer who waits will chase, and every chase is another call or message for you to handle.
FWhere you work
The home page says Exeter, Devon and the South West. The FAQ says you cover “the entire UK, including Devon, Cornwall, London, and the Midlands”, and the about page says “nationwide coverage”. So the site invites enquiries from anywhere, and each one from outside your patch still has to be read and answered.
CAnswering repeat questions
⭐ An FAQ with eight useful answers: install time (1 to 2 days), listed buildings, servicing once a year, brands, the 7-year parts and labour warranty and free quotes. It shows twice on the same page, and it does not cover what happens after an enquiry, survey visits, rough costs or wait times, which are the questions that usually generate calls.
FBooking a survey
Your “How We Work” page describes a consultation and site inspection, but there is no way to pick a survey slot online. Every survey is arranged by phone or message.
FServicing customers coming back
The site talks about yearly servicing and maintenance contracts, but there is no way for a customer to sign up for a service reminder, and nothing shows a reminder system behind it.
BReviews
⭐ Ten Google reviews shown on the site and a link to your Google reviews. They mention you by name, tidy work and quick quotes. What we cannot see is how reviews are asked for: if it is by hand after each job, it is one more admin task.

⭐ None of this needs a new website. Every row is a change to a form, an automatic reply or what happens behind the form.

⚠ Not assessed, and left out of the score rather than guessed: what happens after a form is sent, your back-office software, Google Business Profile and social accounts.
