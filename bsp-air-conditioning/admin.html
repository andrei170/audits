Where the admin usually goes in an air conditioning install firm

Eleven admin jobs, what each could look like automated, and what stays with you. This is typical for a small installer that does its own surveys, quotes and installs. It is not a measurement of your week; week 1 measures that and decides where to start. What you told us is mapped on the start page.

Admin jobWhat it usually looks likeWhat we would automateWhat stays with you
1. Reading and sorting enquiriesEvery call, form, WhatsApp, email and TikTok, Facebook or Instagram message read by you. Some are out of area, some are loft or ducted jobs you do not want, some are the job you want.Everything lands in one inbox with an instant reply from your name. Postcode and system type are asked up front, enquiries are sorted by distance, and jobs outside your radius or your preferred work get a polite no.Which jobs you take.
2. Call backs to get the detailsRinging back to ask where, what, how many rooms, which property, and when.One short form that asks all of that up front, with photos, so most enquiries arrive ready to price.The conversation with customers who need one.
3. Missed callsCalls missed while you are up a ladder or driving, then returned in the evening.A text sent straight back from your number with a link to the form, so the details come in while you work.Calling back the ones that matter.
4. Arranging site surveysBack-and-forth messages to find a time that suits both of you.A booking link showing only the survey slots you choose, with a reminder the day before.Your diary and which slots you open.
5. Building quotesWriting each quote from scratch or from an old one.Quote templates for your common jobs, filled from the survey details, so you check and send rather than write.The price, every time.
6. Chasing quotesRemembering who has not replied and following up, or not following up at all.A short, polite follow-up sequence that stops the moment they reply or accept.Whether to chase at all, and when.
7. Booking installs and ordering kitConfirming dates, deposits and equipment, often by text.Accepted quotes move to a simple job board with the deposit request, install date and a reminder to order kit.Suppliers and the install plan.
8. Job sheets and F-gas recordsWriting up what was installed, refrigerant details and commissioning, and filing it.A phone form on site that saves the job record against the customer, so it is there for warranty and the next service.The engineering.
9. Invoices and payment chasingRaising invoices and chasing late payers.Invoices raised when a job is marked done, with polite reminders if unpaid. Connected to your accounts software if you use one.Any payment conversation that needs you.
10. Service and F-gas remindersRemembering who is due a yearly service or leak check.Automatic reminders on the anniversary of each install, with a booking link.Pricing and the service itself.
11. Asking for reviewsAsking each happy customer by hand, if there is time.A review request sent a few days after each finished job.Nothing: it runs on its own once set.
How to read this
⭐ Automation takes the repetition, you keep the decisions
  • Every automated message goes out in your name, in your words, and you can switch any of it off.
  • Nothing here changes how you price or how you work on site. It removes the typing, chasing and remembering around it.
  • Your data stays yours: customers, quotes and job records sit in accounts in your name.
  • No figures for hours saved until your week is measured.
