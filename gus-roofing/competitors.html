What this means for you
1The roofer licensed after you shows the number and has 52 reviews.

Skyvision Roofing’s license (#1161665) is newer than yours, its site is 9 pages, about the size of yours, and it puts "CA License #1161665" at the very top. It shows 4.8 from 52 on Google. Being new is not the problem; being invisible is.

Source: Skyvision’s home page; Google Maps; CSLB, November 18, 2026.

2Every one of the five shows up on Google Maps with reviews. You do not show up.

29 to 113 Google reviews each. You have none we could find, and a Colorado "Gus Roofing" takes your name in the results. A Google profile and a steady trickle of reviews from past customers is free and closes this gap fastest.

Source: Google Maps from North Long Beach, 8 searches, November 18, 2026.

3Two of them have built a page for each city they work in.

Golem has 36 city pages and Specialist 20. You do not need dozens; a handful for the cities you actually want (Long Beach, Lakewood, Signal Hill, Bellflower, Downey, Seal Beach) would do.

Source: Each firm’s sitemap, November 18, 2026.

4Only one asks the ZIP, and they all say what they do.

Specialist’s form asks the ZIP, and its menu lists repair, replacement, shingle, metal and tile roofs. Your site never names a job type. Saying "roof leaks, repairs and full re-roofs" plainly is what makes the right homeowner call you.

Source: Home page code and text of each site, November 18, 2026.

Where you are already ahead
  • An active C-39 license with bond and workers’ comp, which you can show in one line.
  • Real photos of full tear-offs and new decking, which show more care than several of these sites show.
  • Your own name on the business. Homeowners like dealing with the owner; the site just needs to say it is you.
