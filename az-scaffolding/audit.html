In short

Your rebuilt site is a good base: tap-to-call everywhere, eight town pages, six proper case studies, your reviews on show, and clean titles and headings. You are first in Google Maps for “scaffolding Pontefract”.

What holds it back is narrower: nothing counts enquiries, the quote form lives on one page only, the services share a single page, and the commercial credentials stop at CISRS and “fully insured”. Graded on 13 checks it scores D, 56 out of 100. See the scorecard.

What is strong
  • Tap-to-call on every page. Both numbers are tappable links on all 17 pages, in the header and footer.
  • Six written case studies. An apartment block with a lattice-beam bridge over a vehicle archway (Pontefract), edge protection on live generator units (Knottingley), and four more. Each says the challenge, what you did and the result.
  • Reviews in the right place. A Google reviews widget on the site, and 4.7 stars from 50 reviews on Google itself.
  • Clean page basics. Every page has its own title, its own search description and exactly one main heading.
  • Eight town pages for Pontefract, Knottingley, Castleford, Wakefield, Leeds, Doncaster, South Kirkby and Featherstone.
  • You lead Google Maps at home. First for “scaffolding Pontefract”, “scaffolders Pontefract” and “scaffolding Knottingley” when we searched on 10 November.
What to fix, in order
1. Nothing on the site counts anything

None of the 17 pages carries Google Analytics, Google Tag Manager, a Google Ads tag or a Meta pixel. Neither of us can tell how many people visit, which pages they read, how many tap to call or how many send the form. That also means ads could not be judged on anything except gut feel.

Fix: Install a tag manager with call-tap, form and page tracking, and a simple call log. A few weeks later there is a real starting number.

2. The quote form is one click away from every page but one

The only enquiry form is on the contact page. The other 16 pages have a search box and “Get a Quote” buttons that send people to /contact/. Someone reading your Castleford page on a phone has to go somewhere else to ask for a price.

Fix: Put the quote form at the top of every page, as in the demo site.

3. The form cannot price a scaffold

It asks name, phone, email, job type and location. It does not ask the height, how long the scaffold is needed, or for a photo.

Fix: Add height, duration and an optional photo upload, so most jobs can be scoped before you ring back.

4. Seven services, one page

Domestic, commercial, industrial, new build, temporary roofs, edge protection and specialist access all sit on one Services page of about 950 words. The “More about this service” links on the town pages all go back to it.

Fix: A page per service, each with the matching case study and photos. The demo has six.

5. Town pages that read the same

Each town page is about 1,660 words, but with the town name hidden the Castleford and Doncaster pages are 94% the same words, and none shows a job done in that town. You are not in the 10 Google Maps results for “scaffolding Wakefield” despite having a Wakefield page.

Fix: Add one or two real local jobs and photos to each town page. Your Horbury case study belongs on the Wakefield page, Otley on Leeds.

6. The commercial side shows the least

“CISRS-carded scaffolders” and “fully insured” are the only credentials on any page. No NASC, CHAS or other SSIP scheme, Constructionline, TG20 or insurance figures, and no compliance sheet for a main contractor to download.

Fix: If you hold them, a Health and Safety page and a one-page compliance pack. Only what you actually hold.

7. Small technical points

Your site tells Google you are open every day, round the clock (a plugin default), and lists no areas served. The reviews widget says 47 while Google shows 50. There is no jobs page, though you were hiring.

Fix: Correct the opening hours, add the eight towns as areas served, and add a short “work with us” page.
