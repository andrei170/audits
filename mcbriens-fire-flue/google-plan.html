Your Google plan, in the order we would do it

⭐ You asked for Google first, and to get your name out there. Google gives a local chimney company five ways in. They work best in this order, because each one makes the next one cheaper.

1. Google Business Profile · the map listing
What it is

The listing with your stars, photos and phone that shows on Google Maps and at the top of a local search. For a chimney company it is often where the call comes from.

What we would do
  • Check you own and control it, under one name: McBrien’s Fire & Flue.
  • Set the right main category (chimney sweep) and add chimney services and masonry, with every service listed.
  • Set your service area by town (Hooksett, Manchester, Bedford and the towns you choose), hours, 24-hour emergency, and the discounts.
  • Add your real job photos every week: liners, rebuilds, crowns, caps, before and after.
  • A short post every week through the season.
  • Link the profile to the right page on your site, not just the home page.
Why first

It costs nothing in ad spend, and it is what both Local Services Ads and search ads lean on.

2. Reviews · closing the gap
Where you stand

Ceaser Chimney has 1,038 Google reviews, Armstrong 1,015, Black Moose 376 and Crown Chimney 182. Your site shows 16 written reviews, and we could not find your Google count.

What we would do
  • A text with your Google review link to every customer the day the job is done, from your name and number.
  • A second, friendly reminder a few days later.
  • Put your live Google rating on your site in place of the template badges.
  • Ask the customers who left reviews on other sites to add one on Google as well.
  • Reply to every review, in your words.
3. Local Services Ads · pay per call
What it is

The ads at the very top of Google, with a green check badge that Google only gives after checking a business’s licence, insurance and background. You pay when a customer calls you, not when they click.

What we would do
  • Apply under the chimney category for your towns, and get the checks done.
  • Choose the job types you want, so you are not paying for calls you would not take.
  • Answer fast: these ads rank partly on how quickly and how often you pick up.
  • Mark each call as booked or not, so the leads you do not want stop coming.
4. Google Search Ads · the bigger jobs
What it is

Text ads for searches like “chimney rebuild Manchester NH” or “chimney liner installation”. You choose the searches, the towns and the budget.

What we would do
  • Aim at the bigger jobs first (rebuilds, liners, masonry, crowns) rather than only sweeps.
  • One campaign per job type, each sent to the page about that job, not the home page.
  • Emergency searches (chimney fire, leak, animal in the chimney) on their own, with your 24-hour line.
  • Only switch on once tracking is in, so every call and form shows which ad it came from.
  • Start small, in the towns you choose, and grow only what brings work.
5. SEO · showing up without paying
What we would do
  • A page for each service: cleaning and inspections, liners, rebuilds, masonry and crowns, caps and dampers, stove and fireplace installs, snow removal.
  • A page for each town you want work in.
  • Titles that name the job and the town; one main heading per page.
  • Local business markup with your address, hours, phone and service area.
  • Hooksett, New Hampshire and your address on every page; one name everywhere; social icons pointing at your real pages.
  • Real job write-ups: where, what was wrong, what you did, with your photos.
The local number

Your main number is toll-free. Many local searchers trust a 603 number more. Something to talk about, not a must.

Underneath all of it: tracking
So you can see what Google is bringing in
  • Google Analytics and the Google Ads tag on every page.
  • Call tracking on the website, the Google profile and every ad, so each call shows where it came from.
  • A form that asks the job type and town, and a photo if they have one.
  • A text back within seconds if you miss a call while you are on a roof.
  • One simple monthly report: calls, forms, jobs booked, and where each came from.
