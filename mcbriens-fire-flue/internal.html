Things to check, do not raise as criticism
Discrepancies
  • Footer says “Since 2014”; a search summary of HomeAdvisor said in business since 2017. Ask how long he has been going.
  • The branded truck photo on his site reads 885 581-9005 on the door, not 888. It may be an AI mock-up (other images are named ChatGPT-Image). Mention gently only if it comes up.
  • Rating badges on his home page (4.9 / 5.0 / A+) carry the template’s Google, Facebook and “Construz” logos: they are template leftovers, not linked to anything.
  • Hours: site says Sunday 9am to 3pm; a search summary of Yelp said Sunday 9am to 12pm. Ask which is right.
  • His Google Business Profile could not be read. A directory listing Google ratings for Hooksett does not include him: he may have a profile under a different name or category, or not be verified. Ask him to pull it up on the call.
Ask Omar (in this order)
1. What does “crush the competition” mean to him: more calls, bigger jobs, or being the name people know?Write his answer down and read it back. It becomes the month-one goal.
2. Which jobs pay best: rebuilds, liners, masonry, or sweeps? Typical value of each?Decides what Google Ads aims at. Sweeps are cheap and seasonal; rebuilds and liners are the money.
3. Where does work come from today, and is he paying HomeAdvisor or Angi?If he pays for shared leads, LSA and his own Google profile are the obvious comparison.
4. Google Business Profile: does he have one, who controls it, how many reviews?Get him to open it on screen.
5. Which towns, and how far for a sweep vs a rebuild?Sets the service area and town pages. Starting point if he is unsure: about 25 to 30 miles around Hooksett for sweeps and inspections (Manchester, Concord, Bedford, Goffstown, Londonderry, Derry, Nashua, Merrimack, Pembroke, Bow), wider for rebuilds and liners, where one job pays for the drive. Ads and LSA can target each zone separately, and Crown, Ceaser and Armstrong all sit inside that circle.
6. How many more jobs a month can the crew take this season? Winter is snow removal too.Do not sell volume he cannot do.
7. Who answers when he is on a roof?Missed-call text back is an easy early win.
8. Certifications (CSIA, NCSG), NH licence, insurance?Needed for LSA and to show on the site. Never put them on the site until he confirms.
9. Why the 888 number? Would he use a local 603 number for Google?
10. What would he need to see in month one to know it is working?
11. Budget: only if he is engaged. “If it brought jobs you can count, what would you put into Google each month?”Ask last. No pressure.
Likely offer angle
Google growth, built on his reviews
  • The pitch in one line: “Your customers already love you, Google just cannot see it. We make Google see it: profile, reviews, a proper site with a page per job and town, then Local Services Ads and search ads for the big jobs, all tracked.”
  • Lead with the gap he can feel: Crown Chimney is on his road with 182 Google reviews and ranks first in Hooksett; Ceaser and Armstrong have 1,000+.
  • Show the demo early. It is built from his own words, photos and reviews, so it lands.
  • Offer the free fixes on the call: social icons, the template badges, Hooksett on the site.
  • He wants volume and name recognition, so LSA plus Google Ads is the likely core, with the profile and reviews as the foundation.
  • Do not mention price until he agrees the problem in his own words.
Objections
“Andrew is already doing my marketing.”Andrew does social and he introduced us. We do Google only, and we would share what works with him.
“I get enough from HomeAdvisor.”Those leads go to several companies at once. Your own Google profile and LSA calls come only to you.
“I already have a website.”Agree, and the reviews and offers on it are good. The gaps are specific: no town, three pages, no tracking.
“Ads are a waste of money.”Often, when nothing is tracked. That is why tracking goes in first and ads start small in his towns.
“Send me something.”Send the hub link (assets.html), but book the next step before hanging up.
