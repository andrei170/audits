What you told us you need, one point at a time

Each point below starts with what you said on Friday, then what we found when we checked again on 6 November, then what fixes it. Your figures are as you told us; we have not checked them.

1 · You said

“At least one a day ... maybe one or two a week.”

What we found: Your Google page was down from about May to the beginning of September. On 6 November Google Maps finds Mech Roofing and Gutter with 4.6 stars from 34 reviews, so it is showing again. But the Review Us button and the map link on your site still point to a second Google listing (a different place ID from the one with your reviews). You told us Mill Spring is your address; your site’s business details say 162 E Main St, Forest City. When Google sees two listings and two addresses for one firm, it has to guess which is real.

What fixes it: One Google listing, owned by you, with a second manager so you are never locked out. Review Us pointed at it. Mill Spring everywhere: site, Google, BBB, Yelp, Facebook. Weekly job photos so the listing stays active, and a check every week that it is live, so if it drops again you know in days, not months.

2 · You said

“Google Ads ... they did not work.”

What we found: We cannot see the old account. What we can see is that there is no Google Ads tag, Google Analytics or Tag Manager on any of your 24 pages (rechecked 6 November on your home, Rutherfordton and contact pages). Without a tag, Google is never told which clicks became calls or roofs, so it cannot learn who to show the ads to, and nobody could show you what worked.

What fixes it: Tracking goes in first, in your name, before any money is spent: Google Analytics, the Google Ads tag and call tracking. Then ads only for replacements and storm work, in the towns you pick, with every lead marked booked or not.

3 · You said

“I haven’t gotten one actual lead.”

What we found: That is Local Services Ads over about a year: about “a hundred something dollars” a call, a cap of $3,500 set by PopLocate, a charge the day before our call for an existing customer, and other companies calling you. We could not open your account. Google’s own help pages (read 6 November) say: spam and duplicate contacts are filtered out and not charged; a repeat contact from the same number within 15 days is not charged again; missed calls under 20 seconds are not charged; charged leads are reassessed for 30 days and credits for invalid, duplicate or poor-quality leads are applied automatically; and leads you report as poor quality “may” occasionally be credited. Google does not list “existing customer” as a reason for a credit. You also get to choose the job types and areas you take leads for.

What fixes it: Before its last shot, open the account together and check four things: which job types are switched on (replacement and storm, not small repairs), which towns, whether every bad lead is being rated, and whether the existing-customer calls are people searching your own business name. If it still brings nothing, switch it off and move the money somewhere you can measure.

4 · You said

“They were just, like, crap. They were garbage.”

What we found: That was Roof Nuts last month, about 8 leads. Roof Nuts’ own home page (read 6 November) says it “finds three roofers that fit, then helps you compare their proposals”. So a Roof Nuts homeowner is, by design, comparing you with two other roofers. You pay $2,500 for 10 booked leads, about every two months.

What fixes it: Leads from your own Google listing, site and ads only went to you. Keep Roof Nuts and the new $99 a lead company while they pay, and judge all of them the same way: cost per signed roof, not cost per lead. That needs every lead in one place, which AccuLynx can do.

5 · Noted on the call

Two new reps are discouraged and working part-time jobs.

What we found: At about 3 roofs a week in the busy season and 1 to 2 in the slow season, three commission reps have about one roof a week each at best. Your goal of 6 a week is 2 each.

What fixes it: Enough booked appointments to keep reps busy, and a form that tells them the job before they call: job type, insurance or cash, zip, how soon, a photo. The 3s and 4s go to your best rep first.

6 · Noted on the call

Your quote form asks name, email, phone and notes.

What we found: Confirmed 6 November: Name, Email, Phone, Notes. Nothing about the job, the town, insurance or how soon, so every enquiry is a call back before anyone knows if it is a roof replacement or a gutter clean.

What fixes it: A form that sorts the job: replacement, repair or gutters; insurance claim or cash; storm damage; roof age; zip; how soon; a photo. Each lead scored 1 to 4, and the signed ones sent back to Google and Meta so the ads learn what a good lead looks like.

7 · You said

“I just need to get to the top of that list.”

What we found: You have 10 town pages, one page each. There is no page for Mill Spring (your own town), Spindale, Lake Lure, Mooresboro, Marion or Black Mountain. Hayes Roofing has 57 town pages across 13 towns, including Rutherfordton, Columbus, Tryon and Landrum, and a job gallery with jobs in Mill Spring and Lake Lure. RuFR USA has 14 pages for Rutherfordton alone. See Competitors Pt.2.

What fixes it: On the site you already have, which you want to keep: pages for roof replacement and storm damage in each town you want, with that town’s job photos and reviews from your RealWork log. Business markup on every service and town page. A home page heading that says roof replacement and your area.

8 · You said

“Like 3 million next year.”

What we found: Here is the sum on your own figures. It is arithmetic, not a forecast.

What fixes it: A plan sized to the gap: roughly 4 more roofs a week than this year, mostly replacements. Monday is about how we would get there and what it costs.

⚠ Questions that change what we would build

  • Roof Nuts and the new $99 company: roughly how many of 10 booked leads sign, busy season and slow?
  • Out of 10 inspections your reps run, how many sign?
  • PopLocate: is it month to month? Who owns the Google listing, the website login and the Facebook ad account?
  • Which towns do you most want replacements in, and how far will the crews go?
  • Cash jobs: what would a cash roof look like that you want more of (metal, shingle, size)?

  • The office: if you take it, which address goes on Google? It needs to be the same everywhere.
