Our first call, 6 November

Austin, thanks for the hour on Friday. Here is everything we covered in one place, so Wednesday can be about the plan rather than going back over it.

⭐ What you told us
  • You have been burned before. From the end of 2024 to about March 2025 you spent “almost 30k on advertising” across two companies, “like 15k a piece”, both suggested by another roofer. “He got screwed, and then we got screwed.” So you are “very weary about doing different stuff.”
  • No contracts. “Do you guys do contracts? Because I’m not necessarily interested in contracts.”
  • Where the work comes from: referrals are the biggest, and your rough guess is about a quarter from your website and Google plus Roof Nuts.
  • Your Google page went down from about May to the beginning of September (back up at the end of July, then down again). Calls went from “at least one a day” to “maybe one or two a week”. It is back now, and you are usually 1st to 3rd in your town (2nd at the moment). “I just need to get to the top of that list.”
  • PopLocate built your site 1.5 to 2 years ago and looks after the website, SEO and Google profile, for about $200 a month plus about $70 a month for Facebook posts. “I just kind of stand still with them”, and “they let a page go down twice.”
  • Google Ads “did not work”. You have had Google’s green-tick badge since the start of 2026.
  • Local Services Ads charge per lead, “a hundred something dollars just for that call”, and PopLocate raised the cap to $3,500 (“not that that’s my actual budget”). The day before our call you were charged for a call from an existing customer, and other companies call you off it. “I haven’t gotten one actual lead” in about a year. You told PopLocate it gets one last shot.
  • Roof Nuts books the appointment for you: $2,500 for 10 booked leads (or $3,500 for the bigger package), over about 20 days to a month, and you buy about every two months. Good in the busy season, but in the slow season it can “get a little shaky”: last month about 8 leads and “they were garbage.”
  • Pay-per-lead: a past $99 a lead company sent lots of leads but would not credit the bad ones. A new $99 a lead company checks each one by phone and is “very reasonable with the credit system”.
  • Your numbers: about 3 jobs a week in the busy season and 1 to 2 in the slow season. An average roof is about $10,000, and you keep about $2,500 after paying everyone. Sales were about $1.5 million in 2025, “maybe 800 to a million” in 2026, and the goal is “like 3 million next year”.
  • What you want more of: roof replacements, not gutters, and 6 jobs a week in the busy season. 70 to 80% of your work is insurance and storm claims, and you would like more cash jobs, because “insurance takes forever”.
  • Your team: sales reps on commission (the two newest are discouraged and working part-time jobs), your father in sales on a salary, Allison running the office and AccuLynx, two build crews and a gutter crew. You want 1 or 2 experienced reps.
  • Your address: Mill Spring is the right one. You did not recognise 162 East Main Street, and you did not know there were two Google pages. You have had no office since Hurricane Helene and are looking at one.
  • Your website: you want to keep it, with edits. “I’m game for that.”
  • What you want from a partner: “you do good by us, we’ll do good by you.” And at the end: “If you think all this stuff will work, then I’m game ... maybe it’s time for a change.”
What we showed you
  • The graded scorecard (open it): your home page heading “Roofs Built to Last” names no job and no town, your service and town pages do not tell Google which jobs and towns they cover, and there is no Google Analytics, Google Ads tag or Tag Manager on any of your 24 pages.
  • Your quote form asks only name, email, phone and notes. We showed a form that asks the job type, insurance claim or cash, zip and how soon, and scores each lead 1 to 4.
  • Two Google pages: the Review Us button and map link on your site go to a different Google listing from the one with your 34 reviews. BBB and Yelp show Mill Spring; your site shows Forest City.
  • Your competitors (open it): Hayes Roofing with 372 reviews, a roofer with about 55 pages and Google Ads tracking (“that tells you that Google Ads work in your market”), RuFR USA with 140 pages, O&T Roofing and Sunrise. You have 24 pages, 10 of them town pages, and no Google tags.
  • The demo site (open it): how it works, your services, reviews, questions and a free quote.
⚠ What you asked us
  • “Do you guys do contracts?” No. We do not tie you in. The short videos we are sending cover how that works.
  • “So you guys just do website, SEO?” Ads as well: Google and Facebook, in your name.
  • What it costs. We are working the numbers around what you spend now, for Wednesday.
  • Lead quality, and what happens with bad leads. In the videos, and on Wednesday.
🔴 Still not fixed (checked 6 November)
  • The Review Us button and the map link on your site still go to the other Google listing.
  • No Google Analytics, Google Ads tag or Tag Manager on your home page, your Rutherfordton page or your contact page.
  • The home page heading is still “Roofs Built to Last”. The contact page has no main heading.
  • Your site’s business details still say 162 E Main St, Forest City, the address you did not recognise.
  • The quote form still asks name, email, phone and notes only.
Before Wednesday
  • You: watch the short videos we send, and call the customer who left the 1-star review on 2 November.
  • Us: a plan and pricing built around what you told us. Audit Pt.2 goes through each point you raised, and Competitors Pt.2 compares you side by side with four local roofers.
