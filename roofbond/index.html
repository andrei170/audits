Why This Is Not A Website Audit
There is nothing to audit, and that is the entire point

Every other business we are speaking to this week got a list of things wrong with their website. You do not have one, so you get something different.

What Tim told our team:

  • One to three large jobs a month, and you can take one to two big ones.
  • Word of mouth, referrals and repeat clients. That is all of it.
  • “No online presence whatsoever” — hence the website being built.
  • No advertising of any kind.

⭐⭐ And here is what makes you unusual rather than behind. We looked you up. Roofbond Roof Restoration has been trading since May 1991 — thirty-five years. You file proper accounts, not the cut-down kind a one-man band files. You are an established Cardiff business with three and a half decades of reputation.

🔴 You have built all of that without the internet. That is the opposite of a problem — it means everything online is still available to you, and none of it has been wasted.

🔴 The One Thing That Decides This Conversation
🔴🔴 A website is already being built, and we do not know by whom or to what end

Tim said the site is being updated or installed. ⭐ So the question is not whether you need a website. You have already decided that. The question is what it is being built to do.

Because there are two completely different things people call a website:

⛔ A website built to exist
  • Five pages. Home, about, services, gallery, contact.
  • Looks tidy. Loads fine. Somebody's nephew or a template.
  • Nothing measuring whether anybody visits.
  • Nothing a search engine can tell apart from four hundred other roofers.
  • One contact form nobody is told to fill in.
  • ⛔ You cannot advertise to it. Spend goes in, nothing comes back out that you can count.
⭐ A website built to sell
  • A page for each thing you actually do, so each search has somewhere to land.
  • Your thirty-five years at the top of it, not buried in an About page.
  • Real jobs written up with what was wrong, what you did and what it cost.
  • Measurement from day one, so you know what works.
  • Built so advertising can point at it.
  • ⭐ Says plainly what you do not do, so the wrong enquiries stop arriving.

⚠️ Both cost real money. Only one of them can ever earn it back. And once the first kind is live it is very hard to tell it is not working, because there is nothing measuring it.

⛔ We are not going to tell you to scrap whatever is in progress before we have seen it. It may be perfectly good. But somebody needs to ask which of those two you are paying for, and that is the first thing we will ask at one o'clock.

⭐ What Thirty-Five Years Is Actually Worth Online
⭐ Said properly, because most firms would kill for this and you may not rate it

A homeowner about to spend serious money on a roof is frightened of one thing: the firm vanishing. Roofing has a reputation problem and they know it. Cowboys, no comeback, a guarantee from a company that dissolves in two years.

⭐⭐ “Trading in Cardiff since 1991” answers that in six words, and almost nobody you are up against can write it. It is the single most valuable sentence you own and it currently appears nowhere.

Add to it:

  • A real yard with a real address. Unit 12, The Timberyard. Not a mobile number and a PO box.
  • Thirty-five years of repeat clients. ⭐ Repeat work in roofing is rare, because most roofs do not need doing twice in a decade. Repeat clients means commercial or estate work, which is the better money.
  • Referral-led growth with no marketing at all. That is evidence the work is good, and it is evidence a stranger cannot currently see.
What We Could Not Verify, And Will Not Guess
Said plainly
  • Which of you is which, and who else is involved. The public record lists several officers over the years including Stephen Richard Thorn and Timothy Stuart Bradshaw. ⚠️ Those listings cover current and former officers together, so we are not going to tell you who runs your own company.
  • Your turnover or size. You file abridged accounts, which tells us you are above the smallest reporting band and nothing more precise than that. We have not looked at a single figure and we are not going to speculate.
  • Who is building the website, when it lands, and what it costs. The most important unknown on this page.
  • Whether the company has traded under another name. The record notes previous names. ⚠️ If so, there may be old listings and reviews attached to a name you no longer use, which is worth finding.
  • What “roof restoration” means in your hands — coatings and treatments, or full strip and re-cover? ⭐ They are sold to completely different buyers.
  • Whether you want more work at all. Nobody has asked you, and it is question C above.
What We Would Ask At One O'Clock
  • 1. Who is building the website, and when is it due? ⭐ Everything else depends on this.
  • 2. What did you ask them for? A few pages that look smart, or something built to bring work in? They are different jobs and usually different prices.
  • 3. Will it have anything measuring it? If the answer is "I assume so", it almost certainly will not.
  • 4. What is a big job worth, and could you take one more a month?
  • 5. Restoration and coating, or full replacement? And which would you rather have more of?
  • 6. Has the company traded under another name? There may be old listings worth claiming.
  • 7. After thirty-five years of word of mouth, why now? ⭐ Something changed. The honest answer to that tells us more than the other six.
Question seven is the one we actually care about. A business does not go looking for a website in its thirty-fifth year for no reason. ⭐ Whatever that reason is, that is the thing to fix.
