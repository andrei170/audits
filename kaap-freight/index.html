The five that matter most
1. Emails from your site go to another address

What we saw: The footer shows sales@kaapfreightservices.com, but the link behind it is mailto: benjamin@digiswift.nl.

Where and when: Footer of all six pages, read 21 October 2026.

Why it costs you: It is almost certainly a leftover from whoever set the site up, not anything deliberate. But anyone who taps your email on a phone writes to that address, not to you, and you would never know the enquiry existed.

⭐ The fix: Point the link at your own inbox (five minutes), and check where the contact form’s submissions are sent too.

2. A phone visitor cannot tell what you do, or call you, from the first screen

What we saw: At phone width the first screen is the port video, the logo and a moving line of text. No headline, no “Get a quote” button, no phone number. No phone number on any page is a tap-to-call link (0 found).

Where and when: Homepage in a 450-pixel-wide browser, 21 October 2026; tap-to-call checked in the code of all six pages.

Why it costs you: Every ad click and most search visitors arrive on a phone. If the first screen does not say “freight forwarder, get a quote”, many leave before scrolling.

⭐ The fix: A headline, a quote button and WhatsApp and call buttons that stay on screen, as in the sample website.

3. The homepage video weighs 14.2 MB on a phone

What we saw: The background video is Video-Ad-1-Mobile.mp4 at 14.2 MB for phones and Video-ad.mp4 at 42.7 MB for desktop.

Where and when: File sizes read from your server, 21 October 2026. ⚠ Google’s speed test (Lighthouse) refused our requests, so we cannot give you a Core Web Vitals score.

Why it costs you: On 4G that is several seconds of waiting before the page settles, and it uses the visitor’s data. Google also scores slow landing pages lower, which makes ads cost more.

⭐ The fix: A still image or a short compressed clip under 1 MB, with the headline and quote button loading first.

4. The form collects a message, not a shipment

What we saw: Fields: name, email, phone, message. It promises a reply within 48 hours on business days. Not on the homepage. After sending, nobody is taken to your /thank-you/ page, which exists but is unused.

Where and when: Contact, Import, Export and Relocation pages, 21 October 2026.

Why it costs you: Every quote starts with questions back, and a shipper asking three forwarders often books whoever answers first. With no thank-you page, no ad can count the enquiry it produced.

⭐ The fix: A three-step form asking lane, cargo, weight and dates, an instant WhatsApp reply, and a thank-you page that ads can count.

5. Nothing a buyer can check you against

What we saw: Five testimonials, each a first name and initial (e.g. “Sarah M., Supply Chain Manager”), with no link to Google or Trustpilot. No Google Business Profile or review listing found. No trade membership or cargo insurance shown.

Where and when: Home, Import, Export and Relocation pages; web searches for “KAAP Freight Services” and Trustpilot, 21 October 2026.

Why it costs you: Businesses choosing a forwarder for regular freight check reviews and memberships. AI assistants told our test buyer to check the BIFA member directory and Trustpilot.

⭐ The fix: A Google Business Profile, a review request after every delivery, and any memberships or insurance shown on every page.
