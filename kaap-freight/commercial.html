The honest answer

A UK importer rarely moves its freight to a new forwarder because of one ad. They usually try a new forwarder on one shipment, compare, and only move more once they trust you. You told us the same thing from your side: existing customers book almost every time you quote, new contacts far less often.

So ads do a specific job. They put KAAP in front of the right people when a shipment is due, and keep you there. The follow-up, and a first shipment done well, is what turns a new contact into a regular shipper.

Who buys freight forwarding

The businesses that import into the UK, and the person who usually chooses the forwarder:

  • Wholesalers and distributors: the operations or purchasing manager
  • Retailers and e-commerce brands: the supply chain, buying or operations manager, or the founder at smaller firms
  • Manufacturers bringing in parts or materials: the procurement or logistics manager
  • Firms with their own import team: the import/export manager

Which of these your best customers are is the first thing to agree, because it decides every ad, page and message.

What they check before trusting a new forwarder
  • That KAAP is real and settled: a registered company (yours is, Companies House no. 15676222), an address, and a website that is up and works on a phone
  • Who they will be dealing with: names and faces, and who to call if a shipment goes wrong
  • That you know their lane: China to the UK, sea and air, customs clearance, written plainly on a page about that lane
  • Cover for their goods: whether cargo insurance can be arranged ⚠ confirm with Adi: only shown if you offer it
  • Trade memberships or accreditations, if you hold any ⚠ only listed if held
  • Proof from other shippers: reviews or references they can check
  • How fast you reply and whether they will be kept updated without chasing

Today your site shows the services and WhatsApp, but not your team, any memberships, or reviews a buyer can check (audit, 21 October 2026). So a careful buyer has to take KAAP on trust, and many will pick a name they already know.

Where ads fit
1. Google Search, for importers with a shipment to move

Importer searches only, with parcel and job searches blocked, landing on a lane page with a quote form that asks the shipment details up front.

2. LinkedIn, aimed at the people who choose the forwarder

Operations, purchasing and supply chain managers at UK importers, by job title and company size. The aim is that when their current forwarder lets them down, KAAP is a name they already recognise.

3. Staying in front of people who already looked

Anyone who visits a lane page and does not ask for a quote keeps seeing KAAP for weeks afterwards, which matches how long it takes to switch forwarder.

4. A lane page that does the convincing

One page per lane with what you move, how, who handles it and how to get a quote. The China to UK page is the first.

The follow-up is where a regular shipper is won
Every new contact moves through the same stages, on one screen
  1. New contact
  2. First conversation
  3. Quote sent, with its value and how long the rate holds
  4. Following up
  5. First shipment booked
  6. Delivered, feedback asked
  7. Second shipment
  8. Regular shipper

So at any moment you can see every open quote, what it is worth, and who needs a nudge today.

What the system does for you
  • Replies within a minute to every enquiry, by email and WhatsApp, saying when the quote will follow
  • Follows up every quote on a schedule you approve: a couple of days after, a week after, then before the rate runs out, until they say yes or no
  • Reminds you who to call today, so the follow-ups you used to keep in your head and two spreadsheets keep happening when you are busy
  • Keeps first-time shippers updated at collection, departure, arrival and delivery, so their first job feels like working with a big firm
  • Asks for feedback and a review after delivery, which is what the next new contact wants to see
  • Checks in before a regular shipper’s usual shipping window, so the next booking comes to you first
  • Wakes up old contacts: people in your spreadsheets who were quoted and went quiet get a polite, approved message
  • Records where every contact came from, so money goes to the channel that brings regular shippers
What stays with you

Your rates, the quote and the relationship. Every message is written with you and approved by you before it goes out, and it works through email, which is how your customers already deal with you.

What we would measure

New contacts, quotes sent to them, first shipments booked, and how many come back for a second shipment within 90 days, by source. No number of customers is forecast here. We would rather show you your own figures once the pipeline is running.
