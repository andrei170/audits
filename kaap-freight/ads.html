The short version
📝 Updated after our call on 22 October

You told us your customers are UK importers and exporters, mostly importing, with China to the UK as a core lane and sea and air roughly even. So Campaign 1 (UK importers, including the China to UK ad group) goes first, LinkedIn moves up to run alongside it, exporters come second, and relocation runs as its own lane. The China to UK clicks land on a new page on the sample website.

⭐⭐ Your buyer is not one person, so your ads should not be one campaign. Your site sells three things: imports into the UK, exports out of it, and relocation for families and offices. Each has a different buyer, searches different words, and needs a different page to land on.

⭐ The value is in repeat shippers. So every campaign is built to attract businesses that ship regularly and to filter out one-off parcel senders hunting for the lowest price per kilo before they cost you a click.

Where you are today
One Google ad, not in your own account
  • Google Ads Transparency Centre (UK), 21 October 2026: one search ad for kaapfreightservices.com, listed under the advertiser “1&1 IONOS SE”. That usually means it was bought through your hosting company’s ad package, so the account and its data sit with them, not you.
  • The ad reads: “KAAP Freight Services Ltd - Global Logistics - Logistic Services. Delivering Excellence in Every Shipment Your Trusted Partner in Global Logistics - wheter importing, exporting, or relocating.” It names no route, no service and no reason to click, has a typo, and lands on the homepage, which has no quote form.
  • Meta Ad Library (UK): no ads found for “kaap freight”. LinkedIn: nothing we could see.
  • No Google Ads conversion tag on your site, so that ad could not learn which clicks became enquiries.

The Transparency Centre does not show dates or spend, so we cannot say when it ran or what it cost.

What your competitors do instead
  • Cargo Force (UK to India): about 200 Google ads, prices per kilo in the headline, links to an air-vs-sea comparison and a price calculator.
  • PSS International Removals: about 300 Google ads, one per destination (Dubai, America), each to a free cost calculator.
  • Speedy Freight: 92 Google ads, separate B2B and local ads, with their star rating shown in the ad.

⭐ The lesson is not “spend more”. It is: one ad per service and route, a landing page that matches the search, and a form that collects the shipment details in the first click.

Tracking, set up before a penny is spent
Every quote request traced back to the ad that produced it
  • Your own ad accounts (Google Ads, Meta Business, LinkedIn Campaign Manager), so the data stays yours.
  • Conversion tracking: form sent (via a thank-you page), call clicks, WhatsApp clicks and calls from ads.
  • Every request tagged with service, route and the campaign that sent it, straight into your CRM.
  • Won quotes fed back to Google and Meta, so they learn which clicks become paying shipments, not just form fills.

⚠ No numbers promised

There is no conversion tracking on your site yet, so there is no baseline to forecast from. Anyone quoting you a cost per lead today would be guessing. We would rather show you real numbers from your own account after the first few weeks.

How we would start
Small, measured, then scale what works
  1. Week 1: fix the email link, add tap-to-call, the quote form and a thank-you page, install tracking, and set up ad accounts you own.
  2. Week 2: launch Campaigns 1 and 2 on Google with their landing pages.
  3. Weeks 3 to 4: add relocation on Google and Meta, retargeting, then the LinkedIn test.
  4. Every week: read the search terms, block parcel and job searches, and move budget to the campaign with the lowest cost per qualified quote.
