The idea

You told us on 22 October that everything runs on email and that new contacts and follow-ups live in two spreadsheets. What we would add is the joining up: every enquiry, from the site, an ad, WhatsApp or a call, landing in one place, followed up automatically, and traced back to where it came from, so the ads plan can be judged on booked shipments, not form fills.

One pipeline for every quote
1. New enquiry2. Qualified3. Quote sent4. Following up5. Booked6. In transit7. Delivered8. Review requested
What happens automatically
  • Instant reply. Every quote request gets a WhatsApp and an email within a minute: “Thanks, we have your shipment details, a quote is coming by [time].” Today the site promises 48 hours.
  • Lead routing. Import, export and relocation enquiries go to the right person or list, tagged with the lane and the ad that sent them.
  • Missed-call text back. If a call is missed, the caller gets a text or WhatsApp straight away so they do not ring the next forwarder.
  • Quote follow-up. If a quote is not accepted, polite reminders on day 1, 3 and 7, then a final check-in. Most quotes are lost to silence, not to price.
  • Shipment updates. Messages at collection, departure, arrival and delivery, so customers stop chasing you.
  • Review requests. After delivery, a one-tap link to leave a Google review. This is what builds the trust the audit says is missing.
  • Repeat business. Regular shippers get a check-in before their usual shipping window.
Reporting that answers one question
Which ads produce shipments you win?

Because every enquiry carries its source, a monthly report shows spend, quote requests, quotes sent, shipments booked and the value booked, by campaign. That is what lets the budget move to what works.

Built around what you already use

We would start by looking at what you have automated, keep what works, and connect it rather than replace it. Question for Tuesday: what is in the two spreadsheets today, and who updates them?
