The short version

⭐⭐ Bring KAAP in front of UK importers who need a forwarder, give them a website they trust at first look, and follow up every quote until it becomes a shipment.

  • Who: UK importers first, aimed at the people who book freight. China is the speciality, with the wider Far East and India to the UK alongside. Exporters second. Relocation as its own lane.
  • Where: Google Search for buyers looking now, LinkedIn for the people who book freight, and your website as the proof.
  • What wins it: the personal touch your customers already get (a named person, a fast WhatsApp reply), plus one pipeline with automatic follow-up, so new contacts get the same attention your existing customers do.

Nothing on this page is priced, and nothing forecasts a result.

The problem, in your words

Existing customers book about 9 times in 10 when you quote. New contacts book about 3 times in 10. And new contacts today depend on you having time to pick up the phone, which you told us you no longer have.

So more ads alone would not fix it. The plan has to bring new importers in, make them trust KAAP quickly, and follow up until they book.

What the plan does
1. Brings new importers to you

Google ads for importers searching right now, and LinkedIn for the people who choose a forwarder. New business stops depending on you finding time to cold call.

2. Makes them trust KAAP before you speak

A website that is always up, shows your team, explains the China to UK service plainly and makes asking for a quote easy on a phone.

3. Follows up every quote, every time

One list instead of two spreadsheets, with polite follow-ups sent for you and approved by you, so quotes to new contacts stop going quiet.

What it looks like for one new customer
An example, to show how the pieces fit. Not a real customer.
  1. An operations manager at a UK homeware brand has stock ready at a supplier in China and searches “sea freight from China to UK”.
  2. She sees a KAAP ad about exactly that, taps it and lands on the China to UK page. She fills in the route, cargo, weight and the date she needs it, in about two minutes.
  3. Within a minute she gets an email confirming her request and saying when the quote will come. Your team quotes within 24 hours, as it does today.
  4. She does not reply. Three days later she gets a short, polite follow-up you approved, then another before the rate runs out. She books a first shipment.
  5. She gets an update at collection, departure, arrival and delivery without having to chase. After delivery she is asked for a review.
  6. Before her next shipping window, she gets a check-in. The pipeline shows she came from the Google ad, so you know which ads bring shippers who come back.

The channels that fit a freight business
1. Google Search, for importers who are looking right now

Someone who types “freight forwarder China to UK” or “sea freight from China” has a shipment to move. Your ad only shows on searches like these (searches for parcels, courier prices and jobs are blocked), and the click lands on a page about exactly that, with a quote form that asks the route, cargo, weight and dates. See the China to UK page.

2. LinkedIn, for the people who book freight

The only place we can pick people by job title and company size, so it reaches operations and purchasing managers before they need you. Two parts: a small, fixed ads test, and posts on your company page written for supply chain and import managers (what affects a Far East shipment this month, what customs needs). Your friend can keep designing them in Canva; we supply what each post says. Your competitors post entertaining content; these are written to bring in business.

3. Your website as proof you are reliable

You told us a customer went quiet when the site went down. For a buyer who has never met you, the website is the first check. It needs to be always up, show your real team and story, open on a phone with a clear headline and a quote button, and send emails to the right inbox.

4. One pipeline and automatic follow-up, working through email

Your two spreadsheets become one list on one screen. Every quote gets a polite follow-up if it goes quiet, every new shipper gets updates without anyone chasing you, and every contact shows where they came from. Built around email, because that is how your customers already work with you.

5. Reminder ads, then Google listings and reviews

Reminder ads (called retargeting) keep KAAP in front of importers who visited your site but did not ask for a quote. From month 2: a page for each service and route, a Google Business Profile and reviews, so enquiries keep coming even when ads are paused.

6. Relocation on Google and Meta, kept separate

Its own campaign and page, so it can be judged on its own numbers and turned up or down without touching business freight.

What we would not start with
  • General social posts aimed at everyone. Your buyers are businesses, so every post is written for importers.
  • Broad Google searches before there are conversions to learn from.
  • Price-per-kg ads or prices on the site. You subcontract, so a published rate gets held against you when costs move, and price shoppers rarely come back. If you ever want ranges, they would carry a clear disclaimer.

Words you will hear
  • Landing page: the page someone arrives on after tapping an ad. Each ad gets one about exactly what was searched.
  • Tracking (conversion tracking): a small piece of code that counts each quote request and records which ad brought it.
  • Reminder ads (retargeting): ads shown only to people who already visited your website.
  • Pipeline (CRM): one screen listing every contact and quote, what stage it is at, and who needs a follow-up today.
  • Qualified quote request: a business shipment with a route and a weight, not a single parcel.
