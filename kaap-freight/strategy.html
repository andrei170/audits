The short version

⭐⭐ Bring KAAP in front of UK importers who need a forwarder, give them a website they trust at first look, and follow up every quote until it becomes a shipment.

  • Who: UK importers first, China to the UK, aimed at the people who book freight. Exporters second. Relocation as its own lane.
  • Where: Google Search for buyers looking now, LinkedIn for the people who book freight, and your website as the proof.
  • What wins it: one pipeline instead of two spreadsheets, with automatic follow-up, so new contacts get the same attention your existing customers do.

Nothing on this page is priced, and nothing forecasts a result.

What you told us on 22 October
  • KAAP is business to business. Your customers are UK importers and exporters with their own warehouses.
  • Mostly imports, with China to the UK as a core lane. Sea and air roughly even, plus rail and road.
  • Existing customers book almost every time you quote; new contacts far less often. You put that down to relationship and how solid KAAP looks from the outside.

  • You have never run ads. Relocation is a newer lane you want to grow, so far by word of mouth.

The channels that fit a freight business
1. Google Search, for importers who are looking right now

People typing “freight forwarder China to UK” or “sea freight from China” have a shipment to move. Phrase and exact searches only, parcel and job searches blocked, every click landing on a page built for that search with a quote form that asks the lane, cargo, weight and dates. See the China to UK page.

2. LinkedIn, for the people who book freight

The only channel that targets by job title and company size, so it reaches operations and purchasing managers before they need you. Two parts: a focused ads test, and posts on your page written for importers (what affects a China shipment this month, what customs needs) rather than general posts.

3. Your website as proof you are reliable

You told us a customer went quiet when the site went down. For a buyer who has never met you, the website is the first check. It needs to be always up, show your real team and story, open on a phone with a clear headline and a quote button, and send emails to the right inbox.

4. One pipeline and automatic follow-up, working through email

Your two spreadsheets become one pipeline. Every quote gets a polite follow-up if it goes quiet, every new shipper gets updates without anyone chasing you, and every contact carries where they came from. Built around email, because that is how your customers already work with you.

5. Retargeting, then search pages and reviews

Retargeting keeps KAAP in front of importers who visited and did not ask for a quote. From month 2: pages per service and lane, a Google Business Profile and reviews, so enquiries do not stop when ad spend does.

6. Relocation on Google and Meta, kept separate

Its own campaign and landing page, so it can be judged on its own numbers and switched up or down without touching business freight.

What we would not start with
  • General social posts aimed at everyone. Your buyers are businesses, so every post is written for importers.
  • Broad Google searches before there are conversions to learn from.
  • Price-per-kg ads. That is a race your competitors are already running, and those buyers rarely come back.

Widen what is working

  • Exporter campaign added, once we agree which lanes to lead with

Judge on shipments, not clicks

  • Service and lane pages for search and AI answers
  • First review of new contacts who booked a second shipment

  • Decide together what to scale, change or stop for the next 90 days
