Today’s call

Thanks for walking me through how freight works and how KAAP runs. This page is a short record of what you told us and how it shapes the plan. Nothing here is priced, and nothing forecasts a result.

What you told us
  • KAAP is business to business. Your customers are UK importers and exporters with their own warehouses, not the suppliers and not the public. You are the middle man between them and the shipping lines and airlines, and the one contact who owns the job if anything goes wrong.
  • Mostly imports, with China to the UK as a core lane, plus exports. Sea and air are roughly even: sea for stock that can wait and costs less, air when it is urgent. You also move by rail and road, from a courier box up to full containers.
  • Everything runs on email. A request comes in, your team gets rates from partners in the origin country, you quote door to door, book it, and see it through to delivery.
  • Your team quotes within 24 hours. A team in India prepares most quotes, with you overseeing everything from London.
  • Existing customers book almost every time you quote. New contacts book far less often, and you put the gap down to relationship and how solid the business looks from the outside.
  • A customer once went quiet when your website went down. She told you it shook her confidence. For buyers who have never met you, the website is the first proof that you are reliable.
  • New work has come from you picking up the phone: cold calls, follow-ups and visits, tracked in two spreadsheets. You no longer have the time for as many calls as you used to make.
  • You have never run ads. Your LinkedIn and Instagram posts so far have been general rather than aimed at your buyers.
  • Relocation is a new lane you want to grow. One builder's move from the UK to Australia led to six more jobs, all by word of mouth.
  • The bottleneck is more enquiries, not capacity. You said the team you have could handle roughly twice the work, with perhaps one more operations person in India.
What that changes in our plan
  • Ads lead with UK importers, China to the UK first, by sea and by air, aimed at the people who book freight: operations, purchasing and supply chain managers. Exporters come second.
  • LinkedIn moves up. Your buyers are businesses, so LinkedIn ads and posts aimed at those job titles become a main channel, with posts written for importers rather than the general public.
  • The website becomes a trust asset, not just a brochure. Fast, always up, your real team and story, and a quote form that captures the lane, cargo and dates, so a new contact sees a firm worth trusting before you speak.
  • Your two spreadsheets become one pipeline. New contacts, follow-ups and open quotes in one place, with reminders, so follow-up keeps happening even when you are busy with operations.
  • Quote follow-up is automatic. Every quote gets a polite chase if it goes quiet, which is where new-contact bookings are usually won or lost.
  • Relocation runs as its own campaign on Google and Meta, with its own landing page, so it grows alongside the business freight rather than distracting from it.
  • Every ad is tracked to the quote and the booking, so we can show which channel brings shippers who come back.
