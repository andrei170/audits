The honest answer first

Your testimonials page quotes letters from Whitbread, Spirit Group and The Chef and Brewer Group, and your site says you work on homes, offices, shops and rental properties. One ad does not win a commercial contract. Ads and a clear website get you on a shortlist; follow-up over weeks or months wins the work. If commercial work is part of your plan, this is how it works.

Who buys this work around Pinner and Harrow
  • Pub and restaurant operators: area or maintenance managers, often working to a refurbishment cycle.
  • Letting agents, landlords and block managers: turning flats round between tenants, communal areas.
  • Shops, offices and clinics: the owner or facilities manager, usually for a fit-out or a refresh.
  • Schools and care homes: business or estates managers, often in the holidays.

Which of these are your best jobs is the first thing to agree, because it decides every page and message.

What they check before they ring
  • Insurance and the level of cover (TrustATrader verified yours on 16 September 2026).
  • Who they contract with: a clear business name and address.
  • Photos of similar jobs, start to finish.
  • References from similar customers (you have three commercial letters already).
  • Risk assessments and method statements, and whether you can work out of hours.

Today the site shows the letters but none of the rest. The sample site has a commercial page with a site-visit request form.

Where ads fit
1. Google search, commercial searches only

Searches like “pub refurbishment”, “commercial refurbishment north west London” or “flat refurbishment for landlords”, with homeowner and DIY searches blocked, sent to a commercial page.

2. LinkedIn, aimed at the people who buy

Facilities, estates and property managers by job title, in your area. The aim is that your name is familiar when their next job comes up.

3. Staying in front of people who already looked

Anyone who visits the commercial page keeps seeing your finished projects for months. That needs a pixel on the site, which it does not have today.

The follow-up is where commercial work is won
Every enquiry moves through the same stages
  1. New enquiry
  2. Site visit booked
  3. Quote sent, with its value
  4. Following up
  5. Waiting on budget or sign-off
  6. Won and scheduled
  7. Repeat work due

So at any moment you can see every open quote, what it is worth, and which ones need a nudge today.

What the system does for you
  • Replies within a minute to every enquiry, from your name.
  • Reminds the customer the day before a site visit.
  • Follows up every quote on a schedule you approve, until it is a yes or a no.
  • Nudges before budgets are set and before a repeat job is due.
  • After each job, sends the photos and asks for a Google review or a reference.
  • Shows which enquiries came from which source.
What stays with you

The site visit, the price and the relationship. Every message is approved by you before it goes out.

What we would measure

Commercial enquiries, site visits, quotes sent and their total value, and quotes won. No number of jobs is forecast here.
