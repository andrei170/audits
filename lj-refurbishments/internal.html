Positioning in one line

“You are known as the best carpenter in Pinner. Let’s make you known as the refurbishment company too.” Lead with the reviews gap (101 vs 7) and the 6 of 6 refurbishment searches he is missing, then the follow-up on big quotes.

1. Open (first 3 minutes)
  1. Rapport (1 min): “Jeff, thanks for the time. On a job today or in the workshop?” Let him talk; he is proud of the carpentry, so let him.
  2. Frame (30 sec): “I have had a proper look at L & J online. I will show you what I found, but first I want to understand where you want the business to go. Then you decide if any of it is worth doing.”
  3. Compliment with a fact: 101 TrustATrader reviews at 4.98, and first on Google and the map for “carpenter Pinner”. Most firms would pay for that.
  4. Name the tension early, gently: the business is called Refurbishments, the website sells carpentry. “Which side do you want more of?” That answer sets the whole call.

2. Biggest job this year: what was it, roughly what size, and how did it find him?
3. Where does work come from today, roughly by share? TrustATrader, repeat, referral, Google, Facebook?
4. Typical refurbishment: duration, which subcontractors (reviews name Marv electrics, Oz plastering, Gabriel fitting, Jimmy carpentry), and how many he can run at once.

7. Sync My Media: what they do monthly and what it costs. Open to us working alongside?

4. Likely objections and answers
“I already have someone who does my website.”Do not knock Sync My Media; the site is maintained (updated 21 Sep 2026, 12 blog posts, town pages that rank for carpentry). The gap is focus, not neglect: the site sells carpentry while the money is in refurbs. Position the demo as a brief they could build from. Our value is the system around it: Google profile, reviews, pixel, ads that run, quote follow-up.
“I get enough work from TrustATrader.”Agree, 101 reviews is excellent. Ask how much of it is big jobs. TrustATrader sends people searching TrustATrader; Google sends people searching Google, where he has 7 reviews and is filed as a Carpenter. Moving reviewers to Google costs nothing.
“Facebook ads didn’t work.”They never had a chance: 18 ads, most live under 3 days, no pixel, mostly furniture, pointing at a site that does not mention refurbishments. That is a setup problem, not a channel verdict.
“I’m busy enough.”Then the goal is better jobs, not more: the form filters, the follow-up wins the big quotes he already sends, and ads run only to his capacity.
“Big jobs come from word of mouth.”Yes, and word of mouth now ends in a Google search of his name. Today that search finds a furniture maker. Make it find the refurbishment company the referrer described.
5. Close and next step
  1. Summarise in his words: the work he wants more of, the gap he agreed with, what it costs him (big jobs going to firms that are easier to find, quotes that go quiet).
  2. Ask directly: “Based on what you have seen, do you want us to do this with you?” Then stop talking.
  3. If yes: price live on the call (nothing in the pack is priced). Agree who briefs Sync My Media, Google Business Profile access, and two or three refurbishment jobs with before and after photos.
  4. If he wants to involve his web provider or anyone else: book the follow-up with a date and time before the call ends, and offer to join a three-way call with Sync My Media.
  5. If no: the fallback offer: Google profile categories, review requests to Google, the Meta pixel and quote follow-up first, then pages and ads.
  6. After the call: note his answers to the demo placeholders (days, reply time, cover level, bathroom scope, extensions and lofts) for the follow-up.
Do not
  • No prices in writing, and no forecast of jobs, enquiries or revenue.
  • Do not mention his date of birth, age or the old registered office.
  • Do not knock Sync My Media or the current site; the gap is focus, not neglect.
  • Do not quote years in business beyond what his own site says; the site says over 40 years of carpentry, Google says 15+.
