The order we would do it in

Small steps, in order, each one proving itself before the next. The demo website shows Stages 2 and 3 working.

1Stage 1: free fixes, this week

Costs nothing but a few hours.

  • Add the four service pages to the sitemap and take the empty blog out, then ask Google to re-read it.
  • Make Google and the site agree: hours (Monday to Friday 9 to 5, or whatever is true), one warranty length, one experience figure that says plainly what it covers.
  • Put MCS, with your certificate number, on the home page.
  • Ask every customer you have installed so far for a Google review, with a direct link. Reviews are the biggest visible gap against local firms.
  • Add extra categories and your service area on your Google listing, and post your Burnage and off-grid installs there.
2Stage 2: capture and measure (small, one-off)

So every enquiry tells you where it is and what it is, and you can see where it came from.

  • A short survey form on every page, service pages included: what they want, postcode, home type, electric car or heat pump, then name and mobile. Your patch gets a yes and a survey time straight away; further out gets "we check first"; outside gets an honest no. The demo shows it working.
  • Count calls, WhatsApp taps and form enquiries in the Google tag you already have, and add the Meta pixel before any social advert goes out.
  • An instant text back to every enquiry and missed call, so someone who asks while you are on a roof hears back in minutes.
3Stage 3: proof that sells a 10k job

Built from what you already have.

  • Write up 3 or 4 installs as case studies (area, house, kit list, what the customer says), starting with Burnage and the off-grid system.
  • Separate area pages for Stockport, South Manchester, Wilmslow and Alderley Edge, Altrincham, Marple and Knutsford, from the words already on your service pages, each with a local job and the form.
  • Show your reviews on every page, not only the home page.
4Stage 4: a small, controlled advert test

Only once Stages 2 and 3 are live, at a budget you choose and can stop.

  • One offer, for example a free solar and battery survey, or a battery added to existing panels, shown only to the postcodes you pick.
  • Each advert lands on one page for that offer, with the postcode form.
  • After a few weeks you will know what an enquiry and a booked survey cost. If the jobs pay for the adverts, spend a little more; if not, change the offer or stop. You never spend ahead of what the jobs are paying back.
5Stage 5: scale toward 3 a week

Only as fast as the numbers allow.

  • Add the next offer or area that the test showed works, Google search adverts if the cost per job makes sense, and more area pages.
  • Weekly look at: enquiries by area and source, surveys booked, jobs won. That is what tells you when to step up.
What we are not saying

We cannot promise a number of enquiries, jobs or a ranking. This is a staged plan, not a shortcut: what it does is make sure every enquiry is captured with its postcode, that you can see what each step brings in, and that spend only goes up when the jobs pay for it. Many of the Stage 1 fixes you or your web designer could do yourselves; the demo is a blueprint, not a demand to switch.
