How to read this

Each section starts with something you told us on 20 November, then what we found when we checked it that day, then what would fix it. Your figures are as you gave them and we have not checked them. Everything else has its source and date.

1. "About 2 jobs a month from the website and Google, and no consistent flow"
What we found
  • Your four service pages still have no enquiry form. Rechecked 20 November: the solar installation page only has "Get a Quote" and "Contact Us" buttons that send people to another page. Your home page does have a form.
  • No page for a single offer, and nothing we could see that would measure a social advert (we read the page text, so a tracking script we cannot see is possible).
  • Your Google rating is 5.0 from 3 reviews, as on 17 November (the review widget on your home page still says 3).
What fixes it
  • A short survey form on every page, asking the postcode and the home first, so the right enquiries come through and far-away ones do not. The demo shows it working.
  • Asking every finished customer for a Google review. Word of mouth is about 70% of your work, so the people who would leave one already exist.
2. "The ads are designed but not launched"
What we found
  • Since about the start of October: around £1,100 on website upgrades and ad content, plus the SEO start and £199 for what you understood to be the pixel set-up. The ads are still being revised and are not live.
  • You sent real photos and drone footage of five installs, and the ads kept using AI images. Changes they say are done (the site title, targeting the North West rather than the whole UK) have not appeared, and you cannot get hold of your account manager.
  • No landing page for the ads to go to. On 20 November we could not see a Meta pixel in your pages’ code (we read the page text, so one we cannot see is possible). Worth asking them to show it to you working in Meta Events Manager, since you paid for it.
What fixes it
  • Before any advert spends a pound: one page for the offer, a form that asks the postcode, and tracking so you can see which advert brought which enquiry.
  • Adverts built from your own install photos and drone footage, never AI images: real roofs in real Stockport streets are the same proof that makes word of mouth convert about 9 in 10.
  • You keep the logins: ad account, pixel, video files and website, in your name, whoever runs them.
3. "Word of mouth dries up in January and February"
What we found
  • Your best channel converts about 9 in 10 because people have seen your work. Nothing replaces it in a quiet month at the moment.
  • Energy prices in January have not been set yet. Ofgem publishes the January 2027 price cap on 25 November 2026. The current cap went up 4% from 1 October 2026. Forecasts from suppliers and analysts (Uswitch, Cornwall Insight) point higher, but they are forecasts, not Ofgem’s figure.
What fixes it
  • Having adverts and a working site ready before January, so a quiet month for referrals is not a quiet month overall.
  • Once Ofgem announces on 25 November, one honest line about the new cap on the site and in the adverts.
4. "The Warm Homes scheme starts in April and broadens the market"
What we found on gov.uk
  • The Warm Homes Loan Scheme is for households across the whole UK, not only lower-income ones: "available to households across the entire UK", with the lender running "normal affordability, credit and eligibility checks".
  • No- and low-interest loans, some interest free: up to £15,000 for solar panels and up to £15,000 for a battery.
  • Installs must be done by MCS-certified installers, and MCS installers must also belong to a consumer code. You are MCS certified since October.
  • Separately, lower-income households can get upgrades free of charge through council-run grants (homes with an EPC below C). That is the part aimed at lower incomes.
  • Start date: we found no April date on gov.uk. The government has announced the scheme but we could not confirm when applications open.

Sources: gov.uk Warm Homes Loan Scheme factsheet (27 October 2026), Warm Homes Plan policy paper, government press release of 20 January 2026. Read 20 November 2026. Not financial advice.

What this could mean for you
  • If the loans are open to most households, it widens the market well beyond lower incomes: people who wanted solar but could not pay £10,000 up front.
  • Only one of the six local installers we checked has a Warm Homes Loan page so far (Renewable Planet). Being early and clear about it is an opening. See Competitors Pt.2.
  • Worth checking which lenders and what paperwork you will need, before homeowners start asking.
5. "Too much time lost to quoting and paperwork"
What you told us
  • One job starting next week has been re-quoted nine times, at about three hours each: roughly a working week on one quote.
  • Enquiries arrive by email and you ring them back as fast as you can, often from a roof. Cold social messages ask for a price with no details, when every system is quoted to the house.
What fixes it
  • A survey form that collects the postcode, roof, home type, usage and battery or EV interest before you quote, so the first quote is closer to the last one.
  • A short "what changes the price" section on the site and an automatic reply to price-only messages, so people arrive expecting a quote built for their house.
  • An instant text back to every enquiry and missed call, so nobody waits while you are on a roof.
6. "More Google reviews, kept genuine"
What we found
  • 5.0 from 3 on Google, against 280, 150 and 37 for the biggest local installers. The count is the visible gap, not the score.
  • You ask every customer already. A thank-you for leaving a review would go against Google’s rules (it counts as an incentivised review and can get reviews removed), so it is worth not doing.
What fixes it
  • A direct review link sent by text the day the system is switched on, and a reminder a week later. Same ask, just at the moment people are happiest.
  • Your architect referral partner and past customers are the quickest first reviews.
7. "About 6 jobs a month in 90 days, while I bring in an extra person"
  • Grid (DNO) applications can take up to two months, so installs lag sales: enquiries won in December may not be on a roof until February. The work has to start coming in now for the spring to be full.
  • Commercial: you are staying predominantly domestic. A commercial lead company quoted about £400 a survey at around 20% conversion (roughly £2,000 a job before materials), and commercial cash flow is harder than domestic, where a 10% deposit and 40% on delivery cover the materials. So everything here is built for domestic first.
Questions that change what we would build
  1. Where are you with your current marketing company, and who owns the ads, video and website logins?
  2. The 60-day ad campaign you have already paid for: when does it start, and can we see the ads before they go live?
  3. Which areas do you want the work from, and how far will you travel?
  4. Are you set up for customers paying with finance or a Warm Homes loan, or is that still to sort?
  5. Which install photos, drone footage and reviews can we use?
  6. When does the extra person start, and how many surveys a week can you take before then?
