HOW TO PLAY HIM (read first)

This is not a "your website is bad" call, and it is not a "we'll get you more customers" call. Both lose him. His site is genuinely good and he has more customers than he can handle. His problem is capacity: he turns work away because he cannot staff it. Our edge is that we read his site and his careers page and found the specific reason his hiring is stuck: the careers page cannot take an application, nothing is measuring it, and nothing is driving candidates to it. Lead with respect for the business, point the marketing machine at hiring, and tie everything back to the jobs he is losing. He is a builder, not a marketer, so keep it concrete and about crew and jobs.

Open (with respect, do not skip)

"Santiago, thanks for making the time. I went through pcsantiago.com before today, and first thing I'll say is it's a genuinely good site. A page for every service, real customer reviews with their towns, a gallery, even a careers page, which almost no contractor your size builds. So this isn't a call about your website being bad. Gabe told me your problem is the opposite of what most people I talk to have: you've got more work than you can staff. Is that fair?"

Tie-down: "Is that fair?"

Frame (the capacity wedge)

"Gabe said you've been turning work away for the best part of a year because you can't find reliable people, or the ones you find don't work out. Every job you turn down is money walking out the door. So the question isn't how to get you more customers. It's how to get you more good people, so you can actually say yes."

Reflect it back before you present anything. Make him feel understood.
