The competitive picture

The two races you are in at once

Start here Audit The two races Demo

Most contractors are only fighting for customers. You told Gabe you have the opposite problem: plenty of work, not enough reliable people to do it. So in Union County you are actually running two races at the same time. One is for the homeowner searching "bathroom remodel near me". The other, the one that is costing you right now, is for the skilled tradesperson deciding where to work. Your website is set up to compete in the first race and barely registers in the second.

The pattern. On the customer side you are already ahead of most of the field: a deep site, a form on every page, named testimonials and a gallery. On the hiring side, the firms that fill their crews treat recruiting the same way they treat sales. They put a real "now hiring" page in front of tradespeople, make applying take two minutes from a phone, and run paid reach so the right people actually see it, often in both English and Spanish to reach the whole local labor pool. Your careers page says the right things but cannot take an application, and nothing is driving candidates to it.

Race one: the homeowner searching for a remodeler

When someone in Westfield, Cranford or Scotch Plains searches "kitchen remodel near me" or "basement finishing Union County", the firms that win lead with a visible rating and reviews, a page for each service, captioned project photos, and both a one-tap call and a short form on every screen.

On this race, you are in good shape. The point of the audit was that it is already working; the gaps here are polish, not a leak.

Race two: the skilled tradesperson deciding where to work

This is the race the call is really about. A good remodeler, carpenter or project lead chooses an employer the same way a homeowner chooses a contractor: they look the firm up. The firms that fill their crews make that easy and make applying effortless.

What a good candidate looks forPC Santiago Construction today (measured)
A dedicated careers pageYes. You already have one, which most contractors do not
A real application form (trade, years, availability)No. The only form on the careers page is the customer quote form
A clear picture of who they would work forAn About page exists; a short owner bio and photo would strengthen it

Reaching the whole local labor pool (English and Spanish)Not addressed. No hiring campaign exists to run in either language

Where you already stand

You start ahead of most of the field on the customer race and on the two hardest parts of the hiring race: you already built a careers page, and you already run a business good enough that your customers refer their friends. The firms filling their crews are not better builders than you. They have just made applying as easy as getting a quote, and they pay to put it in front of the right people. The gap is the funnel and the reach, not the work. The demo shows what a "now hiring" page looks like when it can actually take an application.
