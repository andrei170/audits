What we were told, and why it changes the brief
⚠ This section rests on something reported to us, not measured

Our team was told that one person runs all three locations at the same time, and that this is why Oklahoma is the one that suffers.

⚠ We have not verified that and we are not going to pretend we have. If it is wrong, say so on the call and this page is void. But if it is right, it changes what the problem is, and it changes what would be worth doing about it.

⚠ We were also given the name Carlos as the person running the locations. The booking is in Robert's name. We do not know which of you we are speaking to or how the two roles divide, and that is the first thing we will ask rather than guess at.

What your own website says about it
🔴 Four cities, four phone numbers, and nothing catching what the phones miss

From your live site, on 6 October:

  • (405) 296-4202 — Oklahoma City
  • (469) 927-4717 — Dallas, listed as the Help Desk
  • (512) 866-5801 — Austin
  • (316) 306-5599 — Wichita, Kansas

Those four cities span roughly 550 miles end to end. Oklahoma City to Dallas alone is about three hours.

🔴🔴 And there is not one enquiry form anywhere on the site. No analytics either, and no advertising tag. So every single inbound enquiry depends on a human answering one of four telephones, and nothing records the ones that nobody answered.

⭐⭐ That is the Oklahoma problem, written in your own website. It is not that Oklahoma gets less demand. Oklahoma is in the worst hail belt in the country. It is that Oklahoma gets whatever attention is left over, and there is no system underneath to catch what gets dropped.

Why more leads would make this worse

You told our team you do 15 jobs a month and want 20 to 30 more. The instinct is to go and get them.

🔴 But if the constraint is one person's attention rather than demand, buying more enquiries into it does not produce more jobs. It produces slower callbacks, missed adjuster meetings, and the reviews that follow those.

⭐ And in storm work the cost of being slow is unusually brutal. After a hail event the roof goes to whoever inspects it first, because the homeowner signs with the first person who documents the damage credibly. A contractor three hours away is structurally second, every time, no matter how good the claim work is afterwards.

⚠ We are not telling you not to market Oklahoma. We are saying the order matters, and that we would rather say so now than sell you volume into a branch that cannot absorb it.

What we would want to understand first
  • Who actually answers the Oklahoma number when a storm hits? And where are they standing when they answer it?
  • How fast does an inspection get booked after a hail event? Hours, or days? ⭐ This is the number that decides Oklahoma, and we suspect nobody currently measures it.
  • Does Oklahoma have its own crew, or does it borrow from Texas? If it borrows, demand is not the lever at all.
  • How does a lead reach you now, and what happens if nobody picks up? With no form and no tracking, our honest assumption is that it is simply lost and uncounted.
  • What is Oklahoma doing now against Dallas and Austin? Even roughly. The gap between them tells us whether this is a market problem or a management one.
  • Is there anyone in Oklahoma who could own it? Because the cheapest version of this fix may be a person and a process rather than any marketing at all.

⭐⭐ We may well end up telling you that the first thing to buy is not advertising. A way for enquiries to arrive and be caught without depending on who is free to answer, and a measurement of how fast Oklahoma responds, would come before a single dollar of spend.

⚠ Nothing on this page is priced, and we are not going to put a number on any of it before we have the answers above.
