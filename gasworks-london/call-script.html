Before you start
  • Dave asked what we do so his secretary can join. Say hello to her by name, and ask her role: she may be the one who screens applicants.
  • One line on what we are: "We’re not a recruitment agency. We build your own recruiting, so engineers apply to Gasworks, and you keep the page, the list and the ad account."
The call, in order
  1. Big question: "If two good engineers started in January, what would change for you this year?" Write his words down.
  2. The roles: gas engineer, AC (F-Gas) engineer or another apprentice? Employed or self-employed? What would you pay the right person, with van and hours?
  3. What hasn’t worked: where are the Indeed adverts set (job sites read "Acton" as Acton in Suffolk)? What do the two a day look like? What came of the Instagram "extra help" post?
  4. What has worked: "Five apprentices, and they stay. Where did they come from? Would you take another now?"
  5. Capacity and revenue (see the box below), then the pain: "1 to 10, how much would two good engineers change your year?"
  6. Summary back, wait for a clear yes, then share the screen.
Finding capacity and revenue without sounding salesy
  • Ask it as running the jobs, not as money: "How many engineers are on the tools now?"
  • "On a normal day, how many jobs does one engineer get through? Is that a full day of billable work, or is some of it travel?"
  • "How far ahead are you booked? Roughly how many jobs a week are you turning away or pushing back?"
  • "What’s a typical job for you: a service, a boiler swap, an AC install? Roughly what does each come to?"
  • Then let HIM do the sum: "So one more engineer is roughly how much work a week you could say yes to?" Never say his £125/hour back to him.
  • Our rough guess (internal only): about 9 engineers billing £625 to £875 a day is roughly £1.2m to £1.7m a year. Check it against his answers, never say it.
What to show, in order
  1. Start here (the page you share), then the demo careers site: Apprenticeships first.
  2. Apply as an engineer whose registration has lapsed: instant polite no. Again as a registered engineer: shortlisted. Then "What Dave sees".
  3. The audit and scorecard: D 50 now, A- 87 after. Great for customers, nothing for engineers.
  4. Competitors: no local firm has a careers page; no gas apprenticeship advertised near Acton.
How to pitch it
  • Frame: "You’re not short of work, you’re short of people. We build your own recruiting engine: a careers page, small adverts for engineers and apprentices, and screening questions, so you only speak to people who already pass."
  • Anchor on HIS numbers: the work turned away, and what one more engineer bills a week. Then compare: an agency charges 15 to 20% of a first-year salary per head and you own nothing after.
  • Fit: Always-On (two streams: qualified engineers plus apprentices). If he wants to start small: Keep The Bench Warm, one role. Figures are in the brief below. Say them only if he asks, never in writing.
  • No promise of a hire, applicant numbers or dates.
Close
  • "If we had a careers page live, two small adverts running, and you only spoke to people who’d already passed the questions, is there any reason not to start before the AC season?"
  • "Then I need the roles, the terms and a few photos." If he’s not ready, book the follow-up before you hang up.
