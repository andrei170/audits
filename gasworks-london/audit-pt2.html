How to read this

Each section starts with something you told us on 23 November, then what we found, then what would fix it. Your figures are as you gave them. Nothing here is set up or priced; on Thursday we will walk through how each piece would work.

1. "We need an experienced engineer straight away, air conditioning first"
What we found
  • Nothing on your site tells an engineer you are hiring: no careers page and nowhere to apply.
  • Air conditioning is invisible to an F-Gas engineer: it is not on the archived pages we read and not a category on your Google listing.
What fixes it
  • A careers page with the air conditioning engineer role first, and the two or three days a week option stated up front, so the right person knows it fits around what they already do.
  • Small, targeted adverts aimed at West London, on Facebook and Google, sending people to that page, with tracking so you can see which advert brought which applicant.
  • Air conditioning added as a category on your Google listing, so the trade can see you do it.
2. "Indeed and the groups send mostly poor people"
What fixes it
  • References required to apply: name and contact for at least one previous employer, before the form can be sent. As we said, fewer applications, but more of the right ones.
  • Knock-out questions up front: at least five years’ experience, Gas Safe and F-Gas numbers, a full UK driving licence and the right to work. Anyone who does not meet them gets a polite no straight away.
  • You only spend time on people who have already passed.
3. "You only find out what someone is like after three or four weeks"
What would help
  • Your questionnaire as the second step, before the trial day, so the first day with you is spent on people who have already shown what they know.
  • The trial terms written on the careers page (trial day, then a few weeks where either side can walk away), so good people know exactly what they are signing up to.
  • Simple check-in points at week one, two and four, so problems show up before they become habits.
4. "Apprentices stay"
  • Your apprenticeship route works, so keep it full: an apprenticeship page on the careers site built around your own story, and the colleges you already use linked to it.
  • When we checked the government apprenticeship service, no gas apprenticeship was advertised within 15 miles of Acton. Being the visible option nearby is an opening.
5. The office and AI: "We want to streamline the office and use AI a bit more"
What you told us
  • Every phone call goes to your two office staff.
  • You spend your own time quoting, handling complaints and helping with scheduling, across about 10,000 customers.
  • You asked about AI answering phone calls, and about automating WhatsApp.
What we would look at
  • AI call answering for overflow and out of hours: picks up when the office is busy or closed, takes the customer’s details and the job, and passes it to the office or books it. It supports your office team; it does not replace them.
  • Missed-call text back: anyone who rings and is not answered gets a text within a minute, so they do not ring the next firm.
  • WhatsApp: automatic replies, booking confirmations, "your engineer is on the way", and a job-done message with a review link.
  • Quote follow-ups: a short automatic follow-up after every quote you send, so you are not chasing them yourself.
  • Complaints: one simple place they are logged and answered, so none slip through.
  • Service reminders timed for summer: annual boiler and air conditioning service reminders to past customers in the quieter months, when you have the capacity, rather than in winter when you are turning work away.
Questions that change what we would build
  1. For a two or three day engineer: employed or self-employed, a day rate or a salary, and would they need one of your vans?
  2. What do the office team use to book jobs and send quotes now, and do you use WhatsApp Business?
  3. Roughly how many calls come in a day, and how many get missed at busy times?
  4. Which colleges do your apprentices come from?
