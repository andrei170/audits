The four campaigns in one line
Air conditioning first, a gas engineer second, apprentices always, and a reminder for anyone who looked.

Each campaign is its own advert set with its own form, so you can see which one brings the people you actually want. All four point at one careers page on your own site, and every applicant answers the same knock-out questions before you see them, including the references you asked for.

Campaign by campaign
1The air conditioning engineer, two or three days a week

The goal: one experienced F-Gas engineer who can do installs once or twice a week, starting before the air conditioning season.

  • Who sees it: engineers within about 10 to 15 miles of Acton who follow air conditioning, refrigeration and heating trade pages and brands. Aimed by area and interest, never by age or gender.
  • Where: Facebook and Instagram, in the feed and in Stories.
  • What it shows: your own Mitsubishi install photos and a short clip of the team, with the line nobody else near you is offering: two or three days a week, a trial either side can walk away from.
  • Where it sends them: the air conditioning role on the careers page. Five questions: F-Gas certificate, years on installs, driving licence, area, and a previous employer as a reference.

Why this one: when we searched the whole of London we found two part-time engineer adverts, and neither was air conditioning. Everyone else is advertising full time at forty to fifty thousand. This speaks to the engineer who runs his own small air conditioning business or wants fewer days, and that person is not reading job boards.

2The experienced gas engineer

The goal: a second experienced engineer for plumbing, gas and heating, so the work you are turning down gets a yes.

  • Who sees it: gas and heating engineers within reach of Acton, the same way: by area and by the trade pages they follow.
  • Where: Facebook and Instagram.
  • What it shows: your team on real jobs, your 4.9 on Google and 10/10 on Checkatrade, and how long Gasworks has been going. The point is to look like the firm a good engineer would leave his current job for.
  • Where it sends them: the gas engineer role. Gas Safe number and categories, five years or more, driving licence, right to work, and a reference. Anyone who does not pass gets a polite no straight away.

Why this one: the good ones are busy in winter and not on Indeed. An advert in their feed reaches them while they are not looking, and anyone not ready now goes on the spring list.

3Apprentices and improvers

The goal: keep the route that already works for you full, so you are training your own engineers for next year.

  • Who sees it: people within commuting distance of Acton interested in trades and engineering, plus the colleges you already use, linked to the page.
  • Where: Facebook and Instagram, and a free listing on the government’s Find an Apprenticeship service.
  • What it shows: Mason’s story: apprentice to engineer at Gasworks. A real person beats any slogan.
  • Where it sends them: the apprenticeship page. Short questions: driving licence or working towards one, area, whether they are at college or would like to be.

Why this one: you told us apprentices are the people who stay, and when we checked, no gas apprenticeship was advertised within 15 miles of Acton. You would be the visible one.

4A reminder for anyone who looked

The goal: bring back engineers who opened the careers page but did not apply.

  • Who sees it: only people who have already visited the careers page or watched the team clip.
  • Where: Facebook and Instagram.
  • What it shows: a review from a customer, the trial terms in plain words, and “the role is still open”.
  • Where it sends them: back to the same role and form.

Why this one: a good engineer weighs up a move for a few days before he applies. This is the cheapest part to run because the audience is small and already interested.

What one applicant is likely to cost
A rough guide, not a promise. Your real figure shows within the first few weeks.

There is no published figure for gas and air conditioning engineers in West London, so this is built up from what is published and from what makes your roles harder to fill.

To compare it with
  • A recruitment agency: typically 15 to 20% of a first year’s salary. On the forty to fifty thousand air conditioning roles advertised near you, that is about £6,000 to £10,000 for one engineer, and you own nothing afterwards.
  • Indeed: what you told us: two a day or one in two weeks, mostly poor.
  • One week of work you are turning down: you can put a number on that better than we can.
What we are not saying
No hire, number of applicants or date is promised.

Good engineers are scarce in winter, which is the problem you came to us with. What the campaigns do is put Gasworks in front of engineers who are not looking, turn away the wrong ones before they take your time, and keep a list for the spring. The figures above are published benchmarks and our working from them, not a forecast for your account.
