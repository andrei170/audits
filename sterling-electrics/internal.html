Positioning in one line

“You already have the proof: a perfect Which? score and good service pages. Right now a customer on a phone cannot tap to call you and your site cannot agree where you live. Fix that for free, then send small, tracked local ads to your own site and your own number, sized to the days you actually have free.” He has been burnt by SEO promises and dislikes lead-gen pages: lead with ownership, measurement from day one and no ranking promises.

Ask in this order
1. Let him tell the SEO story fully: which 3 companies, what each promised, how long, what he paid, did he own the site and accounts, what reporting he got. That story is the brief. Do not defend the industry.
2. The lead-gen Zooms: which firms, and what exactly put him off (their own landing pages, shared leads, pay per lead, not owning the customer)? Our answer is the opposite model: his site, his number, his accounts.
3. Which jobs he wants more of at GBP100-800. Repairs, sockets, lighting, showers, EICRs for landlords? Which pay best per hour?
4. Where is home base now: Snodland (ME6, contact page and Which?) or Orpington (town pages, map link)? Which radius?
5. CAPACITY. 10 jobs a week, wants 20, works alone, 6-7 small jobs a day possible. Does he have the days free? Would he take on help? Anything we switch on must match the slots he really has.
6. What happens to calls when he is on a job? (No tap-to-call, no missed-call text.)
7. Google Business Profile: does he have one, who controls it, how many Google reviews? The site map link centres on Orpington.
8. NICEIC / NAPIT / Part P: is he registered? Which? text says he issues NICEIC certificates, the site shows nothing.
9. Who built and looks after the site (WordPress + Beaver Builder; About/Contact edited May 2026)? Was that one of the SEO firms?
10. Reviews: why so few since 2022 (3 since Jan 2025)? Does he ask? Would he send a review text after every job?
Objections
“I’ve tried 3 SEO companies and got nothing.”Agree it happens, ask what each promised. Then: we do not sell rankings. Week 1 is free fixes he can see himself (tap-to-call, Snodland everywhere, sitemap), then every call and form is tracked so he sees the result, not a report about positions. Offer to make the numbers tap-to-call as proof.
“I don’t want leads going through someone else’s page.”Same here. Everything goes to his own site and his own number, in accounts in his name. Nothing is shared with other electricians. Point at the search: the top paid spot for “electrician Snodland” is a quote website, exactly the model he dislikes.
“If I double the jobs, can I even handle it? It’s just me.”Good question, and it shapes the plan. Start with the free fixes, set ads to a small budget only in the towns and job types he picks, and turn it up or down to match free slots. Ask what 20 jobs a week looks like in his diary and whether he would add help.
“I get my work from word of mouth.”Keep it. But the person who hears his name still looks him up, often on a phone, and lands on a site with no tap-to-call and two different home towns. The fixes make word of mouth convert better.
“I’m too busy to deal with this.”That is the point of the text back and the sorting form: fewer wasted call backs. Most of week 1 needs nothing from him.
“Send me something to look at.”Send the hub link (assets.html), but book the next call before hanging up.
